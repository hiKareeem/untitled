# Four Pillars Discovery Questions

## Overview

This document contains the standard question templates for exploring the four pillars of story foundation: CHARACTERS, WORLD, THEMES & STAKES, and FRAMEWORK BEATS.

Use these as adaptive guides — probe deeper where the author engages, stay lighter where they're satisfied.

---

## Pillar 1: Characters

### Protagonist Questions

**Basic Questions:**
1. **Who are they?** (name, age, situation)
2. **What do they want consciously?** (the visible goal)
3. **What do they want unconsciously?** (the deep need)
4. **What is their main flaw?** (what limits them)
5. **What is their 'lie'?** (the false belief they must overcome)
6. **What will be their transformation?** (who will they be at the end?)

**Deeper Probes:**
- **What terrifies the protagonist?** (deep fears)
- **What is their greatest strength?** (and how does it become a weakness?)
- **What is their 'ghost'?** (past wound that haunts them)
- **What do they refuse to see?** (blind spot)
- **When do they feel most alive?** (moments of fulfillment)

### Antagonist/Opposition Questions

**Basic Questions:**
1. **Is there a human antagonist?** If so, what are their motivations?
2. **What other forces oppose the protagonist?** (society, nature, themselves...)
3. **How is the antagonist a mirror of the protagonist?** (thematically)

**Deeper Probes:**
- **Does the antagonist believe they are the hero?** (how do they justify their actions)
- **What do the protagonist and antagonist have in common?**
- **What would be the "just" version of the antagonist's goals?**
- **How does the opposition evolve?** (escalation, transformation)

### Secondary Characters Questions

**Basic Questions:**
1. **Who are the protagonist's allies?**
2. **Is there a mentor?**
3. **Which characters embody the themes?**
4. **Is there a relationship (love, friendship) central to the story?**

**Deeper Probes:**
- **Which character challenges the protagonist?** (challenge their worldview)
- **Which character represents the protagonist's "past"?**
- **Which characters have their own arcs?**
- **How do secondary relationships reflect the themes?**

## Pillar 2: World

### Setting

**Basic Questions:**
1. **Where?** (geographic location, type of environment)
2. **When?** (era, duration of the story)
3. **What is the protagonist's 'ordinary world'?** (their daily life before the story)

**Deeper Probes:**
- **What are the key locations in the story?** (and why these locations?)
- **How does time influence the story?** (era, season, duration)
- **What is the "special world" of the story?** (contrast with the ordinary world)
- **How does the setting reflect the themes?**

### World Rules

**Basic Questions:**
4. **Are there fantasy/SF elements?** If so, what rules?
5. **What are the social/cultural constraints?**
6. **What is 'normal' in this world?**

**Deeper Probes:**
- **What is IMPOSSIBLE in this world?** (and why?)
- **What are the consequences of breaking the rules?**
- **How does the world privilege certain characters?** (power, status)
- **How does the world change during the story?**

### Atmosphere

**Basic Questions:**
7. **What is the dominant tone?** (dark, bright, ironic, melancholic...)
8. **What are the colors/sensations of this world?**

**Deeper Probes:**
- **What is the dominant weather?** (and how does it reflect emotion?)
- **What sounds, smells, textures define this world?**
- **How does the atmosphere change with the phases of the story?**
- **Which objects/visual symbols are recurring?**

## Pillar 3: Themes & Stakes

### Themes

**Basic Questions:**
1. **What is THE central question of your story?** (what the story 'asks')
2. **What other themes do you want to explore?**
3. **Is there a message or reflection you want to convey?**

**Deeper Probes:**
- **What is the "truth" the protagonist must learn?**
- **What are the thematic opposites?** (freedom vs security, love vs duty...)
- **How do the themes manifest visibly in the story?**
- **What position does the story take on these themes?**

### Stakes

**Basic Questions:**
4. **What does the protagonist risk losing?** (personal stakes)
5. **What is at stake for others?** (relational stakes)
6. **Are there broader stakes?** (society, world)
7. **How will the stakes escalate?** (rising stakes)

**Deeper Probes:**
- **What is the WORST thing that could happen?**
- **Why THIS moment?** (why the story is happening now)
- **What happens if the protagonist fails?** (specific consequences)
- **How do personal stakes connect to global stakes?**

### Emotional Journey

**Basic Questions:**
8. **What emotion do you want the reader to feel at the beginning?**
9. **And at the end?**

**Deeper Probes:**
- **Which emotion dominates each phase of the story?**
- **How does the emotion change from one phase to another?**
- **Which scenes are emotionally most intense?**
- **How do you want the reader to feel after finishing?**

## Pillar 4: Framework Beats

### Framework-Specific Questions

**Save the Cat (15 beats):**
- Opening Image: How do we see the protagonist at the beginning?
- Theme Stated: What is the "message" stated early on?
- Setup: What is established?
- Catalyst: What event triggers the story?
- Debate: The protagonist hesitates — why?
- Break into Two: The protagonist commits — how?
- B Story: What is the secondary story (often relational)?
- Fun and Games: The heart of the story — what happens?
- Midpoint: The midpoint — false victory or false failure?
- Bad Guys Close In: Forces oppose — how?
- All Is Lost: The darkest moment — what happens?
- Dark Night of the Soul: The protagonist is at rock bottom — what do they realize?
- Break into Three: The solution is found — what is it?
- Finale: The protagonist faces the problem — how?
- Final Image: How do we see the protagonist at the end?

**Hero's Journey (12 stages):**
- Ordinary World: What does normal life look like?
- Call to Adventure: What is the call to adventure?
- Refusal of the Call: Why does the hero refuse?
- Meeting with Mentor: Who guides the hero?
- Crossing the Threshold: The hero commits — how?
- Tests, Allies, Enemies: What challenges and encounters?
- Approach to Inmost Cave: The hero prepares — how?
- Ordeal: The major ordeal — what happens?
- Reward (Seizing the Sword): What is the reward?
- The Road Back: The hero returns — why?
- Resurrection: The final ordeal — what happens?
- Return with Elixir: What does the hero bring back?

**Marie Vareille (4 essential elements):**
- Inciting Incident: What event launches the story?
- Conflicts: What are the main conflicts?
- Actions: What actions does the protagonist take?
- Climax: What is the culminating point?

**Snowflake Method (10 steps):**
- One-sentence summary: What is the story in one sentence?
- One-paragraph summary: What is the summary in one paragraph?
- Character sheets: Who are the main characters?
- One-page synopsis: What is the story in one page?
- Character descriptions: What are the character details?
- One-page expanded synopsis: How does the story develop?
- Character charts: What are the character arcs?
- Scene list: What are the key scenes?
- Scene planning: How do the scenes link together?
- Narrative: How is the story told?

**Custom Framework:**
- What are the structural elements you've defined?
- How do they flow together?
- What are the key turning points?
- How does the story progress through your framework?

## Adaptive Questioning Guidelines

### When to Probe Deeper

**Probe deeper when:**
- Author gives one-word answers
- Author seems uncertain
- Answer reveals contradiction or gap
- Answer opens interesting thematic territory
- Author shows enthusiasm for a topic

### When to Stay Light

**Stay light when:**
- Author gives detailed, confident answers
- Author seems impatient or bored
- Topic is already well-developed
- Answer feels complete and sufficient

### Integration with Character Dossiers

If character dossiers exist:
- **Review them first** — Know what's already established
- **Acknowledge existing work** — "I see that you have already..."
- **Don't ask what's known** — Skip questions answered in dossiers
- **Build on existing depth** — Probe deeper into established elements
- **Connect to framework** — How does this character fit the framework?
