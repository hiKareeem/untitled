# Tension Mapping Procedures

## Overview

This document defines the procedures for mapping narrative tension in chapters, including evaluation criteria, measurement scales, and visualization methods.

## Tension Evaluation Framework

### Tension Definition
Narrative tension = The level of uncertainty, stakes, and emotional engagement the reader experiences at any point in the narrative.

### Tension Scale (1-10)

**1-2: Minimal Tension**
- Low stakes
- Routine activities
- No active conflict
- Comfort/resolution dominant

**3-4: Low Tension**
- Minor concerns
- Background tension
- Anticipation building
- Subtle unease

**5-6: Moderate Tension**
- Clear stakes established
- Active conflict present
- Questions raised
- Reader engagement solid

**7-8: High Tension**
- Significant stakes
- Multiple conflicts
- Urgency present
- Emotional investment high

**9-10: Extreme Tension**
- Life-or-death stakes
- Maximum conflict
- Critical decisions
- Peak emotional intensity

## Tension Evaluation Points

### Mandatory Evaluation Points
Every chapter must evaluate tension at:

1. **Opening Hook** (0-5%)
   - Does the opening grab attention?
   - Are stakes established?
   - Is there immediate engagement?

2. **Scene Endings** (each scene conclusion)
   - Does tension carry forward?
   - Are questions unanswered?
   - Is there momentum?

3. **Revelation Moments** (plot reveals)
   - Impact of new information
   - Shift in understanding
   - New questions raised

4. **Decision Points** (character choices)
   - Weight of the decision
   - Consequences at stake
   - Uncertainty of outcome

5. **Climax/Peak** (60-80% typically)
   - Maximum tension point
   - All forces converge
   - Critical moment

6. **Resolution** (final 5-10%)
   - Tension release
   - Questions answered
   - New setup (optional)

## Tension Criteria Assessment

### Evaluation Components

#### 1. Stakes Level (0-10 points)
**What does the character have to lose or gain?**
- 10: Life, death, irreversible change
- 7-9: Major consequences, significant change
- 4-6: Moderate consequences, temporary change
- 1-3: Minor consequences, minimal change
- 0: No meaningful stakes

#### 2. Conflict Presence (0-10 points)
**Is there active opposition?**
- 10: Multiple conflicts, internal + external
- 7-9: Strong conflict, clear opposition
- 4-6: Moderate conflict, some opposition
- 1-3: Weak conflict, vague opposition
- 0: No active conflict

#### 3. Uncertainty Factor (0-10 points)
**How uncertain is the outcome?**
- 10: Completely uncertain, multiple possibilities
- 7-9: Highly uncertain, unclear outcome
- 4-6: Moderately uncertain, some doubt
- 1-3: Low uncertainty, likely outcome
- 0: No uncertainty, outcome clear

#### 4. Urgency Level (0-10 points)
**Is there time pressure?**
- 10: Immediate action required, life-or-death
- 7-9: Strong urgency, important timeline
- 4-6: Moderate urgency, some time pressure
- 1-3: Low urgency, flexible timeline
- 0: No urgency, unlimited time

#### 5. Emotional Investment (0-10 points)
**How much does the reader care?**
- 10: Maximum engagement, deeply invested
- 7-9: High engagement, strongly invested
- 4-6: Moderate engagement, somewhat invested
- 1-3: Low engagement, minimally invested
- 0: No engagement, indifferent

**Tension Score = Average of all 5 components**

### Tension Curve Evaluation

#### Healthy Curve Indicators
- Opening tension ≥ 5/10
- Clear rising pattern to climax
- Climax tension ≥ 8/10
- Resolution tension appropriately lower
- No prolonged flat sections (>15% of chapter)
- Logical tension progression

#### Problematic Curve Patterns

**Flat Line**
- Tension stays within 2-point range
- Indicates lack of stakes or progression
- Need to add escalation

**Sawtooth**
- Constant up/down without build
- Lacks sustained tension
- Need to connect tension spikes

** Cliff Drop**
- High tension then sudden drop
- Premature resolution
- Need to maintain tension

**Plateau**
- Extended section at same level
- Stagnation, no progression
- Need escalation or release

## Tension Building Techniques

### To Increase Tension
1. **Raise stakes** - Make consequences more severe
2. **Add time pressure** - Create urgency
3. **Introduce uncertainty** - Make outcome unclear
4. **Complicate conflicts** - Add opposing forces
5. **Withhold information** - Create mystery
6. **Internalize conflict** - Add character struggle
7. **Eliminate options** - Reduce alternatives
8. **Reveal complications** - Show new obstacles

### To Decrease Tension
1. **Resolve conflicts** - Provide answers
2. **Lower stakes** - Show consequences manageable
3. **Add time** - Remove urgency
4. **Provide clarity** - Eliminate uncertainty
5. **Show competence** - Demonstrate capability
6. **Add support** - Introduce allies
7. **Create safety** - Establish secure space
8. **Allow rest** - Permit character recovery

### To Maintain Tension
1. **Subtle escalations** - Incremental increases
2. **Question stacking** - Add new questions before answering old
3. **Complication layering** - Add problems without solving existing
4. **Pacing consistency** - Match sentence length to tension
5. **Sensory immersion** - Keep reader in moment
6. **Character engagement** - Maintain emotional investment

## Tension Mapping Procedure

### Step-by-Step Process

1. **Read chapter for overall impression**
   - Note emotional journey
   - Identify major tension shifts
   - Mark peak moments

2. **Divide into evaluation segments**
   - Scene boundaries
   - Chapter sections (opening, middle, climax, resolution)
   - 10% increments for detailed analysis

3. **Evaluate each evaluation point**
   - Assess all 5 components
   - Calculate tension score
   - Document justification

4. **Map tension curve**
   - Plot scores at evaluation points
   - Connect points with curve
   - Identify pattern type

5. **Analyze curve health**
   - Compare to ideal curves
   - Identify problematic patterns
   - Note flat, spiked, or dropped sections

6. **Document findings**
   - Overall curve assessment
   - Specific issues identified
   - Improvement recommendations

## Tension Issues Reference

### Common Problems

**Insufficient Opening Tension (<5/10)**
- Impact: Weak hook, reader disengagement
- Fix: Add immediate stakes, conflict, or question

**Climax Too Early (<50% through)**
- Impact: Rest of chapter feels flat
- Fix: Save peak tension for proper climax point

**Resolution Too Abrupt**
- Impact: Reader feels cheated
- Fix: Gradual tension release, answer questions

**Flat Middle Section**
- Impact: Reader loses interest
- Fix: Add mini-conflicts, complications, or revelations

**Inconsistent Tension**
- Impact: Reader confusion, disorientation
- Fix: Smooth transitions, justify shifts
