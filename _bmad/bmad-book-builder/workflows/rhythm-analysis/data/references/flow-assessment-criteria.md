# Flow Assessment Criteria

## Overview

This document defines the criteria and procedures for assessing narrative flow - the quality of reading experience that determines how effortlessly a reader moves through the text.

## Flow Definition

**Narrative flow** is the cumulative effect of sentence rhythm, paragraph structure, and textual organization that creates the sensation of effortless (or difficult) movement through prose.

### Flow Components
1. **Sentence rhythm**: Pattern of sentence lengths and structures
2. **Paragraph rhythm**: Pattern of paragraph lengths and organization
3. **Momentum**: Forward drive through the text
4. **Continuity**: Logical and thematic connections
5. **Immersion**: Reader's sense of being in the story

## Sentence Rhythm Analysis

### Sentence Length Classification

**Short Sentences (1-8 words)**
- Impact: Quick, punchy, urgent
- Creates: Tension, emphasis, immediacy
- Use for: Action, emphasis, climax, revelation
- Overuse: Feels choppy, simplistic, juvenile

**Medium Sentences (9-18 words)**
- Impact: Balanced, natural, readable
- Creates: Flow, clarity, comfort
- Use for: Most narrative, dialogue, description
- Ideal: ~60% of sentences should fall here

**Long Sentences (19-30 words)**
- Impact: Complex, contemplative, sophisticated
- Creates: Depth, richness, immersion
- Use for: Reflection, description, complexity
- Overuse: Feels dense, exhausting, pretentious

**Very Long Sentences (31+ words)**
- Impact: Epic, overwhelming, exhaustive
- Creates: Intensity, claustrophobia, abundance
- Use for: Rare specific effect, overwhelming moments
- Overuse: Unreadable, confusing, frustrating

### Sentence Structure Variety

**Simple Sentences**
- Subject + Verb + (Object)
- Example: "She ran to the door."
- Effect: Direct, clear, emphatic
- Target: 30-40% of sentences

**Compound Sentences**
- Two independent clauses joined
- Example: "She ran to the door, but it was locked."
- Effect: Balanced, connected, rhythmic
- Target: 20-30% of sentences

**Complex Sentences**
- Independent + dependent clause(s)
- Example: "When she heard the noise, she ran to the door."
- Effect: Subordinate, explanatory, sophisticated
- Target: 25-35% of sentences

**Compound-Complex Sentences**
- Multiple independent + dependent clauses
- Example: "When she heard the noise, she ran to the door, but it was locked, so she pounded on the wood."
- Effect: Rich, layered, potentially overwhelming
- Target: 5-15% of sentences

### Sentence Rhythm Patterns

**Healthy Rhythm:**
```
Sentence lengths: 12, 8, 15, 6, 20, 9, 14, 7, 18, 10
Pattern: Up-down-up-down with variation
Variety: High (different structures)
Flow: Natural, engaging, propulsive
```

**Monotonous Rhythm:**
```
Sentence lengths: 12, 13, 11, 14, 12, 13, 11, 14, 12, 13
Pattern: Flat, repetitive
Variety: Low (similar structures)
Flow: Boring, tedious, sleep-inducing
```

**Choppy Rhythm:**
```
Sentence lengths: 4, 6, 3, 5, 7, 4, 6, 3, 5, 7
Pattern: All short, staccato
Variety: Low (simple structures)
Flow: Abrupt, jarring, exhausting
```

**Dense Rhythm:**
```
Sentence lengths: 28, 32, 25, 35, 30, 28, 32, 25, 35, 30
Pattern: All long, sustained
Variety: Low (complex structures)
Flow: Heavy, exhausting, confusing
```

### Sentence Rhythm Scoring (0-10)

**Variety Assessment (0-4 points):**
- 4: Excellent variety of lengths and structures
- 3: Good variety with minor repetition
- 2: Moderate variety, some patterns
- 1: Limited variety, noticeable repetition
- 0: No variety, monotonous

**Appropriateness Assessment (0-3 points):**
- 3: Sentence length matches content perfectly
- 2: Generally appropriate with minor mismatches
- 1: Some inappropriate lengths
- 0: Sentence length contradicts content

**Readability Assessment (0-3 points):**
- 3: Effortless reading, no stumbling
- 2: Mostly smooth, occasional re-reads
- 1: Some stumbling, needs work
- 0: Difficult to read, constant re-reading

## Paragraph Rhythm Analysis

### Paragraph Length Classification

**Micro Paragraphs (1-2 sentences)**
- Effect: Emphasis, punch, white space
- Use for: Emphasis, dialogue, scene breaks
- Target: 10-20% of paragraphs

**Short Paragraphs (3-4 sentences)**
- Effect: Quick, readable, light
- Use for: Dialogue, action, transitions
- Target: 25-35% of paragraphs

**Medium Paragraphs (5-7 sentences)**
- Effect: Balanced, substantial, comfortable
- Use for: Description, narration, most content
- Target: 30-40% of paragraphs

**Long Paragraphs (8-12 sentences)**
- Effect: Substantial, immersive, dense
- Use for: Deep description, reflection, complex action
- Target: 5-15% of paragraphs

**Very Long Paragraphs (13+ sentences)**
- Effect: Overwhelming, exhaustive, potentially exhausting
- Use for: Rare specific effect only
- Target: 0-5% of paragraphs

### Paragraph Structure Assessment

**Topic Sentence Presence:**
- Does paragraph have clear focus?
- Is main idea evident?
- Easy to follow?

**Cohesion:**
- Do sentences connect logically?
- Is there flow within paragraph?
- Ideas build on each other?

**Unity:**
- Does paragraph stay on topic?
- No digressions or wandering?
- Single clear purpose?

**Development:**
- Is idea sufficiently developed?
- Not underdeveloped or overdeveloped?
- Right length for content?

### Paragraph Rhythm Scoring (0-10)

**Variety Assessment (0-4 points):**
- 4: Excellent mix of paragraph lengths
- 3: Good variety with minor repetition
- 2: Some variety, noticeable patterns
- 1: Limited variety, repetitive
- 0: No variety, monotonous

**Visual Break Assessment (0-3 points):**
- 3: Good white space, inviting layout
- 2: Adequate breaks, readable
- 1: Dense, intimidating
- 0: Wall of text, exhausting

**Organization Assessment (0-3 points):**
- 3: Clear focus, logical flow
- 2: Generally organized
- 1: Some organizational issues
- 0: Confusing, hard to follow

## Momentum Analysis

### Forward Drive Assessment

**Building Momentum:**
- Reader wants to know what happens next
- Each sentence pulls to the next
- Questions raised, not just answered
- Tension or curiosity maintained

**Maintaining Momentum:**
- Consistent forward pull
- No sections that drag
- Pacing matches narrative needs
- Reader never bored or exhausted

**Losing Momentum:**
- Sections where interest drops
- Reader skims or skips
- Dragging or rushing
- Break in engagement

### Momentum Scoring (0-10)

**Engagement Assessment (0-4 points):**
- 4: Consistently engaging throughout
- 3: Engaging with minor lulls
- 2: Some engagement, some drag
- 1: Occasional engagement
- 0: Never engaging

**Pacing Consistency (0-3 points):**
- 3: Pacing consistently appropriate
- 2: Generally consistent
- 1: Inconsistent pacing
- 0: All over the place

**Forward Pull (0-3 points):**
- 3: Strong pull through entire text
- 2: Good pull with weak spots
- 1: Weak pull overall
- 0: No forward drive

## Continuity Analysis

### Logical Continuity
- Do ideas follow logically?
- Are connections clear?
- Cause and effect clear?
- No gaps in logic?

### Thematic Continuity
- Are themes carried through?
- Recurring elements present?
- Threads connect appropriately?
- Cohesive whole?

### Temporal Continuity
- Time flow clear?
- Chronology makes sense?
- Transitions handle time well?
- No temporal confusion?

### Spatial Continuity
- Location changes clear?
- Movement tracked?
- Setting consistent?
- Reader oriented?

### Continuity Scoring (0-10)

**Logical Flow (0-3 points):**
- 3: Perfectly logical, no gaps
- 2: Generally logical, minor gaps
- 1: Some logical issues
- 0: Confusing, illogical

**Coherence (0-3 points):**
- 3: All elements connect beautifully
- 2: Generally coherent
- 1: Some coherence issues
- 0: Incoherent, disjointed

**Consistency (0-4 points):**
- 4: Perfectly consistent throughout
- 3: Consistent with minor lapses
- 2: Some inconsistencies
- 1: Frequent inconsistencies
- 0: Completely inconsistent

## Immersion Analysis

### Reader Presence
- Does reader feel "in" the story?
- Are senses engaged?
- Emotional connection present?
- Vivid mental images?

### Flow State Indicators
- Lost track of time while reading
- Forget you're reading
- Fully absorbed
- Emotional responses

### Breaking Immersion
- Confusion pulls reader out
- Unnatural language
- Logic gaps
- Awareness of writing craft

### Immersion Scoring (0-10)

**Sensory Engagement (0-3 points):**
- 3: All senses engaged, vivid
- 2: Good sensory detail
- 1: Some sensory presence
- 0: No sensory engagement

**Emotional Connection (0-3 points):**
- 3: Deeply emotionally engaged
- 2: Some emotional connection
- 1: Minimal connection
- 0: No emotional engagement

**Believability (0-4 points):**
- 4: Completely believable, real
- 3: Generally believable
- 2: Some suspension of belief needed
- 1: Strains credibility
- 0: Not believable at all

## Common Flow Issues

### Monotony
**Symptoms:**
- Repetitive sentence lengths
- Same paragraph structures
- Predictable patterns
- Boring rhythm

**Detection:**
- Sentence length variance < 5 words
- Same sentence structure repeatedly
- Paragraph lengths within 2 sentences of each other
- No variety in openings

**Fix:**
- Vary sentence lengths deliberately
- Mix sentence structures
- Combine or break paragraphs
- Rewrite with variety in mind

### Choppiness
**Symptoms:**
- Too many short sentences
- Abrupt transitions
- No flow between ideas
- Staccato rhythm

**Detection:**
- Average sentence length < 8 words
- >50% short sentences
- Frequent sentence fragments
- Little to no connection between sentences

**Fix:**
- Combine short sentences
- Add transition words
- Create compound/complex sentences
- Use longer sentences for flow

### Density
**Symptoms:**
- Too many long sentences
- Wall-of-text paragraphs
- Exhausting to read
- No breathing room

**Detection:**
- Average sentence length > 20 words
- Average paragraph length > 8 sentences
- Long sentences dominate
- Little white space

**Fix:**
- Break long sentences
- Create shorter paragraphs
- Add simple sentences
- Increase white space

### Disconnection
**Symptoms:**
- Ideas don't connect
- Jarring transitions
- No logical flow
- Reader gets lost

**Detection:**
- Sentences don't build on each other
- Paragraph shifts are confusing
- No clear progression
- Reader must re-read to follow

**Fix:**
- Add transitional phrases
- Connect ideas explicitly
- Create logical progression
- Use bridges between sections

## Flow Improvement Techniques

### Sentence Level
1. **Vary lengths deliberately**
   - Mix short, medium, long
   - Create rhythm patterns
   - Match length to content

2. **Vary structures**
   - Mix simple, compound, complex
   - Change sentence openings
   - Rephrase for variety

3. **Read aloud**
   - Hear the rhythm
   - Identify stumbling points
   - Find natural breaks

4. **Combine and break**
   - Combine short, choppy sentences
   - Break long, dense sentences
   - Find optimal length

### Paragraph Level
1. **Vary paragraph lengths**
   - Mix micro, short, medium, long
   - Use white space strategically
   - Match length to content

2. **Check topic focus**
   - One main idea per paragraph
   - Clear topic sentence
   - Stays on topic

3. **Build paragraphs**
   - Start with focus
   - Develop idea
   - Conclude or transition

4. **Use white space**
   - Break intimidating blocks
   - Create visual appeal
   - Guide reader's eye

### Text Level
1. **Create forward pull**
   - Raise questions
   - Build tension
   - Maintain curiosity

2. **Maintain momentum**
   - Watch for dragging sections
   - Keep engagement high
   - Match pacing to content

3. **Ensure continuity**
   - Connect ideas logically
   - Maintain consistency
   - Clear transitions
