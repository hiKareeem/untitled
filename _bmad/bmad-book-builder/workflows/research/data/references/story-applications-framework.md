# Story Applications Framework

## Overview

This framework defines how to connect research findings to specific story elements in the research workflow.

## Purpose

The Story Applications table serves as the critical bridge between research and writing, showing authors exactly how their research applies to specific scenes, characters, and plot points.

## Table Structure

| Story Element | Chapter/Scene | How Research Applies |
|---------------|---------------|----------------------|
| [Element name] | Ch. X, Scene Y | [Application description] |
| [Element name] | Ch. X, Scene Y | [Application description] |

## Column Definitions

### Story Element
What aspect of the story this research supports:
- **Characters** — Character profession, background, skills, knowledge
- **Locations** — Setting details, environment, geography
- **Plot Points** — Events, actions, sequences that rely on research
- **Scenes** — Specific scenes where research is critical
- **Dialogue** — Terminology, slang, period-appropriate language
- **Atmosphere** — Cultural context, social norms, period details

### Chapter/Scene
Specific location in the story where this research applies:
- **Chapter references** — "Ch. 5, 8, 12, 15"
- **Scene references** — "Ch. 5, Scene 2" or "Ch. 12, Scene 3"
- **Multiple locations** — List all relevant chapters/scenes
- **TBD** — If not yet determined, mark as "To Be Determined"

### How Research Applies
Clear explanation of the research application:
- **Accuracy confirmation** — "Emergency physician role accurate for 1970s"
- **Authenticity notes** — "Cardiac monitors, defibrillators verified"
- **Warning/caveat** — "Procedure accurate but character needs medical background"
- **Terminology guidance** — "Use SAMU terminology, not 'paramedics'"
- **Constraint** — "Avoid advanced imaging — CT scanners not widespread"

## Application Types

### 1. Accuracy Confirmation
Research confirms story element is accurate:
```markdown
| Dr. Moreau profession | Ch. 5, 8, 12, 15 | Emergency physician role accurate for 1970s |
```

### 2. Detail Verification
Research verifies specific details:
```markdown
| Emergency room equipment | Ch. 5, Scene 2 | Cardiac monitors and defibrillators verified |
```

### 3. Authenticity Warning
Research reveals potential authenticity issues:
```markdown
| Emergency tracheotomy | Ch. 12, Scene 3 | Procedure accurate but character needs medical background |
```

### 4. Terminology Guidance
Research provides correct terminology:
```markdown
| Emergency response | Ch. 8, Scene 1 | Use SAMU terminology, not "paramedics" |
```

### 5. Constraint Identification
Research identifies what NOT to include:
```markdown
| Medical imaging | Ch. 5, Scene 4 | Avoid CT scanners — not widespread in 1970s |
```

## Best Practices

1. **Be Specific** — Identify exact chapters and scenes when possible
2. **Clear Applications** — Explain HOW research applies, not just that it does
3. **Flag Issues** — Highlight authenticity concerns or constraints
4. **Prioritize** — Most critical applications should appear first
5. **Update Regularly** — Add applications as story evolves

## Critical Applications

Flag applications that are **critical for story accuracy:**
- Applications that impact plot points
- Applications that affect character authenticity
- Applications that influence scene descriptions
- Applications that correct common misconceptions

Mark these clearly in the dossier — they're the most important for the author to reference while writing.
