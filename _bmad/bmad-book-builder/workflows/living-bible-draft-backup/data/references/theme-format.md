# Theme Format Reference

This document provides the standard format for theme entries in the living bible.

## Template Structure

```markdown
### [Theme Name]

**Description:** [Brief description of the theme and its significance in the story]

**Progression:**
| Phase | Chapters | Description |
|-------|-----------|-------------|
| 1/5 | [X]-[Y] | [Phase description] |
| 2/5 | [X]-[Y] | [Phase description] |
| 3/5 | [X]-[Y] | [Phase description] |
| 4/5 | [X]-[Y] | [Phase description] |
| 5/5 | [X]-[Y] | [Phase description] |

**Current phase:** [X/5] ([Phase name])

**Theme carriers:**
| Character | Thematic role | Arc |
|------------|-----------------|-----|
| [Character 1] | [Role] | [Arc description] |
| [Character 2] | [Role] | [Arc description] |

**Key thematic events:**
- Chapter [N]: [Event description] (establishment)
- Chapter [N]: [Event description] (development/crisis)
- Chapter [N]: [Event description] (resolution)

**Associated symbols:**
- [Symbol 1]: [How it represents the theme]
- [Symbol 2]: [How it represents the theme]
```

## Example

```markdown
### Trust vs Mistrust

**Description:** The exploration of trust as the foundation of society and survival.

**Progression:**
| Phase | Chapters | Description |
|-------|-----------|-------------|
| 1/5 | 1-5 | Dominant mistrust — individual survival |
| 2/5 | 6-10 | First openings — need for the group |
| 3/5 | 11-15 | Trust crisis — betrayal or sacrifice |
| 4/5 | 16-20 | Rebuilding — new dynamic |
| 5/5 | 21-25 | Resolution — trust gained or lost |

**Current phase:** 3/5 (Trust crisis)

**Theme carriers:**
| Character | Thematic role | Arc |
|------------|-----------------|-----|
| Marc | Protagonist | Mistrust → trust → ultimate test |
| Julie | Mirror | Naive trust → betrayal → healed mistrust |
| Chen | Pragmatist | Pragmatic mistrust → selective trust |
| Elise | Thematic antagonist | Strategic trust (manipulative) |

**Key thematic events:**
- Chapter 3: Marc refuses Julie's help (mistrust established)
- Chapter 7: First act of trust (sharing food)
- Chapter 12: Perceived betrayal (crisis)

**Associated symbols:**
- The bunker key (trust = access)
- The survival capsules (trust = sharing)
```

## Field Definitions

### Description
- Brief statement of what the theme explores
- Why it matters to the story
- Central question or tension

### Progression Table
- **Phase (X/5)**: Phase number in 5-phase system (see Thematic Phases Reference)
- **Chapters**: Chapter range for this phase
- **Description**: What happens with the theme during this phase

### Current Phase
- Current phase number and name
- Tracks where the theme is in its development

### Theme Carriers
- **Character**: Character who embodies this theme
- **Thematic role**: How they relate to the theme (protagonist, antagonist, mirror, etc.)
- **Arc**: Thematic arc - how their relationship with the theme evolves

### Key Thematic Events
- Key moments where the theme is prominently featured
- Include chapter number and brief description
- Note phase progression in parentheses

### Associated Symbols
- Objects, images, or motifs that represent the theme
- Brief explanation of symbolic meaning

## Thematic Roles

### Thematic Protagonist
- Character who embodies the theme's primary arc
- Usually the one learning/growing through the theme

### Thematic Antagonist
- Character who opposes or challenges the theme
- Creates conflict around thematic values

### Thematic Mirror
- Character who reflects alternative perspective on theme
- Shows different aspect or outcome

### Thematic Pragmatist
- Character who takes practical approach to theme
- Grounds theme in reality

### Thematic Victim
- Character most affected by theme's conflicts
- Often bears consequences of thematic tensions

## Thematic Arc Patterns

### Trust vs Mistrust
- 1/5: Mistrust dominates - survival mode
- 2/5: First openings - necessity of group
- 3/5: Trust crisis - betrayal or sacrifice
- 4/5: Reconstruction - new dynamics
- 5/5: Resolution - trust earned or lost

### Power and Responsibility
- 1/5: Power sought, responsibility ignored
- 2/5: Power gained, consequences emerge
- 3/5: Power crisis - must choose
- 4/5: Responsibility accepted, power transformed
- 5/5: Power and responsibility integrated

### Individual vs Collective
- 1/5: Individual needs dominate
- 2/5: Collective necessity appears
- 3/5: Crisis - self vs group conflict
- 4/5: Balance being negotiated
- 5/5: Integration achieved

### Identity and Adaptation
- 1/5: Fixed identity established
- 2/5: Challenges to identity appear
- 3/5: Identity crisis - who am I?
- 4/5: New identity being forged
- 5/5: Adapted identity embraced
