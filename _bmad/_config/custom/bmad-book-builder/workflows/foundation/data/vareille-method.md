# Marie Vareille Method — Practical Structure

**Origin:** Marie Vareille, author of 11 published novels, guide "How to Write a Novel" (2017)

**Inspired by:** American creative writing techniques + classical dramaturgy (Aristotle)

**Best for:** French-speaking authors, first novels, pragmatic and accessible approach

**Philosophy:** "Writing a novel is like running a marathon — step by step." Discipline, tenacity, and hard work are as important as talent.

---

## The Pillars of the Method

### 1. Preparation — Before Writing

**Character Sheets**
Create a detailed sheet for each main character:

| Element | Description |
|---------|-------------|
| Full Name | |
| Age | |
| Physical Appearance | Precise details |
| Personality Traits | Strengths and weaknesses |
| Backstory | What shaped them |
| Deep Motivation | What they truly want |
| Transformation Arc | How they evolve |
| Voice/Speech Pattern | Language tics, language level |

**Photo Album**
- Collect photos of people who resemble your characters
- Use these visuals to maintain coherence
- Keep them at hand while writing

**The Outline**
- At minimum: know where you're going, where you're taking characters and readers
- An outline prevents discarding chapters or entire manuscripts
- Can be detailed or flexible depending on your style

### 2. Narrative Structure — Essential Elements

Marie Vareille recommends a classic but effective structure:

```
INCITING INCIDENT
      ↓
   CONFLICTS
      ↓
   ACTIONS
      ↓
CHARACTERS WHO EVOLVE
      ↓
  ACTIVE HERO
      ↓
 RISING STAKES
      ↓
    CLIMAX
      ↓
  RESOLUTION
```

**Detail of each element:**

#### Inciting Incident
- The event that launches the story
- Disrupts the initial balance
- Forces the protagonist to react
- *Question:* What tips your hero's life off balance?

#### Conflicts
- Internal (doubts, fears, contradictions)
- External (antagonists, obstacles, society)
- Relational (tensions between characters)
- *Question:* What obstacles prevent your hero from reaching their goal?

#### Actions
- The hero must ACT, not endure
- Each action has consequences
- Choices define the character
- *Question:* How does your hero react to obstacles?

#### Characters Who Evolve
- Visible transformation between beginning and end
- Learning, perspective shift
- The arc must be credible and gradual
- *Question:* How is your hero different at the end?

#### Active Hero
- The protagonist must drive the story
- No passive hero enduring events
- They make decisions, make mistakes, learn
- *Question:* Does your hero advance the plot through their choices?

#### Rising Stakes
- Stakes increase progressively
- What's at risk becomes more and more important
- Growing tension until the climax
- *Question:* What does your hero risk losing?

#### Climax
- Culminating point of the story
- Final confrontation with the main obstacle
- Moment of truth for the protagonist
- *Question:* What is the most intense moment in your story?

#### Resolution
- Consequences of the climax
- New balance (different from the initial one)
- Emotional satisfaction for the reader
- *Question:* How has your hero's world changed?

### 3. Writing Techniques

**Vivid Dialogue**
- Each character has their own voice
- Dialogue advances the plot
- Subtext: what isn't said is as important

**Point of View (POV)**
- Choose and maintain your POV
- First person: intimacy
- Third person: narrative distance
- Multiple POVs: richness but complexity

**Style**
- Find your unique voice
- Reread and refine
- Read a lot to nourish your style

---

### 4. Discipline — Key to Success

**The 4 Essential Qualities (according to Marie Vareille):**
1. **Aptitude for work** — Writing is a trade
2. **Discipline** — Write regularly, even without inspiration
3. **Tenacity** — Go all the way despite doubts
4. **Talent** — Develops with the first three qualities

**Writing Routine**
- Set a regular schedule
- Realistic daily goal (word count or time)
- Protect your writing time
- Accept bad days

## Practical Sheets

### Summary Sheet — Before Starting

- [ ] I created sheets for my main characters
- [ ] I know my inciting incident
- [ ] I know how my story ends
- [ ] I identified the main conflicts
- [ ] My hero is active (not passive)
- [ ] The stakes are clear and important
- [ ] I have an outline (even minimal)

### Summary Sheet — During Writing

- [ ] Does my hero evolve?
- [ ] Do the stakes rise?
- [ ] Does each scene advance the plot?
- [ ] Are my dialogues vivid?
