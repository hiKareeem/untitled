# Save the Cat — 15 Beats

**Origin:** Blake Snyder's "Save the Cat! The Last Book on Screenwriting You'll Ever Need"

**Best for:** Commercial fiction, genre novels, page-turners, stories with strong protagonists

**Philosophy:** Every beat serves a purpose. Structure is invisible to readers when done well.

---

## The 15 Beats

### ACT I — SETUP (0-25%)

**1. Opening Image (1%)**
- First impression of the story world
- Visual "before" snapshot of the protagonist
- Sets tone and mood
- *Question to ask:* What does your protagonist's ordinary world look like at the start?

**2. Theme Stated (5%)**
- Someone states the theme (often to the protagonist)
- Protagonist doesn't understand it yet
- Planted seed that will bloom later
- *Question to ask:* What life lesson will your protagonist learn?

**3. Setup (1-10%)**
- Introduce protagonist and their world
- Show their flaws and needs
- Establish the status quo
- Introduce supporting characters
- *Question to ask:* What's missing in your protagonist's life (even if they don't know it)?

**4. Catalyst (10%)**
- Life-changing event that disrupts everything
- Can be good news, bad news, or both
- No going back to normal after this
- *Question to ask:* What event forces your protagonist out of their comfort zone?

**5. Debate (10-25%)**
- Protagonist hesitates
- Should they take the journey?
- Last chance to cling to old life
- *Question to ask:* Why would your protagonist resist this change?

### ACT II-A — FUN AND GAMES (25-50%)

**6. Break into Two (25%)**
- Protagonist makes a choice
- Enters the "upside-down world"
- Active decision, not passive
- *Question to ask:* What choice does your protagonist make that changes everything?

**7. B Story (22%)**
- Usually the love story or friendship
- Carries the theme
- Provides contrast to A Story
- *Question to ask:* Who will help your protagonist learn the theme?

**8. Fun and Games (25-50%)**
- The "promise of the premise"
- What the trailer would show
- Protagonist in their new world
- *Question to ask:* What are the fun/exciting/scary moments that made you want to write this story?

**9. Midpoint (50%)**
- False victory OR false defeat
- Stakes are raised
- Fun and games are over
- *Question to ask:* Does your protagonist appear to win or lose at the midpoint?

### ACT II-B — BAD GUYS CLOSE IN (50-75%)

**10. Bad Guys Close In (50-75%)**
- Internal and external pressures mount
- Team/allies may start to fall apart
- Enemies regroup
- *Question to ask:* How do things get worse for your protagonist?

**11. All Is Lost (75%)**
- The "whiff of death"
- Lowest point
- Something or someone dies (literally or metaphorically)
- *Question to ask:* What's the absolute worst thing that could happen?

**12. Dark Night of the Soul (75-80%)**
- Protagonist processes the All Is Lost
- Wallowing before action
- Old self must die
- *Question to ask:* How does your protagonist react to hitting rock bottom?

### ACT III — FINALE (75-100%)

**13. Break into Three (80%)**
- Solution discovered
- A and B stories merge
- Protagonist understands the theme
- *Question to ask:* What realization gives your protagonist the key to victory?

**14. Finale (80-99%)**
- Protagonist takes action
- Confronts all enemies
- Applies what they've learned
- New world created
- *Question to ask:* How does your protagonist prove they've changed?

**15. Final Image (99-100%)**
- Mirror of Opening Image
- Shows how things have changed
- Visual proof of transformation
- *Question to ask:* What does your protagonist's world look like now?

## Mapping to Phase Structure

| Beat | Typical Phase Placement |
|------|------------------------|
| Opening Image + Setup | PHASE 1 |
| Catalyst + Debate | PHASE 1-2 transition |
| Break into Two | PHASE 2 start |
| Fun and Games | PHASE 2 |
| Midpoint | PHASE 2-3 transition |
| Bad Guys Close In | PHASE 3 |
| All Is Lost + Dark Night | PHASE 3-4 transition |
| Break into Three + Finale | PHASE 4 |
| Final Image | PHASE 4 end |
