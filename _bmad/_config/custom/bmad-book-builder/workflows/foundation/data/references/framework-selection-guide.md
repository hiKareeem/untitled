# Framework Selection Guide

## Overview

This document provides comprehensive information about narrative frameworks for story foundation. Use this as reference material when guiding authors through framework selection in step 3.

## Philosophy

**A framework is NOT a prison — it's an analytical lens** that helps us see the natural structure of a story. Like an architect choosing a style (gothic, modern, minimalist) to guide their design, we choose a framework that harmonizes with the author's vision.

**Key Principle:** Structure serves creativity, never the reverse.

---

## The Six Frameworks

### 1. Save the Cat (Blake Snyder)

**Best for:**
- Commercial/mainstream fiction
- Stories with strong emotional arcs
- Pacing-driven narratives
- Authors wanting clear structure

**Style:**
- 15 precise beats with recommended timing
- Focus on pacing and emotional turning points

**Strengths:**
- Highly structured and accessible
- Proven by Hollywood success
- Easy to follow without getting lost
- Excellent for pacing

**Considerations:**
- Can feel formulaic if poorly applied
- May not suit literary/experimental fiction

**Ideal for first novel:** YES — Simple, clear, won't overwhelm

---

### 2. Hero's Journey (Joseph Campbell)

**Best for:**
- Adventure stories
- Fantasy and mythic narratives
- Deep transformation tales
- Stories with archetypal resonance

**Style:**
- 12 universal mythological stages
- Focus on psychological and spiritual transformation

**Strengths:**
- Archetypal resonance
- Deep psychological foundation
- Universal appeal
- Timeless structure

**Considerations:**
- Can feel too "masculine"/linear for some stories
- May not suit intimate/domestic narratives
- Requires understanding of mythic patterns

**Ideal for first novel:** MAYBE — More complex but powerful if story fits

---

### 3. Snowflake Method (Randy Ingermanson)

**Best for:**
- Complex plots
- Multiple POVs
- Detailed planners
- Intricate narratives

**Style:**
- Progressive construction (simple → complex)
- Builds from sentence → paragraph → page → synopsis

**Strengths:**
- Organic development
- Controls complexity effectively
- Develops story layer by layer
- Excellent for complex narratives

**Considerations:**
- Longer process
- Less intuitive for some writers
- Requires patience with incremental building

**Ideal for first novel:** NO — Better for experienced authors with complex stories

---

### 4. Méthode Marie Vareille

**Best for:**
- Francophone authors
- First novels
- Pragmatic approach preferred
- Writers who want focus on discipline over theory

**Style:**
- Essential elements (trigger, conflicts, climax, resolution)
- Focus on writing discipline

**Strengths:**
- Highly accessible
- Flexible
- Focus on writing, not theory
- Created by a French author for French authors

**Considerations:**
- Less detailed than other frameworks
- May not provide enough structure for complex plots

**Ideal for first novel:** YES — Created specifically for first-time authors

---

### 5. Custom Framework

**Best for:**
- Experimental fiction
- Non-linear narratives
- Authors with specific structural vision
- Hybrid genre stories

**Style:**
- Author defines their own structural elements
- Complete freedom

**Strengths:**
- Total creative freedom
- Adapted to unique vision
- No constraints

**Considerations:**
- Requires narrative experience
- Higher risk of structural weakness
- May need more revision

**Ideal for first novel:** NO — Requires structural knowledge

---

### 6. Psychological 5-Phase Structure (AgentAdam-Based)

**Best for:**
- Character-driven novels
- Psychological studies
- Deep transformation stories
- Authors with developed characters (5+ contradictions)

**Style:**
- 5 phases based on character psychology (NOT plot)
- Each phase = stage of inner transformation

**Strengths:**
- Exceptional psychological depth
- Coherent character arcs
- Aligned with AgentAdam methodology
- Avoids clichés through psychological authenticity

**Considerations:**
- Requires well-developed characters
- Less focus on external plot
- May not suit action-driven stories

**Ideal for first novel:** MAYBE — If characters are already well-developed

## Educational Content

### Why Use a Framework?

✅ **Provides a clear roadmap** — Know where you're going
✅ **Prevents getting lost** — Stay on track during writing
✅ **Ensures coherence** — Your story has solid structure
✅ **Highlights gaps** — See what's missing before writing

### Can I Change My Mind?

**YES!** The framework is a guide, not a prison. You can and should adapt it to your vision.

### Framework as Architectural Style

Think of frameworks like architectural styles:

- **Save the Cat** = Modern commercial — Clean, functional, proven
- **Hero's Journey** = Gothic cathedral — Mythic, soaring, archetypal
- **Snowflake** = Organic architecture — Builds layer by layer
- **Marie Vareille** = Pragmatic design — Simple, functional, accessible
- **Custom** = Avant-garde — Unique, personal, experimental
- **5-Phase Psychological** = Psychological space — Designed for inner experience

### Framework Selection Process

1. **Assess the story type** — Commercial, literary, experimental, etc.
2. **Consider author experience** — First novel or published author?
3. **Match framework to story** — Which framework serves this story best?
4. **Recommend with reasoning** — Explain WHY this framework fits
5. **Let author choose** — It's their story, their choice
