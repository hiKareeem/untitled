# Thematic Phases Reference

This document defines the 5-phase thematic progression system for tracking theme development in the living bible.

## Phase System Overview

Themes progress through 5 phases during story development. Each phase represents a stage in how the theme is explored, challenged, and resolved throughout the narrative.

## Phase Definitions

| Phase | Nom | Description |
|-------|-----|-------------|
| 1/5 | Introduction | Thème établi, tension initiale |
| 2/5 | Développement | Thème exploré, complications |
| 3/5 | Crise | Thème en conflit maximal |
| 4/5 | Résolution | Mouvement vers conclusion |
| 5/5 | Conclusion | Thème résolu (positivement ou négativement) |

## Phase Details

### Phase 1/5: Introduction (Establishment)

**Characteristics:**
- Theme is introduced to readers
- Initial tension established
- Core question or conflict posed
- Stakes around the theme become clear

**Indicators:**
- Thematic values stated or demonstrated
- Characters take initial positions related to theme
- Inciting incident related to theme
- Reader understands "what's at stake"

**Duration:** Story beginning (typically chapters 1-5)

**Example (Trust vs Mistrust):**
- Marc refuses help, insists on working alone
- Key established: "Trust gets you killed"
- Survival depends on self-reliance
- Theme tension: individual vs group survival

### Phase 2/5: Développement (Development)

**Characteristics:**
- Theme is explored through multiple situations
- Complications challenge initial positions
- Characters test thematic boundaries
- Nuance and complexity introduced

**Indicators:**
- Different characters embody different aspects
- Thematic tensions tested in various scenarios
- Characters begin questioning initial positions
- Exceptions to "rules" appear

**Duration:** Early to middle story (typically chapters 6-10)

**Example (Trust vs Mistrust):**
- Group forms out of necessity
- First tentative acts of trust (sharing resources)
- Some trust rewarded, some punished
- Characters see both sides of theme

### Phase 3/5: Crise (Crisis)

**Characteristics:**
- Theme reaches maximum conflict
- Characters forced to choose sides
- Thematic values tested to breaking point
- No going back to previous understanding

**Indicators:**
- Major plot events centered on theme
- Characters face impossible choices
- Betrayals, sacrifices, or revelations
- Theme cannot be ignored

**Duration:** Middle story (climax of thematic arc, typically chapters 11-15)

**Example (Trust vs Mistrust):**
- Major betrayal or sacrifice occurs
- Character must choose: trust or survive alone
- Previous approach fails completely
- Thematic crisis: "Trust or die" vs "Trust and die"

### Phase 4/5: Résolution (Resolution Movement)

**Characteristics:**
- Movement toward thematic conclusion
- Characters make choices about theme
- New understanding emerges
- Consequences of choices play out

**Indicators:**
- Characters commit to thematic positions
- New patterns established
- Some characters transform, others don't
- Thematic questions find answers (even partial)

**Duration:** Middle to late story (typically chapters 16-20)

**Example (Trust vs Mistrust):**
- Characters choose their path (trust or not)
- New group dynamics solidify
- Consequences of choices visible
- Some relationships rebuilt, others ended

### Phase 5/5: Conclusion (Conclusion)

**Characteristics:**
- Theme resolved (positively or negatively)
- Final statement on thematic question
- Characters live with consequences
- Theme's meaning fully revealed

**Indicators:**
- Thematic tension resolved
- Characters' relationship to theme fixed
- Clear answer to thematic question
- Theme's impact on story clear

**Duration:** Late story to resolution (typically chapters 21-25)

**Example (Trust vs Mistrust):**
- Community either survives through trust or falls through mistrust
- Character who trusted either rewarded or punished
- Theme's "answer" revealed
- Final statement on trust's value

## Usage Guidelines

### Progression Rules

1. **Evidence Required**: Each phase must be supported by story events
2. **Multiple Characters**: Theme may manifest differently through different characters
3. **Parallel Progression**: Multiple themes can progress simultaneously
4. **Thematic Resonance**: Themes should echo and reinforce each other

### Tracking Format

When updating theme progressions:

```markdown
**Évolution thématique : [Theme Name]**

**Phase précédente :** [X/5] - [Phase description]

**Événements thématiques récents :**
- Chapitre [N]: [How theme manifested]
- Impact: [What this means for theme progression]

**Nouvelle phase :** [X/5] - [New phase description]

**Manifestations observées :**
- Dialogue : [Quote or paraphrase illustrating theme]
- Action : [Event that embodies theme]
- Symbole : [Object or image representing theme]

**Porteurs du thème :**
- [Character 1]: [How they carry this theme]
- [Character 2]: [How they carry this theme]
```

### Character-Theme Connections

Track how individual characters relate to themes:

```markdown
### [Character Name]

**Thèmes portés :**
| Thème | Rôle | Évolution |
|-------|------|-----------|
| [Theme 1] | Protagoniste | Arc de [X] → [Y] |
| [Theme 2] | Antagoniste | Résiste au changement |

**Événement thématique récent :**
- Chapitre [N]: [How character embodied/challenged theme]
```

### Thematic Resonance Mapping

Track how themes echo across the story:

```markdown
**Résonances thématiques :**

**[Theme 1] ↔ [Theme 2]:**
- Tension : [How these themes create conflict]
- Résolution potentielle : [How they might resolve]

**Patterns observés :**
- Chapitre [N] fait écho au Chapitre [M] via [theme]
- [Symbol/Object] lie [Character A] et [Character B] thématiquement
```

## Common Thematic Patterns

### Trust vs Mistrust
- **Question**: Can trust survive in hostile conditions?
- **Progression**: Mistrust → Necessity → Crisis → Choice → Consequence
- **Archetypal moment**: Character must trust to survive or mistrust and die

### Power and Responsibility
- **Question**: Does power corrupt or ennoble?
- **Progression**: Power sought → Power gained → Power crisis → Responsibility accepted → Power transformed
- **Archetypal moment**: Leader must sacrifice power for group or keep it and lose group

### Individual vs Collective
- **Question**: Where is the line between self-preservation and group survival?
- **Progression**: Individual needs → Group necessity → Self vs group → Balance or sacrifice → Resolution
- **Archetypal moment**: Character must choose personal desire or group good

### Identity and Adaptation
- **Question**: Who am I in this new world?
- **Progression**: Fixed identity → Identity challenged → Identity crisis → New identity forged → Identity integrated
- **Archetypal moment**: Character must abandon old self to survive or die clinging to it

### Justice and Revenge
- **Question**: Is justice possible in survival situations?
- **Progression**: Justice system → Justice breaks down → Revenge cycle → Justice redefined → Justice restored or abandoned
- **Archetypal moment**: Character chooses revenge or forgiveness

### Hope and Despair
- **Question**: Is hope a strength or weakness?
- **Progression**: Hope present → Hope challenged → Hope lost/fought for → Hope tested → Hope affirmed or denied
- **Archetypal moment**: Character must maintain hope in darkest moment or abandon it
