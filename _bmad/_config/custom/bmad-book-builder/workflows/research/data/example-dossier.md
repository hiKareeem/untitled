## Overview

This dossier covers medical procedures, diagnostic tools, and emergency department practices in 1970s hospitals. Essential for maintaining historical accuracy in medical drama set in this period. Key focus: what was NOT available in 1970s vs. modern medicine.

---

## Key Facts

### Diagnostic Tools (1970s)

- **X-ray technology:**
  - **Source:** "History of Medical Imaging" - Radiology Journal (Academic)
  - **Verification:** ✅ Verified
  - **Priority:** Critical
  - **Details:** X-rays available but required 30+ minute film development. No instant digital results. Films developed in darkroom, brought to radiologist for interpretation.

- **CT Scan availability:**
  - **Source:** "Medical Technology Timeline" - Medical History Museum (Academic)
  - **Verification:** ✅ Verified
  - **Priority:** Critical
  - **Details:** First CT scanner introduced 1972, but extremely rare. Only major research hospitals had one by 1975. Most community hospitals: none until early 1980s.

- **MRI technology:**
  - **Source:** "Imaging Innovation Timeline" - NIH Archives (Academic)
  - **Verification:** ✅ Verified
  - **Priority:** Critical
  - **Details:** NOT INVENTED until 1977. First human MRI scan: 1977. Not available in hospitals until mid-1980s. If your story is pre-1977, MRI does not exist.

- **Ultrasound:**
  - **Source:** "History of Medical Imaging" - Radiology Journal (Academic)
  - **Verification:** ✅ Verified
  - **Priority:** Standard
  - **Details:** Available for obstetrics by early 1970s, but image quality poor. Not yet used for general diagnostic purposes.

### Emergency Procedures (1970s)

- **CPR standardization:**
  - **Source:** "Emergency Medicine Evolution" - American Heart Association Archives (Academic)
  - **Verification:** ✅ Verified
  - **Priority:** Critical
  - **Details:** CPR introduced 1960s, but not universally taught until 1970s. Technique different from modern: focus on chest compressions, but compression rate slower. "ABC" (Airway, Breathing, Circulation) protocol established 1973.

- **Defibrillator technology:**
  - **Source:** "Medical Equipment History" - Journal of Emergency Medicine (Academic)
  - **Verification:** ✅ Verified
  - **Priority:** Critical
  - **Details:** Defibrillators existed but bulky (cart-sized), manual operation only. No AEDs (automated external defibrillators). Required skilled operator. Success rate lower than modern devices.

- **Intubation procedures:**
  - **Source:** "Airway Management History" - Anesthesiology Journal (Academic)
  - **Verification:** ✅ Verified
  - **Priority:** Standard
  - **Details:** Endotracheal intubation practiced, but laryngoscope blades primitive. No video laryngoscopy. Higher failure rate on difficult airways.

### Medications Available (1970s)

- **Pain management:**
  - **Source:** "Pharmaceutical Timeline" - FDA Archives (Academic)
  - **Verification:** ✅ Verified
  - **Priority:** Standard
  - **Details:** Morphine, Demerol available. No modern synthetic opioids. NSAIDs limited to aspirin and early ibuprofen (approved 1974).

- **Antibiotics:**
  - **Source:** "Pharmaceutical Timeline" - FDA Archives (Academic)
  - **Verification:** ✅ Verified
  - **Priority:** Standard
  - **Details:** Penicillin, streptomycin, tetracycline available. No fluoroquinolones (Cipro not until 1987). No cephalosporins widely available until late 1970s.

- **Psychiatric medications:**
  - **Source:** "Psychopharmacology History" - NIH (Academic)
  - **Verification:** ✅ Verified
  - **Priority:** Standard
  - **Details:** Thorazine, lithium available. NO SSRIs (Prozac not until 1987). Limited options for depression, anxiety.

### Hospital Environment (1970s)

- **Record keeping:**
  - **Source:** "Hospital Administration History" - Medical Records Journal (Academic)
  - **Verification:** ✅ Verified
  - **Priority:** Standard
  - **Details:** All paper charts. No computers in patient areas. Charts filed in massive filing rooms. Handwritten notes only.

- **Communication:**
  - **Source:** "Healthcare Communication Evolution" - Hospital Management Review (Media)
  - **Verification:** ✅ Verified
  - **Priority:** Background
  - **Details:** Overhead paging system standard. No cell phones, no pagers widely available until late 1970s. Doctors called via overhead page or phone to nursing station.

## Technical Details

### Emergency Department Layout (1970s)

- Open bay design (not individual rooms)
- Curtains for privacy only
- Central nursing station
- No dedicated trauma bays
- No specialized pediatric areas

### Diagnosis Speed

**1970s reality:**
- Blood work: 2-4 hours for results
- X-ray: 30-60 minutes for film + interpretation
- CT scan (if available): Schedule next day, rare emergency use
- Cultures: 24-48 hours minimum

**Modern comparison:**
- Blood work: 30 minutes
- X-ray: Digital, immediate
- CT scan: 15 minutes, instant results
- Cultures: Still 24-48 hours (some things don't change)

## Common Misconceptions

- **Misconception 1:** Doctors could quickly diagnose conditions with scans
  - **Reality:** Diagnosis in 1970s was slower, relied heavily on physical exam and clinical judgment. Scans rare and slow. Doctors used "wait and observe" more often.
  - **Source:** "Emergency Medicine Evolution" - AHA Archives

- **Misconception 2:** All modern emergency drugs existed
  - **Reality:** Many life-saving drugs (modern clot-busters, advanced cardiac meds) not invented until 1980s-1990s. Treatment options more limited.
  - **Source:** "Pharmaceutical Timeline" - FDA Archives

- **Misconception 3:** Paramedics provided advanced care in ambulances
  - **Reality:** Early 1970s paramedics just beginning. Many ambulances still "scoop and run" with minimal treatment. Advanced prehospital care didn't exist until mid-late 1970s.
  - **Source:** "EMS History" - National EMS Museum (Academic)

- **Misconception 4:** Emergency departments were fast-paced like TV shows
  - **Reality:** Slower pace, less technology, more "watchful waiting." Dramatic saves happened but less frequent due to limited tools.
  - **Source:** Emergency physician interviews - Oral History Project (Media)

## Notes for Author

**Critical reminders for writing 1970s medical scenes:**

- NO instant results. Build in waiting time for tension.
- Doctors rely on physical exam skills more than tests.
- Technology is bulky, slow, requires skilled operators.
- Communication is slower (no cells/pagers early on).
- If you mention a drug, check it existed in 1970s.
- MRI absolutely did not exist before 1977.

**Opportunities for drama:**
- Diagnostic uncertainty (can't just order a scan)
- Equipment failures (manual devices, no backups)
- Time pressure (waiting for X-ray films to develop)
- Communication delays (overhead paging, running to phones)

**Avoid these anachronisms:**
- "Order a CT scan" (unless late 1970s, major hospital, with explanation of rarity)
- "Check the MRI" (absolutely not)
- "Page the doctor" (early 1970s, overhead only)
- Modern drug names (check each one)
- Digital anything (all analog, all paper)
