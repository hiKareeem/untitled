# Beat Mapping System

## Overview

This document defines the narrative beat identification and mapping system used in rhythm analysis. Beats are the smallest units of narrative meaning and serve as the building blocks of pacing and tension.

## Beat Definition

A **narrative beat** is a single, meaningful unit of story action or revelation that accomplishes one clear narrative purpose. It is the smallest atomic unit of plot progression.

### Beat Characteristics
- **Single purpose**: Accomplishes one narrative goal
- **Self-contained**: Has beginning, middle, end
- **Measurable**: Can be identified by its impact
- **Essential**: Removing it would leave a gap
- **Impactful**: Changes story state or reader understanding

## Beat Types

### Reveal Beat
**Definition**: New information revealed to character and/or reader

**Subtypes:**
- **Information reveal**: Facts learned
- **Character reveal**: True nature exposed
- **Motivation reveal**: Why someone acts
- **Secret reveal**: Hidden information surfaced
- **Capability reveal**: Skill/power/resource shown

**Impact**: Increases tension, creates questions, shifts understanding

**Identification markers:**
- "Wait, you're..."
- "I didn't know..."
- "The truth is..."
- "Actually, I..."
- Sudden realization moments

### Reversal Beat
**Definition**: Story direction shifts unexpectedly

**Subtypes:**
- **Fortune reversal**: Good → Bad or Bad → Good
- **Ally reversal**: Friend → Enemy or Enemy → Friend
- **Goal reversal**: Objective changes completely
- **Truth reversal**: What seemed true is false (or vice versa)
- **Power reversal**: Who has control shifts

**Impact**: Major tension spike, recontextualizes previous beats, forces new direction

**Identification markers:**
- "But then..."
- "However..."
- "Until I realized..."
- "Everything changed when..."
- Sudden plot shifts

### Decision Beat
**Definition**: Character makes a choice that matters

**Subtypes:**
- **Moral decision**: Right vs wrong
- **Practical decision**: Between options
- **Sacrificial decision**: Giving something up
- **Commitment decision**: Dedicating to a course
- **Rejection decision**: Refusing a path

**Impact**: Shows character agency, sets future actions, reveals values

**Identification markers:**
- "I choose..."
- "I'll..."
- "I won't..."
- "I have to..."
- Decision statements, commitments

### Action Beat
**Definition**: Significant event occurs that changes story state

**Subtypes:**
- **Conflict action**: Fight, argument, struggle
- **Discovery action**: Finding something/someone
- **Departure action**: Leaving, escaping
- **Arrival action**: Entering, encountering
- **Creation action**: Making, building, destroying

**Impact**: Advances plot, creates consequences, raises stakes

**Identification markers:**
- Physical actions with consequences
- Events that can't be undone
- Moments that change what's possible
- Scene climaxes

### Emotional Beat
**Definition**: Character experiences significant emotional shift

**Subtypes:**
- **Realization emotional**: Understanding feeling
- **Connection emotional**: Bond formed/deepened
- **Loss emotional**: Grief, separation
- **Triumph emotional**: Victory, success
- **Despair emotional**: Defeat, hopelessness

**Impact**: Deepens character, creates reader empathy, resonates emotionally

**Identification markers:**
- Emotional peaks/valleys
- Character processing moments
- Relationship shifts
- Internal change moments

## Beat Identification Process

### Step 1: Segment by Scene
Break chapter into scenes first. Each scene typically contains 2-5 beats.

### Step 2: Identify Change Points
Look for moments where something changes:
- Information state (what characters know)
- Emotional state (how characters feel)
- Physical state (where characters are/what they have)
- Relationship state (how characters relate)
- Goal state (what characters want)

### Step 3: Classify Beat Type
For each change point, determine which beat type best fits.

### Step 4: Verify Significance
Ask: "If this beat were removed, would the story have a gap?"
- If yes: It's a genuine beat
- If no: It's supporting detail, not a beat

### Step 5: Map Position
Record beat location as percentage through chapter:
```
Beat Position = (Beat word count / Chapter word count) × 100
```

## Beat Spacing and Rhythm

### Optimal Beat Frequency

**For Action Chapters:**
- 1 beat every 300-500 words
- 8-12 beats per 3000-word chapter
- Steady, accelerating pace

**For Emotional Chapters:**
- 1 beat every 500-800 words
- 4-7 beats per 3000-word chapter
- More contemplative spacing

**For Transition Chapters:**
- 1 beat every 400-600 words
- 5-8 beats per 3000-word chapter
- Moderate spacing

### Beat Distribution Patterns

**Healthy Distribution:**
```
Opening:   Beats every 400-500 words (establish context)
Rising:    Beats every 300-400 words (accelerate)
Climax:    Beats every 200-300 words (peak intensity)
Resolution: Beats every 500-600 words (decelerate)
```

**Problematic Patterns:**

**Clumped Beats:**
- Multiple beats in short span (<200 words apart)
- Reader overwhelmed
- No time to process
- Fix: Space beats, add reaction beats

**Sparse Beats:**
- Long stretches without beats (>800 words)
- Reader disengaged
- Pacing drags
- Fix: Add reveal or emotional beats

**Front-Loaded Beats:**
- Most beats in opening 30%
- Middle/end feel empty
- Fix: redistribute or add later beats

**Back-Loaded Beats:**
- Opening has few beats
- Slow start, rushed ending
- Fix: add earlier setup beats

## Beat Impact Assessment

### Impact Levels

**Critical Impact (9-10):**
- Changes story direction
- Alters character fundamentally
- Reveals major information
- Creates irreversible consequences
- Must be executed perfectly

**High Impact (7-8):**
- Significantly advances plot
- Deepens character meaningfully
- Reveals important information
- Creates notable consequences
- Important but not essential

**Moderate Impact (5-6):**
- Advances plot moderately
- Adds character depth
- Provides useful information
- Creates minor consequences
- Nice addition, not critical

**Low Impact (3-4):**
- Minor plot advancement
- Small character insight
- Trivial information
- Negligible consequences
- Optional detail

**Minimal Impact (1-2):**
- Tiny plot movement
- Minimal character insight
- Obvious information
- No real consequences
- Could be cut

### Beat Effectiveness Scoring

For each beat, assess:

**Clarity (0-2 points):**
- Is the beat's purpose clear?
- Does the reader understand what happened?

**Impact (0-2 points):**
- Does the beat matter to the story?
- Will reader remember it?

**Execution (0-2 points):**
- Is the beat well-written?
- Does it land effectively?

**Placement (0-2 points):**
- Is this the right place for this beat?
- Does it follow/setup other beats well?

**Necessity (0-2 points):**
- Is this beat essential?
- Would story work without it?

**Beat Effectiveness Score = Sum of criteria (0-10)**

## Beat Analysis by Chapter Section

### Opening Beats (0-15%)
**Purpose:** Hook reader, establish context, launch story

**Required beats:**
- Setup beat (what's normal)
- Inciting beat (what disrupts)
- Character beat (who we care about)

**Ideal:** 2-4 beats, spaced every 400-500 words

### Rising Action Beats (15-60%)
**Purpose:** Build tension, develop complications, deepen engagement

**Required beats:**
- Complication beats (obstacles)
- Character beats (development)
- Information beats (context)
- Mini-reversal beats (shifts)

**Ideal:** 5-8 beats, accelerating spacing (500→300 words)

### Climax Beats (60-85%)
**Purpose:** Maximum tension, peak conflict, critical moments

**Required beats:**
- Major reversal beat (direction shift)
- Critical decision beat (character choice)
- Peak action beat (confrontation)
- Maximum reveal beat (truth exposed)

**Ideal:** 3-5 beats, tight spacing (200-300 words)

### Resolution Beats (85-100%)
**Purpose:** Release tension, answer questions, establish new normal

**Required beats:**
- Resolution beat (conflict ends)
- Emotional beat (processing)
- Setup beat (for future)

**Ideal:** 2-3 beats, relaxed spacing (500-600 words)

## Common Beat Issues

### Missing Beats
**Symptoms:**
- Story feels like it's missing something
- Reader questions unanswered
- Character development gaps
- Plot holes

**Detection:**
- Unexplained events
- Sudden character changes
- Jumped conclusions
- Questions raised but never answered

**Fix:**
- Add reveal beats before gaps
- Insert character motivation beats
- Create decision beats to explain actions

### Weak Beats
**Symptoms:**
- Beat happens but doesn't land
- Reader doesn't care
- No real impact
- Forgettable moments

**Detection:**
- Low effectiveness score (<5/10)
- No consequences follow
- Characters don't react
- Could be removed without effect

**Fix:**
- Raise stakes for beat
- Add character reaction
- Connect to other beats
- Increase consequences

### Misplaced Beats
**Symptoms:**
- Beat feels out of place
- Disrupts flow
- Undermines other beats
- Reader confusion

**Detection:**
- Beats too close together (<200 words)
- Beats interrupt other beats
- Wrong beat type for section

**Fix:**
- Move beat to better position
- Add setup beats before
- Add reaction beats after
- Consider if beat is needed
