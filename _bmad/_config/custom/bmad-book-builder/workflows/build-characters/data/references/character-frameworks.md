# Character Frameworks Reference

## 5-Phase Psychological Development Framework

This framework guides the systematic development of psychologically authentic characters through five phases of exploration.

### Phase 1: Conscious Desires (What They Think They Want)

**Goal:** Establish the character's stated goals and visible motivations.

**Key Questions:**
1. What does the character consciously want most? (The thing they'd tell you if you asked)
2. Why do they want it? What need does this desire satisfy?
3. What are they doing to get it? Actions, plans, strategies

**Purpose:** Creates the character's explicit goal-line — what drives their visible actions and decisions.

---

### Phase 2: Unconscious Desires (What They Actually Need)

**Goal:** Discover the deeper need beneath the conscious desire.

**Key Questions:**
1. What does the character ACTUALLY want? (The desire beneath the desire)
2. How is this different from what they think they want?
3. What happens when these two desires conflict? Which one wins — and at what cost?

**Purpose:** Creates depth and tension. The gap between conscious and unconscious desire is where character complexity lives.

---

### Phase 3: Deepest Fears (What Limits Them)

**Goal:** Identify the core fears that constrain and motivate the character.

**Key Questions:**
1. What terrifies the character most? (The thing they won't even name)
2. What's the origin of this fear? Where did it come from?
3. How does this fear limit them? What won't they do because of it?
4. What would happen if this fear came true? What's the nightmare scenario?

**Purpose:** Fear is the engine of character. What they avoid defines them as much as what they pursue.

---

### Phase 4: Internal Contradictions (What Makes Them Human)

**Goal:** Develop the tensions that make the character authentically human.

**Minimum Requirement:** 5 contradictions per character (based on AgentAdam methodology analysis)

**Types of Contradictions:**

#### Type 1: Values vs Actions
- What does the character believe?
- How does their behavior contradict those beliefs?

#### Type 2: Self-Image vs Reality
- How does the character see themselves?
- How are they actually perceived by others?

#### Type 3: Conscious vs Unconscious
- What do they consciously want?
- What do they unconsciously need?
- How are these in conflict?

#### Type 4: Idealism vs Pragmatism
- What are their ideals?
- What compromises do they make to survive?
- When principles conflict with reality, what do they choose?

#### Type 5: Past vs Present
- Who were they?
- Who are they becoming?
- What old patterns persist despite their efforts to change?

#### Type 6: Additional Identified Tensions
- Any other forces pulling them in opposite directions

**Purpose:** Contradictions create internal conflict, which drives external action. A character without contradictions is a stereotype.

---

### Phase 5: Blind Spots (What They Cannot See)

**Goal:** Identify what the character cannot acknowledge about themselves.

**Key Questions:**
1. What's obvious to everyone else but the character can't see?
2. What do they refuse to admit about themselves? (Even to themselves)
3. How do other people see the character — and how is this different from how they see themselves?

**Purpose:** Blind spots are where growth happens or tragedy strikes. What a character can't see about themselves determines their fate.

## Character Arc Types

### Growth Arc
Character overcomes their flaw and becomes a better version of themselves.
- **Starting Point:** Character has a misapprehension or flaw
- **Catalyst:** Story forces them to confront this flaw
- **Journey:** Struggle, resistance, incremental growth
- **Ending:** Character transcends their limitation

### Fall Arc
Character descends into their flaw and is destroyed by it.
- **Starting Point:** Character has a hidden flaw or weakness
- **Catalyst:** Temptation or pressure reveals this flaw
- **Journey:** Increasing entanglement with their flaw
- **Ending:** Destruction or diminished state

### Flat Arc
Character already has the truth and transforms the world around them.
- **Starting Point:** Character has clarity or wisdom
- **Catalyst:** World challenges their conviction
- **Journey:** Pressure to compromise, resistance
- **Ending:** Character maintains truth, world changes

### Transformation Arc
Character becomes something fundamentally different (neither better nor worse).
- **Starting Point:** Character has a defined identity
- **Catalyst:** Event shatters their understanding of themselves
- **Journey:** Reconstruction of identity
- **Ending:** Character is unrecognizable from starting self

### Complex Arc
Combination or subversion of the above types.

## Quality Standards Reference

### Completeness
All 9 sections populated with meaningful content (not placeholders or "À venir")
- Basic Information
- Apparence physique
- Personnalité
- Désirs et peurs
- Arc de transformation (or Contexte et histoire)
- Compétences et incompétences
- Relations et connections
- Voix et manière d'être
- Thèmes explorés

### Specificity
Content is unique to THIS character, not generic or applicable to anyone.
- Physical appearance: Specific details, not "attractive" or "average"
- Personality: Distinctive traits, not generic archetypes
- Background: Specific experiences, not vague "normal childhood"
- Voice: Distinctive patterns, not could-be-anyone speech

### Contradictions
At least 5 genuine internal tensions (conscious vs unconscious, belief vs reality, etc.)

### Psychology
Desires, fears, and contradictions form a coherent psychological whole.
- Do desires, fears, and contradictions connect logically?
- Does background explain current psychology?
- Do fears make sense given history?
- Is blind spot believable?

### Voice
Distinctive speech patterns, mannerisms, and thought style — recognizable without tags.
- Specific speech patterns identified?
- Mannerisms and habits noted?
- Would this character be recognizable without dialogue tags?
- Does voice match psychology?

### Arc
Clear starting state, catalysts, transformation type, and ending state.
- Starting point established (who they are, their flaw)
- Catalysts of change identified
- Transformation type clear (Growth/Fall/Flat/Complex)
- Ending point established (who they become)

### Consistency
All sections align — voice matches psychology, arc grows from background, etc.
- Voice matches psychology
- Arc grows from background
- Relationships align with psychology
- Appearance reflects or contrasts with self-image (intentionally)

### Story Integration
Character serves clear story role, creates/embodies conflict, arc is essential.
- Role in story is clear
- Creates or embodies conflict/tension
- Arc is essential to story
- Relationships are story-driving

### Emotional Truth
Character feels authentic, not like a construct or stereotype.
- Character feels emotionally authentic
- Reader would care about them
- They could surprise you (not predictable)
