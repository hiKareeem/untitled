# Author's Note

I did not write this book. I conducted it.

The prose in this trilogy was generated by Claude Opus 4.6, a large language model made by Anthropic. Every word of narrative text — dialogue, description, narration, epigraphs, in-world documents — was produced by the model. No exceptions.

What I did: conceived the story, designed the characters, built the world, architected the structure across three books, planned each chapter, directed the generation, reviewed every line of output, revised what didn't work, and approved what did. The ideas are mine. The characters are mine. The thematic argument is mine. The voice is calibrated to match mine. But the language is the system's output — directed, constrained, reviewed, and selected by me.

The distinction matters because I think it should be disclosed, not because I think it diminishes the work. A conductor does not play every instrument. The orchestra still performs the conductor's interpretation of the score. Whether that analogy holds for AI-assisted prose is a question I don't claim to have fully answered. I have a body of work and a willingness to discuss the rest.

The complete creation history — every commit, every draft, every revision, every planning document — is publicly available at:

**https://github.com/hiKareeem/untitled**

The repository is not a summary of the process. It is the process. Any passage in this book can be traced back through its development. The audit trail exists because I believe transparency is the minimum standard for this kind of work.

The planning architecture was built with Claude Sonnet 4.5 and 4.6, using the BMAD Method, an open-source framework for AI-assisted development. The prose model — Opus — is not interchangeable with the planning models. Opus generates the voice. The others scaffold the structure the voice moves through.

I am a physician by training. I am not a writer by trade. I built a system that could produce the book I wanted to read, and then I spent months making sure it did. Whether that makes me an author in the traditional sense is a question for critics, copyright law, and the reader holding this book. I've made my argument. The repository is the evidence. You can decide.
