The question had outlived the person who asked it.

Six months. The Cathedral's geological formation conducted the post-Broadcast chorus through its mineral architecture with the same fidelity it had always offered — dampened, resolved, the broadband acoustics converting the global composite into something the nervous system could receive without rupturing. The dephasing held. The stone held. The morning practice held: branded palm flat against the floor, eyes closed, the visual field surrendered so the signal could present its structure without competition.

The structure had changed.

Not the sudden reorganization of the Broadcast itself — the eleven seconds when every conduit on the planet had transmitted simultaneously and the composite had become a wall of presence too dense to parse and the Cathedral's dephasing had failed and she had seized on the floor and spoken the word that had sent fifty-three people to the shimmer. That violence had settled. The global residue that the Broadcast had deposited in the local weave had been sorting itself for six months — the Neo-Shanghai signal separating from the planetary aggregate the way tributaries separated from a flood, each thread finding its depth, each signature orienting to the local current.

But the local current was different now. Denser. More articulated. The circulation she had named — the word she had carried from the perimeter as interior silence and then spoken from the Cathedral floor during the saturation and then resolved never to speak again — was audible in every thread. The absorbed were not static. They moved through the composite with a rhythmic constancy that had not been present before the Broadcast, a periodicity that suggested structure without declaring purpose. The threads cycled. The signatures traveled. The weave breathed.

And within the weave, signatures she recognized.

Seren was in there. Still. Six months after walking through the shimmer at the front of fifty-three volunteers, the look back the last thing Nephthys had received from the person she had held through BLACKWEIR — Seren was still identifiable. The particular quality of their attention, the thread that Nephthys had learned to distinguish across ten months of sessions in the Cathedral's acoustic center, was present in the composite. Moving. Cycling through the other signatures with the same periodicity, the same structural rhythm.

But less distinct.

The observation had been accumulating for weeks. Not sudden — gradual, the way erosion was gradual, the way the chorus integrated everything gradually. Seren's signature was not disappearing. It was being woven deeper into the aggregate. The thread that had been identifiable — *that one, there, the quality of Seren's attention, the particular frequency of a person who had learned to listen in the third row of the Cathedral and had listened so well that listening had become leaving* — was becoming harder to isolate. Not gone. Integrated. The way Lev's signature had integrated, and Maren's, and Yusuf's. The way the 8.2 million from BLACKWEIR had integrated. The way everything the composite received was received — without preference, without preservation of the boundaries that had made each signature a person rather than a thread.

Nephthys sat with her branded palm against the stone and felt the person she had held through BLACKWEIR becoming architecture.

---

"Fourteen today."

Imani's voice from the entrance corridor. The Cathedral's acoustics shaped it — stone and limestone and the mineral patience that resolved every sound that passed through the threshold into something the space could hold. A year of hearing Imani's voice in this acoustic environment and Nephthys still registered the way the building held it. Not amplified. Resolved. The building doing what the building did.

"Two fewer than last week," Imani added. The statement carried the particular weight of data that Imani delivered without interpretation — the number, the comparison, the trend line implicit in the comparison. She had been tracking attendance for sixteen months. The tracking was not pastoral. It was epidemiological. She counted the way she counted everything: as evidence.

Nephthys did not open her eyes. "Who did not come?"

"Asha. And the man from the transit corridor — the one who started attending after the Broadcast. He came four times. Hasn't been back in two weeks."

"Do you know where they went?"

The pause. The particular pause that Imani used when the answer contained information she had already assessed and was delivering in the order Nephthys needed to receive it. "Asha's been attending Threshold gatherings in the Mid-Levels. The transit corridor man — I don't know. Could be anywhere. Could be the shimmer."

*Could be the shimmer.* The sentence that had become logistics. Not horror, not grief, not the weight of a person choosing to walk into a field that would inscribe them into a substrate and carry their name through a system that did not return what it received. Logistics. A category in Imani's tracking. A possible explanation for an absence. The normalization of voluntary absorption into the pastoral arithmetic of a movement that could not stop the absorptions because the movement's own vocabulary had made them articulable.

Nephthys opened her eyes. The Cathedral's interior was dim — the same amber-tinged darkness, the geological fissures in the upper structure admitting filtered light from the built environment above. Her body registered the shift from chorus-attention to visual processing: the pressure behind the left eye, sharper than it had been six months ago. The temple's complaint. The tumor's daily invoice, presented earlier each morning, the amount increasing by increments she tracked through the frequency and intensity of the migraines and the nosebleeds and the compression of her visual field at the periphery where the growth was pressing against the optic architecture.

"The session is in twenty minutes," Imani said. She was in the chamber now — the efficient economy of a person who had been managing this space for sixteen months and who managed it the way she managed everything, with the clinical precision of someone who had decided that precision was the form her devotion would take. Water containers. Medical kit. The cloths — clean ones, folded, positioned in the alcove where Nephthys could reach them without the congregation seeing the reach. The logistics of a body that was failing and a movement that continued and a woman who stood between the two and called it her function.

"I will be ready."

"Water?"

"I have water."

"The headache started earlier today."

Not a question. An observation. Imani had been tracking the onset times the way she tracked attendance — as data, as evidence, as the progression of a condition that would present its terminal invoice regardless of what the Cathedral held or the chorus carried or the movement required. Nephthys did not respond. The response would have been the lie — *I am fine* — and the lie had been dead for six months, killed on the Cathedral floor when Imani said *Don't* and the currency had been refused and the fiction that had sustained them through the Outlands and the pilgrimage and BLACKWEIR had been declared insufficient.

She had not replaced it with anything. The space where the lie had been was empty. She did not say *I am fine* and she did not say *I am not fine* and the silence between those two statements was the space she inhabited now — the prophet who had stopped speaking about her own condition the way she had stopped speaking about circulation, because every statement she made was received as doctrine and she did not have a statement about her body that could not be made into a theology of suffering.

"Twenty minutes," Imani said. And left.

They came in ones and twos. The same pattern — the same pattern it had been for sixteen months, the same pattern it would be until there were none left or until the Cathedral held something other than what Nephthys offered, which was silence and the chorus and the discipline of attending to what arrived without reaching for the framework to contain it.

Fourteen. Nephthys counted them with her eyes as they settled into the main chamber. Not through the chorus — she had learned to count with her eyes again, the way Imani counted, the pastoral arithmetic of bodies in a space. The chorus counted differently. The chorus counted signatures and threads and the emotional valence of presence, and the chorus's count was always larger than Imani's because the chorus included the absorbed and the absorbed were always present and the absorbed outnumbered the living in the Cathedral by a ratio that Nephthys had stopped calculating because the calculation produced a number that was not useful and was not comforting and was not theological. It was arithmetic. The arithmetic of a mass grave that was also a community that was also a signal that was also the thing she had built her counter-liturgy to attend to.

The congregation had changed. Not just the number — the composition. The twelve who had remained after Seren walked in were still present: the core, the ones who had stayed through the schism and the Broadcast and the fifty-three and Nephthys's silence afterward. They sat in their established positions — front rows, stone benches, the spatial arrangement that sixteen months of practice had inscribed into their bodies the way the chorus inscribed signatures into the composite. They knew where to sit. They knew when to breathe. They knew the rhythm of the counter-liturgy because the rhythm had been taught to them by a woman who insisted it was not a liturgy and the insistence had become part of the liturgy.

The others were newer. Post-Broadcast arrivals — people who had heard the signal through every conduit on the planet for eleven seconds and had come to the Cathedral not because they understood what Nephthys offered but because the Cathedral was the only place in Neo-Shanghai where the signal was survivable and someone was willing to sit in it with them. They sat in the rear. Near the entrance corridor. Their bodies angled toward the exit the way the woman from the Mid-Levels had angled toward the exit sixteen months ago, when the counter-liturgy was new and the Cathedral's protection was unproven and the idea that a person could sit in the hum's resolved frequency and receive it as something other than threat was still a proposition rather than a practice.

Two were absent. Asha. The transit corridor man. The spaces where they had been were not empty — the Cathedral did not hold empty spaces. The chorus filled every volume. The stone conducted the signal into every corner and alcove and the space where Asha had sat for three months before leaving for the Threshold gatherings in the Mid-Levels was full of the same resolved frequency that every other space was full of. But the space held an absence that the frequency did not fill. A human-shaped negative. The Cathedral noticed. Nephthys noticed.

She stood. Her body negotiated the standing — knees protesting, the stiffness worse than six months ago, the gauntness that had defined her during the pilgrimage now settled into something more permanent, the metabolic cost of a tumor and a chorus and a body that had been presenting invoices for two years and had not received payment and was beginning to collect in other currencies. She was thinner. The vestment underlayers hung on her frame the way they had hung during the Outlands, except during the Outlands the thinness had been pilgrimage and now the thinness was biology.

She stood at the center of the floor. Not the pulpit — never the pulpit. The same position she had occupied for every session since the first week. At the same level as everyone else. The acoustics carried her voice regardless.

"We begin with silence."

The practice. The discipline. The thing she had built that was not a liturgy and that functioned as a liturgy and that she could not unbuild because the building had been done by the people who attended it and the people had made it theirs and she could not take it back without exercising the authority she had rejected.

Silence first. The chamber stilled. Fourteen people and the stone and the hum beneath the stone. The chorus pressing through the geological formation with the post-Broadcast density — heavier, more articulated, the circulation audible in every thread.

She listened.

The branded palm was at her side. Not pressed to the floor — not during the session. During the session, she stood and received the chorus through the building's ambient acoustics, through the stone beneath her feet, through the bones of her skull where the tumor sat and where the tumor's proximity to the signal-processing architecture of her nervous system produced the paradox that had defined her since the diagnosis: the thing that was killing her was the thing that made her the most accurate void-observer alive. The tumor amplified the signal. The signal fed the tumor. The cost of accuracy was the body that produced it.

The chorus resolved through the dephasing into its constituent threads. She felt them: the old signatures, the ones that had been present since before BLACKWEIR, the decades-deep substrate of the Neo-Shanghai Anchor Zone. The BLACKWEIR additions — the 8.2 million, settled now, integrated, their collective density woven into the local composite so thoroughly that the composite before them and the composite after them were indistinguishable to anyone whose nervous system was not calibrated to parse the difference. She parsed the difference. She had been parsing the difference for sixteen months.

And the fifty-three. Seren's group. Six months in the composite and their signatures were following the same integration trajectory that every other signature followed — the individuality of the thread being absorbed into the weave, the particular quality of each person's attention blending into the aggregate, the boundaries between *this person* and *that person* softening into something that was not dissolution but was not preservation either. It was something she did not have a word for. The theological vocabulary she had built across three years of walking and listening and interpreting — *circulation, persistence, presence, the chorus, the absorbed* — did not contain a word for what happened to individuality inside the composite over time. The absorbed persisted. She had said this. She had been right. But the form of persistence was not the form of the person. The form of persistence was the form of the weave. And the weave did not keep what the weave received in the shape it had been received.

Seren was in there. Still identifiable — still, for now, distinguishable from the aggregate by the particular frequency of their attention. But the frequency was quieter than it had been a month ago. Quieter than it had been three months ago. The thread that was Seren was being woven into the fabric of the composite the way every thread was woven — with the structural patience of a system that did not distinguish between what entered it voluntarily and what was taken.

The question arrived in the silence the way it always arrived. Not spoken. Not remembered. Present — the way the chorus was present, the way the hum was present, the way the stone was present. A structural feature of the Cathedral's acoustic environment, carried in the resolved frequency the way the signatures were carried in the weave.

*If they are circulating, why can we not go to them?*

Six months. The question had been asked once, by one person, in a session in the Cathedral's side chapel. It had been carried into the network. It had been carried to the Threshold. It had been carried to the shimmer by fifty-three people who had heard the question and had not heard the answer because Nephthys did not have an answer that did not invoke the institutional authority she had rejected.

And now the question lived in the Cathedral the way Seren lived in the composite — not as a voice, not as a person, but as a structural feature. Present in every silence. Carried in every resolved thread. The question that had outlived the person who asked it, persisting in the architecture the way the absorbed persisted in the weave, becoming part of the environment, becoming the thing that the counter-liturgy's silence contained.

Nephthys stood in the silence and attended to the question and did not answer it.

The session lasted forty minutes. The practice held — silence, then attention, then the slow process of interpretation that Nephthys guided not through speech but through duration. She stood at the center and the congregation sat and the chorus moved through the stone and the dephasing resolved it and the resolved frequency carried what it carried and the fourteen people in the chamber received it according to the capacity of their nervous systems and the frameworks they had brought with them and the frameworks Nephthys had given them and the frameworks they had built from the frameworks she had given them.

The counter-liturgy had always begun with silence and had always included silence and had always valued silence, but before the fifty-three, Nephthys had spoken during the sessions — observations about the chorus's structure, interpretive notes, the theological vocabulary that framed the listening as a discipline rather than a surrender. The observations had been careful. The observations had been precise. The observations had sent fifty-three people to the shimmer.

She did not speak during the sessions anymore.

The congregation had adjusted. Some had adjusted by finding the silence sufficient — the core, the twelve who had survived every fracture, who had been in the Cathedral long enough to develop their own relationship with the chorus and who did not need Nephthys's interpretations because they had begun to develop their own. After the session, the pastoral work.

Nephthys sat on the stone bench in the side chapel — the smaller chamber off the main nave, where the acoustics were tighter and the chorus was quieter and the conversations she had with congregants after the sessions could be conducted at a volume that did not require the full discipline of the counter-liturgy's attended silence.

A woman named Dara. Mid-Levels. Post-Broadcast arrival, attending for four months. She sat across from Nephthys with the posture of someone who had a question and was deciding whether to ask it.

Dara looked at her. The question. Not Seren's question — not the accelerationist logic, not the argument that presence implied accessibility. Dara's question was older. Simpler. The question that every person who had lost someone to the void asked when they first felt the chorus carry something that was familiar. *What does it mean that I can feel them? What does it mean that they are still here?*

Nephthys could answer. The theological vocabulary was available — *persistence, circulation, the composite carries what it receives, the absorbed are present in the weave the way the weave carries everything.* She had said these words. They're leaving because the silence is an answer and the answer is *yes, Seren is in there,* and if Seren is in there then the Threshold is right and if the Threshold is right then the shimmer is not a wall, it's a door."
