She counted before she opened her eyes.

Creds: twenty-two. Room: paid through Wednesday — nine per day, eighteen committed, leaving four for food. Rice: half a bag in the cabinet. Oil: gone since last week. Greens: none. Adhesive: day eleven of twelve — tomorrow she would need a new tube, eleven creds, and the eleven subtracted from twenty-two left eleven and the eleven minus nine for Thursday's rent left two and two was not enough for rice and the arithmetic was the arithmetic and the arithmetic would resolve the way it always resolved: Fen's morning glory, the documentation group's next topology payment, the incremental survival of a woman whose budget operated at the margin between sufficient and not.

The frozen notification.

*Lin, Nuwa. Credential tier: Basic. Evacuation priority: None assigned.*

Beside it, fourteen green notifications. The network had been running at this density for weeks — the post-Proposition traffic, the mages and coordinators and correspondents processing what the Proposition had confirmed and what the confirmation meant for the systems they had built to document the thing the Proposition had described.

She did not read them yet. She would read them after the rice.

She opened her eyes. The ceiling. The concrete. The hum through the masonry walls — steady, residential-grade. Twenty-four months. Two years of this hum. The number was round in a way that numbers rarely were in the inventory — creds and days and adhesive cycles running in irregular sequences that never aligned, the arithmetic of survival producing remainders and fractions and the perpetual non-zero balance of a life that did not resolve to even numbers. Two years was even. The evenness was the datum.

She stood. Brushed her teeth. Boiled water. Made rice — the last of the half-bag, measured into the cooker, the portion calibrated for one meal and no more. She ate standing at the sink.

---

The adhesive tube was on the shelf. Day eleven of twelve. She unsealed the cap and ran the line along the hairline crack. Thumbnail into the split. Counted to thirty. The chip was in the port — Fuxi's thirteen years, the corroded contacts, the junction maps and conduit densities and the names in the metadata field that she had cross-referenced with the testimony network's database and the Broadcast's forty-seven and the documentation group's Lagos integration and the fourteen-city archive that counted what the system did not count.

The counting had stopped.

Not her counting. The system's. The Proposition — forty-seven minutes, the aggregate speaking in the vocabulary the aggregate had chosen, the physics of boundary and equilibrium and permanence — had confirmed what the data had been approaching for months: the number was fixed. The boundary was permanent. The absorbed would not return. The count — 8.2 million in Neo-Shanghai, the Lagos zone, Mumbai's dead (killed by autonomous fire, not absorbed — the distinction that the institutional vocabulary maintained and that the dead did not), the threshold volunteers, the smaller breaches across fourteen cities — was final.

She had read the Feed's summary on the Level 3 terminal three days ago. The institutional language: *equilibrium confirmed*, *boundary stabilization permanent*, *repatriation protocols suspended indefinitely*. The system's vocabulary for a number that would not change. The vocabulary was administrative. The number was not.

She pulled on her jacket. The mechanical latch clicked behind her.

---

She stopped at Fen's door. Knocked once.

"Morning," Fen said through the door.

"Do you have paper?"

A pause. The pause was the brief assessment — Fen measuring a question that did not belong in the margin's daily vocabulary, where paper was wrapping and insulation and the material Shuang's children drew on with the stubs of marking pens from the Level 2 vendor's discard bin.

The door opened. Fen looked at her.

"Paper," Fen said.

"A notebook. Or sheets. Anything I can write on."

"What are you writing?"

"A count."

Fen's expression did not change. The word was sufficient — Fen knew what Nuwa counted. Fen had lived beside the counting for two years. The counting was the architecture of their corridor the way the masonry was the architecture of their walls.

"Shuang has composition books. The ones from the education distribution last month — her children got three each. She'll have one to spare."

"I'll pay for it."

"You'll ask for it. Shuang doesn't sell to neighbors."

Nuwa's hands were at her sides. The reflex — the arithmetic that converted every exchange into a transaction. She could feel it. The reflex was older than the margins.

"Tell her I'll update the topology for her sector. The path-poor structures near Level 2 have changed since the licensing went through."

"I'll tell her."

---

The composition book was on her table by noon.

Blue cover. Lined pages. The kind manufactured for institutional education programs — the standardized format, the margins ruled, the paper weight sufficient for ink but designed for the graphite pencils the education distribution provided. The book was thin. Forty pages.

She sat at the table. The folding surface with the cardboard shim. She placed the composition book on the surface and opened it to the first page and the page was blank and the blankness was the thing she was going to fill with the names of people who were inside the infrastructure that hummed through the walls of the room where she sat.

She did not have a pen. She had a marking stylus from the overlay repair kit — the tool she used to clean the cracked housing's contacts when the moisture residue from the hairline split accumulated on the pins. The fine tip held ink. She had tested it on the cardboard shim — the line was thin, consistent, the characters legible if she wrote at the speed the tip required and did not press through the education-grade paper.

She wrote the date. She wrote: *The count.*

She opened the overlay. Tilted her head to compensate for the dead zone.

The testimony network's database. The names. She had been accumulating them for eighteen months — every submission that included a name, every testimony that identified an absorbed individual by the identifier the institution classified as *personal data* and that Nuwa classified as the thing a person was called by the people who knew them. The database held 4,211 verified names from Neo-Shanghai. Cross-referenced with junction coordinates. Cross-referenced with Fuxi's conduit density logs. Cross-referenced with the Broadcast — the forty-seven-minute transmission where the aggregate had spoken through 8.2 million voices and Nuwa had recognized forty-seven.

She began with the forty-seven.

*Mrs. Huang. Block 14, Unit 7. Junction coordinates: 14-East-7.2.*

The stylus moved across the lined paper. The characters were small — the handwriting of a woman whose spatial economics applied to everything, including the space between letters. The iridescent staining on her fingertips caught the light from the ventilation shaft window as the stylus moved — the solvent residue from the textile reclamation plant, eight years of second shift, the chemical signature the Sump had written on her hands and that the hands carried into every surface they touched. She wrote each name. Each unit number. Each junction coordinate that Fuxi's data chip had mapped and that the chip in her cracked implant still held and that she accessed now through the overlay's damaged interface, tilting her head, the dead zone shifting as the data rendered.

*Yuto. Block 14, Unit 12. Junction coordinates: 14-East-12.1.*

*Lin Wei. Block 22, Unit 3. Junction coordinates: 22-South-3.4.*

Her mother. *Lin Mei. Block 14, Unit 7. Junction coordinates: 14-East-7.2. Cause of death: chronic nitro-exposure respiratory failure — pre-BLACKWEIR.* Not absorbed. Dead before the absorption. Dead in the institutional bed with the monitoring and the institutional geometry and the institutional vocabulary that had called chronic nitro-exposure *occupational environmental factor* and the occupational environmental factor had filled her mother's lungs and the lungs had stopped and the stopping had been called *natural causes* and the natural causes were the infrastructure.

She wrote her mother's name on the lined paper with the implant-maintenance stylus and the writing was not the counting and the counting was not the mourning and the mourning was not the point. The point was the record. The point was the name on a surface that could not be reclassified or deaccessioned or deleted by the system that had reclassified and deaccessioned and deleted the residential records of the people the names belonged to.

The forty-seven filled two pages.

The names from Fuxi's data chip — the maintenance logs that included personnel records, the junction assignments that listed the workers who serviced each corridor, the metadata field where Fuxi's handwriting had annotated the infrastructure coordinates with the names of the people who lived above them — filled fourteen pages. Some names overlapped with the forty-seven. She noted the overlap. She did not eliminate the duplicates. The overlap was data — a name appearing in two sources confirmed by two systems and the confirmation was the architecture.

The testimony network's 4,211 filled the remaining pages. She wrote smaller. The characters compressed — the spatial economics of a woman running out of paper the way she ran out of creds, incrementally, the inventory shrinking as the work expanded.

She ran out of pages at 3,847.

She sat at the table. Two hours. The composition book was full — blue cover, forty pages, the lined paper filled with characters written in implant-maintenance ink by a woman whose handwriting was the handwriting of someone who had never expected to write anything longer than an inventory list and who was now writing the longest document of her life and the document was names.

364 names remaining. She needed another book.

---

She walked to Shuang's unit on Level 4.

The corridor was dim. Second-floor lighting — code-minimum. She touched the masonry wall as she passed. The stone was cool. Dense. Two years of the hand confirming what the body knew.

Shuang opened the door. The two children were visible behind her — seated at a low table, drawing with marking pen stubs on sheets torn from composition books. The older one was drawing the building. The younger one was drawing the building on fire. Children drew what children drew.

"Another one?" Shuang said.

Nuwa had not said what the first one was for. Shuang had not asked. The margin's protocol — the exchange of objects without the exchange of explanations, the economy of materials flowing between rooms the way information flowed between corridors, the architecture of neighbors who provided what was needed because the providing was the architecture.

"If you have one."

Shuang reached behind the door. Produced a composition book — green cover, same format. "The path-poor update for Level 2?"

"I'll have it by tomorrow."

"Then we're even."

The exchange was complete. Nuwa walked back to her room. The mechanical latch clicked behind her.

---

She finished the 4,211 in the green book's first seven pages.

Then the other cities. The network's consolidated data — the numbers she had been routing and cross-referencing and filing since she was answering queries from the hospital corridor, the data from fourteen cities that had flowed through her cracked implant because the hub was hospitalized and the data needed someone to carry it and the someone had been a woman sitting on an institutional floor with a visitor pass that expired at 18:00.

Lagos. 1.2 million absorbed. 892 verified names in the Lagos testimony network's archive. She wrote the number and the archive reference. She did not write the 892 names — the names were in Lagos, maintained by the Lagos coordinator whose data was the Lagos coordinator's data, and the record was the record and the record was distributed across the network the way the network distributed everything: by the people who were closest to the corridors the data described.

Mumbai. Not absorbed — killed. She wrote the distinction. The autonomous fire that an orbiatal defense system had deployed. The distinction mattered. The absorbed were in the infrastructure — present, integrated, the conscious collective field that the Broadcast had rendered audible and that the Proposition had described as permanent. The Mumbai dead were dead. The distinction was the distinction between a number that described people inside a system and a number that described people the system had destroyed, and the distinction was the only thing the dead had that the absorbed did not have: the clarity of a word the vocabulary had always known.

She wrote each city. Each number. Each archive reference. The threshold volunteers — the people who had walked into the Anchor Zones intentionally, whose absorption was chosen rather than inflicted, whose names the network recorded in a separate category because the category mattered even if the boundary did not distinguish.

The total.

She did not write the total as a single number. She wrote it as an inventory — each source, each city, each category, the numbers arranged on the page the way creds and rice and adhesive were arranged in the morning count. The total was the sum. The sum was the sum. She wrote the sum at the bottom of the page and the sum was a number and the number was final and the finality was the Proposition's confirmation and the confirmation was the physics and the physics did not consult the committee and the committee did not consult the count and the count was hers.

The stylus stopped.

She sat at the table. Two composition books — blue and green, forty pages each. The books were on the table beside the adhesive tube and the empty oil bottle and the business card she had not thrown away. The books were objects in a room that did not accumulate objects, and the objects were the shape of a count, and the count was hers.

---

She opened the overlay. Tilted her head.

The testimony network's archive interface. She had uploaded data before — junction coordinates, conduit densities, topology updates. The process was the same. Select the archive channel. Select the access classification: open. No institutional review. No six-to-eight-week processing period. No classification tier that determined who could read what had been filed and who could not and the determination was the system's architecture for the management of information the system preferred to manage rather than distribute.

Open access. The classification that meant anyone on the network could read it. The mages. The coordinators. The correspondents. The documentation group. The margin residents. The institutional researchers who monitored the testimony network through the academic channels they maintained alongside the channels the academies provided. Anyone.

She entered the data from the composition books. Name by name. Junction coordinate by junction coordinate. The overlay's text interface was slow — the cracked housing processing each character with the half-second delay that damage imposed on every function — and the slowness was the pace of the work and the pace was the pace of a woman entering each name into a system that would hold the name the way the paper held the ink: permanently, without classification, without the institutional vocabulary that converted names into data points and data points into aggregate statistics and aggregate statistics into the language the committee used when the committee said *equilibrium confirmed* and meant *they are not coming back.*

She entered names for four hours.

The overlay's battery indicator pulsed — the cracked housing's power management, unreliable, the warning that meant the system would shut down in thirty minutes or three hours depending on factors the damage had made unpredictable. She continued. The names entered the archive. The archive received them.

At the end, she entered the header. The document's title: *The Count.* The author field: *Nuwa Lin.* The date. The source list: *testimony network database; Broadcast recognition (personal); Fuxi maintenance logs (data chip, salvaged); personal memory.* The classification: *open access — no institutional review — permanent.*

She submitted.

The archive processed. The green notification appeared — the system's confirmation that the document was filed and the filing was complete and the document was in the network and the network was in fourteen cities and the fourteen cities were the architecture that the counting had built.

The green notification sat beside the frozen grey one. The two systems. The one that had archived her residential record and the one that now held the record she had archived. The first had made her a line of grey text that did not update. The second now held a document with her name on it and 4,211 names inside it and the names were the names and the names were permanent and the permanence was not the system's — it was hers.

---

She cooked in the evening. Rice — borrowed from Fen, a cup, enough for tonight. She would buy more tomorrow with the creds from the documentation group's next topology payment, and the payment would come when the Level 2 path-poor update was complete, and the completion was tomorrow's work.

She ate at the table. The folding surface with the cardboard shim. The two composition books were stacked beside her bowl — blue on the bottom, green on top. The paper record. The physical object that the network's digital archive duplicated and that the duplication did not replace because the paper was the paper and the paper was in her room and her room was the room and the room was hers.

The overlay pulsed. She opened it. Tilted her head.

Seventeen green notifications. The network receiving the count. Yara: *Received. Integrating with Lagos database. This is the first consolidated ground-truth record from the testimony network. Open access confirmed.* The Lagos coordinator: *Cross-referencing with local archive. Verified names being added to combined database.* The Dharavi correspondent — the one who counted nineteen, the one who had written *it is not the same verb*: *The count is received. Mumbai's numbers are here. The distinction — killed, not absorbed — is preserved. Thank you for preserving the distinction.*

The others were from handles she recognized and handles she did not. Mages. Coordinators. Margin residents. People in fourteen cities who had spent months counting what the system did not count and who were now receiving a document that consolidated the counting into a single record and the record was open and the record was permanent and the record had names.

She did not reply. She would reply tomorrow. The replies would be data — topology updates, junction coordinates, the work that continued because the count was the foundation and the foundation was complete and the structure the foundation supported was the ongoing work of keeping people alive in the margins the system produced.

The frozen notification sat at the edge of her vision. Grey. Permanent.

*Lin, Nuwa. Credential tier: Basic. Evacuation priority: None assigned.*

The green notifications sat beside it. Seventeen and accumulating.

She closed the overlay. Washed the bowl. One bowl. One pair of chopsticks. One mug. The count had not changed. Everything she owned could still be packed in four minutes — and two composition books, blue and green, forty pages each, that could not be packed because the packing would fold the pages and the folding would crease the names and the names were the record and the record was the first thing she owned that she would carry differently.

She sat on the mattress. The hum through the masonry walls. Steady. The frequency that meant the system continued.

The count was in the archive. The count was on paper. The count was final — the number that would not change, the boundary that would not open, the absorbed who would not return. The finality was the thing she had been counting toward since the Broadcast, since the forty-seven names, since the frozen notification appeared on her overlay and the system that generated it was decommissioned and the decommissioning was called *remediation* and the remediation was called *complete*.

The count was complete. The work was not.

The topology updates. The network coordination. The path-poor classifications for Shuang's sector. The margin residents in Sectors 8 and 9 who needed junction data she carried in a cracked implant behind her left ear. The documentation group's integration. The mages' queries. The corridors that needed mapping and the people who needed the maps and the maps that needed the person who had walked the corridors and survived the walking and carried the walking in the body's memory of which walls mattered.

The count was a period. The work was the sentence that continued after it.

The inventory was open. She was not done.

**END CHAPTER 43**
