> *"When a boundary revision supersedes a prior survey, the superseded map shall be retained in the national archive in its original form, uncorrected. The archive preserves what the territory was. The territory does not consult the archive."*
>
> — Cartographic Standards for Boundary Revision in Disputed Territories, 2161 ed., §14

The Jordanian flag had lost the red.

Sixteen months. The holographic emitter in the northeastern corner of the Hall of Nations had progressed past flicker into selective failure — the red triangle no longer cycling through its dissolution-reconstitution pattern but absent entirely, the display rendering the white star suspended in null space above the green, white, and black horizontal stripes, a section of the projection that the emitter's degrading components had stopped attempting to produce. The flag of a country whose diplomatic tradition Zeyad carried in his skeleton now flew in the Hall of Nations as three-quarters of itself. The white star floated in the absence where the red had been. The stripes held. The maintenance request — three filings, two follow-up annotations, one formal query through Dr. Diallo's channel, all predating his suspension — remained in the procedural queue of the Building Services division, which reported to the Grounds and Protocol office, which reported to the Administrative Secretariat, which reported to the budget committee, which had not convened on non-emergency facilities items since the Broadcast because the Broadcast had converted every agenda item into an emergency item and the emergency items had consumed the committee's calendar and the non-emergency items had settled into the procedural sediment where all unfunded requests settled, and the flag was not an emergency.

He watched it from the gallery. Third row, eastern section. The grey unlaminated access pass was clipped to his breast pocket — sixteen months old, the stock so softened by the friction of daily use that the edges had lost their geometry entirely, the corners not rounded but dissolved, the institutional typography of his name beginning to merge with the grey of the stock beneath it. VISITOR — GALLERY ACCESS — RESTRICTED. The letters were legible. They would not be legible much longer. The pass would outlast its own readability, the credential persisting after the credential's content had worn away — the institutional equivalent of the flag, displaying its category without displaying what the category contained.

The pen was in his breast pocket. The notebook was in his jacket's inner pocket — the black hardcover purchased at the Rue du Rhône stationer sixteen months ago, now filled past the seven-eighths mark, the remaining pages measurable in days rather than weeks. He did not take either out. The session below was procedural. The emergency session's Standing Committee on Nitro Governance was hearing motions.

NitroCore's motions.

---

The de-escalation proposal had been introduced eleven weeks ago by a coalition of fourteen delegations — the same number, Zeyad had noted in the margin of his notebook, as the FAS infrastructure summit vote forty-one years ago. Fourteen delegations who had looked at the Broadcast data, at the synchronization evidence, at the atmospheric modeling that every credentialed and non-credentialed researcher on the planet had confirmed in the six months since the void spoke through the world's infrastructure, and had proposed what the data demanded: a phased de-escalation of conduit throughput in breach-adjacent territories, beginning with the seven largest Anchor Zones, reducing output by twelve percent over eighteen months, with monitoring provisions and economic transition support funded by a levy on grid-dependent extraction revenue.

The proposal was not radical. The proposal was less aggressive than the twenty-percent reduction the emergency session had voted for and NitroCore had injuncted in forty minutes. The proposal was a compromise of a compromise — the institutional vocabulary for a position that had already conceded the structural argument in order to achieve a procedural outcome. Twelve percent instead of twenty. Eighteen months instead of five years. Breach-adjacent territories instead of global. The proposal had been drafted in the language the committee required, invoked the provisions the committee recognized, and followed the procedural pathway the committee's rules prescribed for emergency-session recommendations.

NitroCore's legal team had filed seventeen motions in response.

Not seventeen objections. Seventeen motions — each a discrete procedural action requiring committee acknowledgment, each acknowledgment requiring a response from the proposing coalition, each response requiring a committee vote on whether the response was procedurally sufficient, each sufficiency vote requiring a quorum that the committee's attendance patterns made achievable only on Tuesdays and Thursdays between 10:00 and 16:00. The motions did not address the proposal's content. The motions addressed the proposal's procedural architecture — jurisdictional scope, evidentiary standards, economic impact assessment methodology, the definition of "breach-adjacent," the classification of monitoring data, the legal standing of non-signatory researchers cited in the supporting documentation.

Seventeen motions. At the committee's current processing rate — two motions per session, two sessions per week — the motions would require approximately nine weeks to resolve. By which time NitroCore's legal team would have filed additional motions addressing the procedural outcomes of the first seventeen, and the committee would process those, and the processing would produce more motions, and the motions would produce more processing, and the proposal would move through the institutional machinery at the speed the machinery was designed to move: the speed of a system whose function was not to decide but to defer, and whose deferral was not dysfunction but architecture.

The committee was on motion seven.

Zeyad watched from the gallery as the committee chair — Delegate Pereira, the Brazilian representative who had replaced Dr. Adeyemi on the Standing Committee after Adeyemi's rotation to the tribunal panel — read motion seven into the record. The motion challenged the evidentiary basis for the proposal's definition of "breach-adjacent territory" on the grounds that the atmospheric data cited by the proposing coalition had been collected by researchers whose institutional affiliation did not meet the Accords' standard for independent verification. The same argument. The same classification. *Non-institutional.* The word that had been applied to Sofia Reyes's presentation, to Mirelle's evidence, to every piece of data that originated outside the corporate-governmental complex whose throughput the data threatened. The institution did not need to refute the evidence. It needed only to classify the source.

The committee voted on the motion's admissibility. The vote was procedural — not on the substance of the challenge but on whether the challenge met the formal requirements for committee consideration. The vote passed twelve to four. The challenge was admitted. The proposing coalition was given fourteen days to respond with supplementary evidentiary documentation meeting the Accords' institutional affiliation standard.

The proposal was not rejected. The proposal was tabled indefinitely.

The distinction was the machinery. Rejection required a vote on substance. Tabling required only the accumulation of unresolved procedural motions sufficient to prevent the substance from reaching the floor. The committee had not voted against de-escalation. The committee had voted to process motions about de-escalation until the de-escalation became procedurally inaccessible. The outcome was identical. The record was different. The record showed a committee diligently processing a complex proposal through the appropriate channels. The record did not show a committee killing a proposal. The record showed a proposal dying of natural causes — procedural attrition, the institutional equivalent of exposure.

Zeyad's hands were on the gallery railing. The composure held — the same load-bearing architecture, the same practiced stillness, the same institutional posture that had carried him through thirty-four years of committee sessions and ten months of tribunal and the Lagos breach and Mumbai and the Broadcast and the forty minutes. The composure held because the composure had no other function. The composure was what remained after every other institutional tool had been stripped — the credentials, the floor access, the filing authority, the committee vote, the diplomatic register's capacity to convert speech into action. The composure was the last institutional artifact. It performed its function. Its function was to hold.

Below, the committee moved to motion eight.

---

The diplomatic residence was three blocks from the UGC headquarters. The same building. The same room. The same desk. Sixteen months of transitional housing — the institutional euphemism had long since ceased to describe a transition and now described a permanence that the institution could not acknowledge as permanent because acknowledging permanence would require converting his status from *respondent in active proceedings* to something the institutional vocabulary did not contain: a man the institution had finished with but could not discharge because discharging him would close the proceedings and closing the proceedings would require a finding and a finding would require the tribunal to assess what the institution had done and what the institution had done was the thing the institution was prosecuting Zeyad for documenting.

He sat at the desk. The pen was beside the notebook. The grey pass was beside the pen — placed on the desk surface the way he had placed three credentials before it: the diplomatic pass, the COURTESY badge in Lagos, the grey unlaminated stock that had been his daily credential for sixteen months. The ritual of arrival. The ritual persisted. The credential it organized had changed three times. The ritual did not consult the credential.

The notebook was open to the page where he had stopped writing two days ago. The replacement document. Sixty-three pages of handwritten text — small, precise, the lettering of a man who had spent thirty-four years writing in institutional margins and was now writing the document the margins had been preparing for. Not a framework submission. Not a committee memorandum. Not the Lagos document that had been tabled without discussion. The replacement.

*What follows is an account of what the Accords were designed to do, what they did instead, and what must replace them.*

The opening line, written during the Broadcast event in the gallery, the ink from the pen that had signed the original Accords. The document had grown from that sentence the way the Accords themselves had grown from Khalil's handshake — one commitment producing the architecture that would contain it, the architecture producing the provisions that would govern it, the provisions producing the institutional machinery that would process it. But the replacement document was not institutional architecture. The replacement document was the record of the architecture's failure written in the same hand that had operated within the architecture for thirty-four years, by a man sitting in a diplomatic residence three blocks from the building where the architecture was at this moment processing motion eight of seventeen motions designed to prevent the architecture from being modified.

He picked up the pen. He opened the notebook to the next blank page — one of the last, the remaining stock thin enough to feel through the cover.

He wrote:

*Section 9: The Procedural Neutralization of Democratic Action. On this date, the Standing Committee on Nitro Governance processed motion seven of seventeen motions filed by NitroCore Energy Holdings in response to the De-Escalation Proposal (coalition of fourteen delegations). The motions do not address the proposal's substance. The motions address the proposal's procedural architecture. The effect is identical to rejection without the institutional record of rejection. The proposal will be tabled. The tabling will be recorded as procedural necessity. The record will show a committee processing motions. The record will not show a committee preventing action. The distinction between the two is the Accords' terminal function: the architecture processes. The processing prevents. The prevention is the architecture.*

The pen moved with the same precision it had always moved with — small, controlled, the handwriting steady despite the vibration through the building's foundation. The hum. New Geneva's register — ambient, institutional, the low-frequency presence through the residence's concrete and steel that Zeyad had felt for six years as an attaché and sixteen months as a defendant and that he felt now as a man whose body had been to Lagos and whose body remembered what the hum could become when the system that managed it exceeded the parameters the system had been designed to maintain. The New Geneva hum was in the foundation. It had always been in the foundation. The Lagos hum — the one that had risen through his soles, his ankles, his shins, his chest — was in his memory. Both hums were present. One was infrastructure. The other was the body's record of what the infrastructure could do.

He wrote three more paragraphs. The analysis was precise — the same procedural pattern recognition he had applied to thirty-four years of institutional documents, the same instinct for the shape of suppression that had identified the Alpha-7 classification as anomalous before he had any evidence, the same capacity to read the gap between what the process should produce and what the process actually produced. The gap was the document's subject. The gap was sixty-three pages wide and growing.

The notebook was nearly full.

He paused. The pen was still. The last pages — five, perhaps six — would hold the document's final sections. The conclusion. The recommendation that was not a recommendation because a recommendation required an institution to receive it and the institution that should receive it was the institution the document diagnosed. The replacement document would be complete. It would be the most thorough analysis of the Accords' structural failure ever written — more comprehensive than the Lagos framework submission, more detailed than the disclosure that had produced his prosecution, more architecturally precise than anything the committee's own archives contained. It would describe what the Accords were designed to do. It would describe what they did instead. It would describe what must replace them.

It would be written in a notebook purchased at a stationer on the Rue du Rhône, three blocks from the building where his great-grandfather had purchased the pen. It would be written in ink from a cartridge replaced in Lagos. It would be written by a man with a grey unlaminated access pass and no institutional authority and no audience and no channel through which the document could reach the committee that should read it, because the committee that should read it was at this moment processing motion eight of seventeen motions filed by the corporation whose throughput the document proposed to reduce, and the committee would process the motions and the motions would process the committee and the processing would continue and the document would be complete and the document would exist in a notebook in a desk in a diplomatic residence three blocks from the Hall of Nations where the Jordanian flag had lost its green.

The hum was in the foundation. The pen was in his hand. The ink was not fading. The flag was. The notebook was filling. The institution was processing. The replacement document was approaching its conclusion. The conclusion would be written. The conclusion would describe what must replace the Accords. The conclusion would be filed — because filing was what the hand did, because filing was the inheritance, because the pen continued.

The pen continued.
