She counted before she opened her eyes.

Creds: forty-one. Room: paid through Saturday — nine per day, thirty-six committed, leaving five for food. Rice: half a bag in the cabinet — three days if she split the portions. Oil: a quarter bottle. Greens: a bundle of water spinach from the Level 3 market, bought yesterday, in the damp cloth on the shelf. Adhesive: day two of twelve. The tube was new.

The arithmetic resolved without remainder.

She had not noticed this immediately. The numbers had run the way they always ran — automatic, the mind's first language, arriving before anything else. But the numbers had arrived and the numbers had settled and the settling was different. Not surplus — forty-one creds was not surplus. Forty-one creds was the margin between sufficient and not, the same margin she had operated inside for two years. But the margin was holding. The documentation group's topology payments had stabilized — forty creds per update, one update every ten days, the data flowing from her cracked implant into the fourteen-city database and the payment flowing back and the flow was steady and the steadiness was not the system's architecture. It was the architecture she had built beside it.

The frozen notification. Still there. Still the first thing the overlay rendered, before the transit map, before the Feed, before the permanent grey of the dead zone in the upper-right quadrant.

*Lin, Nuwa. Credential tier: Basic. Evacuation priority: None assigned.*

Beside it, three green notifications. She would read them after the rice.

She opened her eyes. The ceiling. The concrete. The hum through the masonry walls — steady, residential-grade. Twenty-six months.

She stood. Brushed her teeth. Boiled water. Made rice.

She sliced the water spinach. Heated oil in the pan — a thin layer, enough to coat the surface. The greens went in. The edges crisped. The smell filled the room — green, sharp, the smell of a choice made with oil and greens and the time it took to heat the pan and wait for the edges to darken.

She ate at the table.

The folding surface with the cardboard shim. The two composition books beside her bowl — blue on the bottom, green on top. They had been there since she wrote them. Two months. The books did not move. They were the first objects in the room that had a position that was not temporary — not propped against the wall like the table before she started using it, not on the shelf beside the adhesive tube in the rotation of things that depleted and were replaced. The books were permanent. The books held names.

She ate. The rice and the greens and the oil. The table. The morning light from the ventilation shaft — grey, reflected, sufficient.

---

The adhesive tube was on the shelf. Day two of twelve. She unsealed the cap and ran the line along the hairline crack in the implant housing behind her left ear. Thumbnail into the split. Counted to thirty. The chip was in the port — Fuxi's thirteen years, the corroded contacts, the junction maps and conduit densities that were now in a database in fourteen cities and in two composition books on a folding table and in the archive of a testimony network that had received the count and integrated it and continued.

She pulled on her jacket. The mechanical latch clicked behind her.

She did not stop at Fen's door. Fen was at the market — Wednesday, the early delivery, the produce stall where the woman who accepted unregistered cred set out the morning glory and the cabbage before the regular customers arrived. Nuwa knew Fen's schedule the way she knew the adhesive cycle and the topology payment dates and the hum's daily variation — by the pattern, by the inventory, by the body's registration of the rhythms that organized the building's life the way the hum organized the building's walls.

She descended the stairs. Second-floor lighting. Code-minimum. The masonry walls carried the hum muffled and reduced. She touched the wall as she passed. The stone was cool. Dense. Twenty-six months of the hand confirming what the body knew. The confirmation was not checking anymore. The confirmation was the hand doing what the hand did — touching walls, reading surfaces, the tactile habit that had begun as implant-itch avoidance and become survival intelligence and become the knowledge she traded for topology payments and become the hand touching a wall because the wall was there and the hand was there and the touching was the relationship between a body and the structure that held it.

---

The Level 3 market was quiet. Wednesday morning — the early hour, before the transit junction's foot traffic reached the stalls, before the district's tourism infrastructure activated its platforms and its vendors and its observation decks. The market existed in the gap between the district's sleeping and its waking, and the gap was the time when the margin residents shopped and the shopping was the commerce that preceded the tourism and that would continue after the tourism because the commerce was food and food did not require a proximity experience licensing framework.

She bought greens. A tube of adhesive — eleven creds, the same price, the one commodity in the margin whose cost had not shifted. The arithmetic: forty-one minus eleven minus the greens left enough. She did not calculate the remainder to the cred. She calculated it to the category — enough or not enough — and the category was enough and the category was sufficient.

The woman at the far stall counted the payment. Unregistered cred. The counting was transactional, efficient, the arithmetic of a vendor whose inventory was produce. The woman did not look up. The woman had been accepting Nuwa's unregistered cred for twenty-six months and the twenty-six months had produced the thing that repetition produced in the margins — not friendship, not familiarity, but the absence of assessment. The woman did not assess her. The woman counted the creds and handed her the greens and the exchange was the exchange and the exchange did not require the architecture of recognition or the vocabulary of introduction or the weight of a name said in a transit corridor by a stranger who had found her in a database.

She walked home along the masonry wall.

---

The overlay pulsed. She opened it at the table. Tilted her head to compensate for the dead zone.

Three green notifications.

The first was from the Lagos coordinator. The weekly data summary — the consolidated archive report that listed the network's current holdings by city, by contributor, by access classification. Her count was a line in the summary. *Neo-Shanghai Ground-Truth Record: Nuwa Lin, primary contributor. Status: integrated. Cross-references: 14 active databases.* She read the line the way she read everything — once for inventory, once for structure. The line was an inventory entry. The line placed her count inside a larger architecture. The line was administrative. The line was sufficient.

The second was a topology update from a handle she recognized — a margin resident in Sector 9 whose query she had answered months ago and who now contributed corridor-level data to the network's database independently. New construction on Level 3 — a path-rich structure, metal-framed polymer, being built over a junction node she had flagged in a previous update. The resident had measured the conduit density with a borrowed industrial monitor. The reading was attached.

She filed it. Forwarded it to the documentation group. The work. The work that continued because the count was the foundation and the foundation was complete and the structure the foundation supported was the ongoing work of keeping people alive in the margins the system produced.

The third was from the Dharavi correspondent. The one who counted nineteen. The one who had written *it is not the same verb.*

*A woman came to the textile workshop last week. She was looking for the wall. The wall that saved me. She said she had read about it in the testimony network's archive — the brick-lime boundary, the loom frames, the nineteen names. She was the daughter of one of the nineteen. She touched the wall. She did not take a piece of it. She touched it.*

Nuwa read the message. Once for inventory. Once for structure. Once more.

The daughter of one of the nineteen. Touching the wall that had not saved her parent. The parent absorbed. The wall still standing — masonry, brick-lime boundary, the structure that had survived because the structure was the kind of structure that survived. The daughter had found the wall through the archive. Through the count. Through the names that Nuwa and the Dharavi correspondent had written and uploaded and classified as *open access — no institutional review — permanent.* The names had led a daughter to a wall. The wall was still there. The daughter touched it.

She did not take a piece.

In the Anchor margin district four blocks south, the masonry chips were still for sale. Eighty creds each. The wall that had saved Nuwa — the masonry annex in Block 14, the poured concrete, the room she had sat in with a wet cloth on her face while the eleven seconds consumed everyone who was not behind walls like that one — was a product in a basket at a vendor stall. The tourists bought the chips and carried them home and the carrying was proximity and the proximity was the product.

The daughter in Dharavi had touched the wall and not taken a piece. The touching was not proximity. The touching was not a product. The touching was a hand on a surface and the surface was the surface that had held and the hand was the hand of someone whose parent the surface had not held and the not-holding was in the count and the count was in the archive and the archive had brought the daughter to the wall and the daughter had touched it and the touching was enough.

She would reply tonight. The reply would be data — the Level 3 junction update, the new path-rich construction. And one line that was not data: *She touched it. That is what the count is for.*

She closed the overlay.

---

Fen knocked at six.

The sound was small. Knuckles on metal. The building's cheapest doors — hollow-core, the vibration traveling through the frame into the masonry beside it.

Nuwa opened the door. Fen was holding a cabbage. Half a head. The outer leaves trimmed.

"Extra," Fen said.

Nuwa looked at the cabbage. The reflex — the arithmetic that converted every offer into a transaction, the Sump reflex, the calculation of cost and repayment and the ledger that kept exchanges balanced because balanced exchanges were the only safe way to receive anything in a system where unbalanced exchanges were debt and debt was how you lost.

The reflex was still there. The reflex would always be there — in the hands that tightened, in the mental ledger that opened, in the body's architecture of self-protection that had been built by an eleven-year-old who learned that people could be processed out of existence in six forms and a credit transfer. The reflex was the Sump in her body the way the chemical staining was the Sump on her hands. Permanent. Unremarked. The residue of a system she no longer lived inside but that lived inside her.

"Thanks," she said.

She took the cabbage. The word arrived the way it had arrived the first time Fen offered spring onions in a corridor twenty-six months ago — not easily, not without the reflex's resistance, but arriving. She had said *thanks* then. She said *thanks* now. The word was the same. The cost of the word had not changed. What had changed was that the cost was in the budget.

Fen nodded. Turned. Her footsteps on the corridor floor — the efficient stride of a woman whose room was organized for the speed of leaving. Nuwa listened until the footsteps reached the stairwell. Then she closed the door.

She put the cabbage on the counter beside the rice cooker and the adhesive tube and the business card she had not thrown away. The card was still there — the media consultant's embossed corporate seal, the offer she had refused, the shape of a refusal that had cost eight hundred creds and that had been the correct refusal and that was now an object on a counter beside a cabbage and an adhesive tube and a rice cooker. The objects did not comment on each other. The objects were the inventory. The inventory was the room.

---

She cooked in the evening. Rice. The cabbage — Fen's, sliced thin, dry-fried in the pan, the edges browning without oil because the oil was for the morning greens and the evening cabbage did not require oil and the not-requiring was not scarcity. It was cooking. It was the choice of a woman who knew the difference between browning cabbage with oil and browning cabbage without oil and who chose the without because the without produced a different edge and the different edge was hers.

She seasoned it. Salt from the small jar. Two creds. The extravagance that had been extravagance in the Sump and that was extravagance here and that she maintained because the maintaining was the domain where she chose and the choosing was the thing the system had never been designed to track because the system tracked populations and populations did not season their cabbage.

She ate at the table. The folding surface with the cardboard shim. The composition books beside the bowl. Blue on the bottom. Green on top.

She did not open them. She had opened them last week — turned the pages, read the names, the handwriting, the implant-maintenance ink on the education-grade paper. The names were there. Mrs. Huang. Yuto. Lin Wei. Her mother. The 4,211. The forty-seven. The cities. The archive references. The count. The count was complete. The count was in the archive. The archive was in fourteen cities. The cities maintained the count. She maintained the notebooks.

The notebooks were warm from the room's air. The blue cover's edge was softening — two months of steam from the rice and the cooking and the condensation from the ventilation shaft. The aging was habitation. The aging was the room becoming hers not by ownership but by use, the way the table had become hers by use, the way the masonry wall had become hers by the hand's repeated touching, the way the corridor had become hers by the twenty-six months of walking it.

She washed the bowl. One bowl. One pair of chopsticks. One mug. The count had not changed. Everything she owned could still be packed in four minutes — and two composition books, blue and green, that she would carry differently.

---

She sat on the mattress. The hum through the masonry walls. Steady. The frequency that meant the system continued.

The overlay was dim. The frozen notification at the edge of her vision — the permanent display, the dead credential, the first and last thing she saw every day.

*Lin, Nuwa. Credential tier: Basic. Evacuation priority: None assigned.*

She had carried this for twenty-six months. The system's inventory of her. The line of grey text that located her in the taxonomy of a city that had consumed its lowest district and archived the records and continued. The notification did not update. The notification would not update. The system that had generated it was gone and the notification was the residue and the residue was permanent.

The notification was a record. The record said: the system knew her name. The system classified her. The system assigned her an evacuation priority of none and the assignment was the system's vocabulary for the expendability the system required and the expendability was the architecture and the architecture had killed everyone she knew and the architecture had not killed her because masonry did not conduct the absorption front and she had walked toward masonry because masonry did not make her implant itch.

The system's count of her: one line. One classification. One priority.

Her count: 4,211 names in an archive. Forty-seven from the Broadcast. Junction coordinates from a dead man's chip. Cities. Categories. Open access. Permanent.

The system counted populations. She counted people. The system produced aggregate loss estimates. She produced names. The system's final report said *no names are listed.* Her archive said the names.

The green notifications were routine now. Three, four per day. The network running at the frequency of a system that held without requiring her to hold it. She was not the hub. She had never been the hub. She was a woman who had walked corridors and survived the walking and carried the walking in the body's memory of which walls mattered and who had traded the memory for topology payments and composition books and a count that was now in an architecture larger than the cracked implant behind her left ear and larger than the room and larger than the margin and the architecture held the names and the names were the names.

She was not invisible anymore. She was not a network node anymore. She was a woman in a room with two composition books and a folding table and a cabbage and a cracked implant and a frozen notification that would never update and green notifications that would continue and a scar on her inner left wrist from a pact she had not kept and chemical staining on her fingertips from a plant that no longer existed and a chip in her implant port from a brother whose thirteen years of maintenance logs had become the foundation of an archive the system could not produce.

She was twenty-seven. The pact had said twenty-five. She had not gotten out. She had gotten to a room where the walls were masonry and the table was used and the food was cooked and the count was complete and the count was in the world and the world held it.

The room was quiet. The hum through the walls. The ventilation shaft's light gone to dark. The composition books on the table. The empty bowl in the rack. The adhesive on the shelf. The mechanical latch on the door.

What remained was the room. The table. The work that continued — topology, junction data, the corridor-level measurements that kept people alive in the margins the system produced. The work was the sentence after the period. The count was the period.

What remained was what she counted. What remained was enough.
