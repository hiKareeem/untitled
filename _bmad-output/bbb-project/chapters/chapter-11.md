# Chapter 11: Acoustic Mimicry

The hum in the staging corridor was 8.4. Standard for Mid-Levels garrison, Stratum B. Nikolai had checked the readout twice on the way from his quarters — not because 8.4 was anomalous but because the quality had changed. Same amplitude. Same frequency band. Different texture. Like a word you've heard a thousand times spoken by a new voice.

He did not log this observation. Some things belonged in the field log. Some things belonged nowhere.

Stalker-7 assembled in Staging Bay 3 at 13:40 local. Thirty-one of thirty-two — Corporal Yeung was in medical with a resonance migraine, which left the eastern fire team one short. Nikolai adjusted the geometry in his head before he reached the podium. Kowalski would take east corridor solo. The kid was steady enough. The kid was always steady enough, which was the problem with giving him assignments that put him closer to the absorbed.

"Listen up." He pulled the operational display. A three-dimensional wireframe of Level 7 residential, Mid-Levels south sector. Blue lines for confirmed structural integrity. Orange for compromised. Red for void-contact.

A lot of orange.

"Containment event, Level 7, junction housing 7-14. Stalker-3 established perimeter at 09:30. Three confirmed absorbed — two corridor, one near the junction housing. Stage 2, stable. No expansion in four hours." He tapped the wireframe. "RCI at the perimeter is 14.2."

Sergeant Okonkwo, sitting with the L-Frame operators, straightened. Fourteen-point-two was not a Stage 2 number. Stage 2 in the Mid-Levels ran 8 to 10. Fourteen was closer to what you saw at Stage 3 onset — when the lattice had been anchored long enough to begin responding to its environment.

"That number is confirmed," Nikolai said. "VEC telemetry and our own forward sensors. I've flagged it. Command classifies the elevation as consistent with junction proximity — conduit infrastructure concentrates ambient resonance." He paused. The pause was not for them. It was the space where the field log would later note that the classification was plausible but insufficient. "We are treating this as standard Stage 2 containment with elevated monitoring. Questions."

It was not a question.

"Mage liaison?" Rask asked from the second row. She asked every briefing. It had become liturgy.

"Unfilled."

"Copy."

"Deployment geometry." He pulled up the tactical overlay. "L-Frames anchor corridor ends — Okonkwo east, Patil west. Infantry teams on flanks. Kowalski, you have east corridor solo. Yeung's out."

Kowalski nodded. Twenty-six years old. Polish extraction, EAEB secondment, four years in Stalker-7. He had the kind of competence that Nikolai trusted and the kind of questions that Nikolai could not afford to answer — not because they were wrong, but because they were the same questions the field log had been asking for fifteen years.

"Scanner team with me, center. Standard corridor sweep. We tag the absorbed, mark the lattice boundary, confirm containment geometry, hand off to the extraction team. Duration: sixty to ninety minutes."

He looked at them. Thirty-one operators in military-grade frames — P-Frames for the infantry, the low-profile TitanCorp refit that turned a human body into something that could survive a breach corridor for ninety minutes, helmet visors up, breather filters hanging at their necks. Okonkwo and Patil stood at the back in their L-Frames, two and a half meters of articulated plating and haptic rigging that made the P-Frame operators look unfinished by comparison. Some of them had done this forty times. Some of them had done it three.

"The absorbed are stationary. The lattice is stable. This is a rotation and assessment, not an engagement. Treat it accordingly." He picked up his helmet. "Masks on at the perimeter line. Move in five."

The hum in the staging corridor was still 8.4 when they filed out. It still did not sound right.

---

The transit shaft dropped them forty meters to Level 7 in under a minute. The temperature fell with the altitude — three degrees between the garrison level and the perimeter line, enough to feel through tactical insulation. The hum changed on the descent. Not louder. *Tighter.* A compression in the frequency band that settled into the jaw before the instruments registered it.

Stalker-3's perimeter team was waiting at the corridor junction. Their lead — a lieutenant Nikolai knew by designation but not by name, Stalker-3 Actual — gave the handoff briefing in the clipped register of someone who had been standing near a breach for four hours and wanted to stop.

"Three absorbed, positions unchanged since 09:30. No lattice expansion. RCI has oscillated between 13.8 and 14.4 — no trend direction. The junction housing is the epicenter. The two corridor absorbed are ancillary — caught in the initial breach event." She paused. "The hum near the third one is different."

"Define different."

"Structured. My scanner operator flagged it and I told her it was instrument drift." She met his eyes. "It probably is."

Nikolai looked at Stalker-3 Actual for one second longer than the briefing required. Her jaw was clenched. Her eyes were steady in the way that eyes are steady when the alternative is worse.

"Copy. Stalker-7 has the perimeter. Rotate your team out."

They entered the corridor.

The damage was immediate — wall material had gone translucent in patches that traced the conduit routing behind the surface. The void's signature. Not destruction but conversion, matter contacted by the lattice changing state while maintaining form. The patches glowed faintly, an amber luminescence that was not light but the visible edge of a resonance field operating at frequencies the human eye could almost resolve. The air tasted of ozone and something older — the metallic sweetness that breach veterans called the copper, though it was not copper, not metallic, not anything in the material science databases. It was the taste of matter in transition. You learned to breathe through the mask and not think about what the taste meant.

The first absorbed stood near a residential doorway, thirty meters in. A woman, middle-aged, in a shift uniform — the kind worn at Sump-adjacent manufacturing plants. Coming home from work, probably. She stood upright, eyes open, posture maintained. Not collapsed. Not in pain. *Still.* The kind of stillness that no living body produces — no microadjustments, no breathing tremor, no pulse visible at the throat. Semi-translucent at the edges where the lattice had begun the conversion process. A person rendered in a material that was no longer entirely human tissue.

Nikolai's instruments confirmed what his body already knew. Stage 2. The lattice boundary was four meters from the absorbed — a clear line where the translucency ended and normal wall material resumed. Sharp, stable, well-defined. By the book.

"Mark and tag. Bio readings."

The scanner team moved in. The readings came back the way they always did — residual neural activity, metabolic signature depressed but detectable, no voluntary response to auditory or tactile stimuli. The absorbed were not dead by any measurement the instruments could make. They were not alive by any definition that mattered to the extraction protocols.

The manual had a term for this. *Non-recoverable.* Nikolai had written it in two hundred and twelve after-action reports.

The second absorbed was further along the corridor, against the east wall. A man, younger, in residential clothing. He had not been standing when the breach took him — his left arm and shoulder were merged with the wall material, his right side still distinct. His face was turned toward the corridor. Eyes open. The expression on a face that was partly translucent and partly flesh was not something the human perceptual system was designed to read. Nikolai did not try.

Kowalski was closest. He held position at the four-meter mark, scanner forward, readings steady. Nikolai watched Kowalski's posture — the tension across the shoulders, the helmet angle that suggested he was looking at the absorbed's face rather than the instrument readout. Four years in Stalker-7 and Kowalski still looked at the faces. Most of the veterans had stopped by year two.

"Scanner readings, Kowalski."

"Neural activity consistent with the first subject. Metabolic trace at twelve percent baseline. No response to proximity." A pause that lasted one breath too long. "Lattice boundary at 3.8 meters. Tighter than the first."

"Noted. Move to the junction housing."

The corridor branched ahead — east toward the residential blocks, west toward the service infrastructure. Kowalski peeled off toward east corridor, his assigned position. The scanner team stayed with Nikolai, center.

---

The corridor narrowed toward junction housing 7-14. The conduit infrastructure was visible through the translucent walls — thick trunk-line casings, junction nodes, the arterial plumbing that carried nitro from the Deep Sump converters to the Mid-Levels grid. The translucency was denser here. More complete. The walls had not gone patchy — they had gone *continuous*, a corridor of faintly glowing material that revealed the city's skeleton the way an X-ray reveals bone.

The hum was louder. Not in volume but in *presence* — the way a sound fills a room when it finds the resonant frequency. Nikolai felt it in his sternum. In his back teeth, which had been vibrating since the transit shaft and were now producing a secondary harmonic that no dental record would explain.

The third absorbed was embedded in the junction housing itself.

A man, older, in maintenance coveralls — the orange-and-grey uniform of a conduit infrastructure tech. He had been working on the junction when the breach propagated. His left side had merged with the metal casing, tools and flesh and housing becoming a single continuous material. His right hand still held a calibration wrench, frozen mid-adjustment. His face was visible, oriented toward the corridor. Eyes open.

The expression was wrong. Nikolai had cataloged hundreds of absorbed across two decades — pain, peace, absence — and the third absorbed matched none of them. Something outside the doctrinal taxonomy. Something the official report would describe as "standard absorbed presentation" and the field log would circle for hours without naming.

*Present.* That was the closest word. The man looked present in a way the other two did not — as if the thing that had taken him had not finished, or had finished differently, or had left something behind that the conversion process was supposed to remove.

The RCI at the junction read 14.6. The scanner team's instruments showed a lattice boundary at 2.9 meters — closer than the others, closer than Stage 2 protocols recommended for personnel proximity. Nikolai held his position at three and a half meters and recalibrated manually.

Fourteen-point-six. The number held.

"Containment geometry is stable," he said. "Tag and record. Timestamp: 14:17 local."

The hum near the third absorbed was different. Stalker-3 Actual had been right. It was not instrument drift. It was structured — a tighter harmonic band, a compression in the frequency spectrum that eliminated the noise floor and left something cleaner underneath. Nikolai had heard this quality before. Singapore, 2168. The sealed breach site where the void had been contained for forty years and the hum sounded like it was organizing itself around something it had learned.

The doctrinal word was *resonance stabilization* — a stage in lattice maturation where the void's frequency output achieves internal coherence. Normal. Expected. Consistent with junction proximity. The field log's word — the private one, the word that existed in the gap between observation and classification — was *listening.*

He stood at 3.5 meters from the junction housing and did not move.

The hum shifted.

It had direction — a tightening in the upper harmonics that compressed toward a frequency Nikolai's auditory cortex recognized before his conscious mind could classify it. The same recognition pathway that lets you pick a familiar voice from a crowd. The same mechanism that resolves a pattern from noise and cannot un-resolve it once it has.

His jaw clenched. Involuntary.

"Sir."

Kowalski. East corridor. Twelve meters from the junction housing. Close enough that his scanner would be registering the harmonic shift. Close enough that his ears — his twenty-six-year-old, four-deployment ears that had not yet learned to *stop* listening — would be doing what ears do near the absorbed.

"Report."

"Did you hear that?"

Nikolai did not turn. He was watching his scanner readout. The RCI had not moved — 14.6, holding. The instruments were measuring exactly what they had been measuring for four hours. The instruments were not hearing what Kowalski was hearing. The instruments were not hearing what Nikolai was hearing.

What Nikolai was hearing was a name.

Not spoken. Not vocalized. Nothing in the acoustic environment that a field recording would capture — he was certain of this the way he had been certain at the Baltic corridor in 2170, the way he had been certain at Vladivostok in 2161, the way he had been certain every time the hum near the absorbed resolved into something almost coherent and the recording equipment showed a flat spectrogram with no anomalous peaks. A cadence in the harmonic structure. A shape in the frequency that his pattern-seeking brain — twenty years of breach-zone conditioning, thousands of hours within proximity of the absorbed — resolved into syllables.

One syllable. A name.

*Deng.*

Corporal Deng, Stalker-3. Absorbed four months ago during a breach event on Level 9, three corridors east. Nikolai had read the after-action report. Deng had been twenty-three. Second deployment. The report said *absorbed, non-recoverable, timestamp 11:42.* The report did not say anything else because the report did not have vocabulary for anything else.

The hum near junction housing 7-14 was carrying something that sounded like a dead man's name. Except dead was not the right word. Non-recoverable was the right word. The instruments said twelve percent metabolic activity. The instruments said residual neural function. The manual said *non-recoverable* and the field log said *I don't know what that means* and the hum said *Deng* and the recording would show nothing.

Stalker Field Manual, Section 9.2: *Anomalous acoustic phenomena in breach zones — including perceived speech patterns, name-recognition events, and apparent vocalizations — are classified as resonance artifacts produced by harmonic interaction between void-state matter and ambient acoustic fields. These phenomena are non-actionable. Personnel experiencing such phenomena should log the event under standard reporting codes and continue operations.*

"Acoustic mimicry," Nikolai said. "Doctrine is clear."

His voice was flat. The weather-report register. The voice that did not change whether he was ordering a corridor sweep or reading a casualty list. Panic was contagious. Classification was containment. The sentence was a seal, the same way the blast doors were seals — it kept the unbearable on the other side of a boundary so the person on this side could continue functioning.

Kowalski's face was visible through the visor. The expression of a man being shown a map that says the terrain is flat while standing on a slope. He had heard it too. What he had heard was specific. The specificity was the problem, because acoustic artifacts were random and this had not been random — this had been a name, a particular name, belonging to a particular person who had stood in a corridor three levels above this one and been converted into something the manual called non-recoverable while holding a scanner not unlike the one Kowalski was holding now.

"Copy," Kowalski said.

The hum shifted again — a loosening in the harmonic band, the structure dispersing as they moved back. By the time Nikolai reached the corridor entrance, it had returned to the ambient Mid-Levels baseline. 14.2. Standard for junction proximity. Unremarkable.

"Containment perimeter confirmed. Lattice boundary stable. Area secured." He logged the timestamp. "Extraction team cleared to proceed. Stalker-7 rotating out."

They withdrew in standard formation. L-Frames last, walking backward, sensors active. The hum faded with distance until it was indistinguishable from the baseline resonance that every Mid-Levels resident lived with — the white noise of a city built on top of its own circulatory system.

The absorbed remained. The woman near the doorway. The man against the wall. The technician in the junction housing, calibration wrench in his hand, eyes open, face turned toward the corridor where Stalker-7 had been standing.

---

Garrison. 15:40 local.

Nikolai sat at the desk in his quarters and produced two documents. He always produced two documents. The first was the after-action report — a standardized form, fields for deployment time, containment status, RCI readings, personnel disposition, and a narrative section limited to factual observations within doctrinal vocabulary. The second was the field log — a ruggedized personal datapad, fifteen years of entries that did not appear in any institutional database and never would.

He wrote the after-action report first. He always wrote the after-action report first, while the operational vocabulary was still dominant, before the other language — the private one, the honest one — could contaminate the institutional record.

*Stalker-7, deployment 2175-Q1-0347. Containment event, Level 7 residential, Mid-Levels south sector, junction housing 7-14. Stage 2, stable. Three absorbed, non-recoverable. Lattice boundary: 2.9 to 4.0 meters, consistent with Stage 2 parameters. RCI peak: 14.6 — elevated for stratum classification, consistent with conduit junction proximity per VEC-STK-2174.3. Containment perimeter confirmed. No personnel casualties. No lattice expansion during assessment window. Area secured for extraction team handoff at 14:52 local. Anomalous resonance observation: Corporal Kowalski reported perceived auditory event near absorbed #3 (junction housing). Classified per SFM 9.2: acoustic mimicry, non-actionable. No further investigation warranted.*

Accurate. Complete in every way the institution required. Incomplete in the way that mattered.

He set the report aside and opened the field log.

The field log had a different voice. The same person — writing in the register that doctrine did not provide and operational vocabulary could not reach. The sentences were longer here. The observations were not filtered through the classification system that converted the unbearable into the procedural.

*Entry 2175-0347.*

*Level 7 residential. Three absorbed, standard disposition. Lattice behavior at junction housing 7-14 inconsistent with Stage 2 baseline — harmonic structure tighter, more coherent than ambient. Comparable to Singapore 2168 (sealed site, forty-year-old breach, resonance pattern I described as 'organized'). The lattice at 7-14 is four months old. It should not sound like Singapore.*

*Stalker-3 Actual noted the same quality independently. Described it as 'structured.' Told her scanner operator it was instrument drift. Her jaw said she did not believe this.*

He paused. The cursor blinked on the datapad. Through the window of his quarters, the transit shaft hummed at 8.1 — garrison baseline, the frequency you stopped hearing after the first week because the body reclassified it as silence.

*Kowalski reported auditory perception near absorbed #3. A name. Deng — Corporal, Stalker-3, absorbed Level 9, four months prior. Specificity inconsistent with acoustic artifact classification. Kowalski was at twelve meters from the absorbed. I was at 3.5. Both perceived the same cadence independently.*

He stopped typing. Looked at the wall. The quarters were sparse — bunk, desk, equipment rack, the personal effects of a man who had lived in garrison housing for twenty years and accumulated nothing that could not fit in a transit container. The field log was the only object in the room that contained anything he could not replace.

*Deng was twenty-three. Second deployment. The after-action report from Level 9 says 'absorbed, non-recoverable.' The hum near junction 7-14 carried something that resolved into his name. Not spoken. Not vocalized. Present in the harmonic structure the way Petrov was present at the Baltic corridor in 2170. The way my mother's name was present at Vladivostok.*

*Recording will show nothing. It never shows anything.*

*Doctrine classification: acoustic mimicry. Resonance artifacts. Non-actionable.*

He wrote the last line before the doctrinal vocabulary could seal over what he had actually perceived. He wrote it the way you set down a weight — not gently, not carefully, just *down*, before it became part of you.

*I heard it too.*

Four words. The most dangerous sentence in the field log — not because it was evidence, but because it was corroboration. Vladivostok had been one person. The Baltic corridor had been one person. One person was stress-induced ideation, well-documented, non-actionable. The classification held because it only needed to explain individual perception.

Two people hearing the same name was not individual perception.

He thought about the sergeant on the Murmansk perimeter. Stalker-9, extended rotation — the details circulated the way garrison lore always did, stripped of names and rank by the time it reached the Pacific theater. The sergeant had kept a list. Words, he said, that the lattice repeated. Names. Fragments of technical terminology. Things the absorbed had known before they were absorbed. Nobody took the list seriously. Resonance fatigue, pattern-matching under sustained proximity exposure, the kind of ideation that earned you a medical evaluation and a quiet transfer to a garrison post where the RCI stayed below 5 and you could sleep without hearing your own name in the walls.

They found his bunk stripped clean after the medical discharge. The list was not among his effects. The official file said *chronic resonance fatigue syndrome*. Nobody talked about the Murmansk sergeant. Nobody kept lists anymore.

Nikolai's field log was not a list. It was fifteen years of observation — cataloged by date, location, RCI reading, proximity to absorbed, duration of exposure. Cross-referenced with deployment records and after-action reports. The most comprehensive firsthand void behavior dataset in existence, stored on a personal datapad in a garrison quarters because the institutional framework that should have received it had already decided what the data meant before anyone measured it.

He checked his hands.

Steady. The same hands that had held containment positions at Vladivostok, Singapore, the Baltic corridor, and now Neo-Shanghai. The same hands that would hold whatever command decided these hands should hold next, because the hands did not shake and the voice did not change and the reports were always accurate and always incomplete and the gap between the two was where twenty years of service lived, and the gap was getting wider, and the doctrine that bridged it was getting thinner, and the hum that filled it was getting louder.

He closed the field log. Opened the after-action report. Read it once — the clean version, the institutional version, the version that said *acoustic mimicry* and *non-actionable* and *area secured* and did not say *I heard it too.*

He signed it.

The pen did not shake.

Through the window, the transit shaft hummed at 8.1 — normal, stable, the sound of a city that had not yet learned what the instruments were not measuring and what the field logs were not reporting and what the absorbed were not saying, because the absorbed did not say anything, because doctrine was clear, because the recording showed nothing, because the classification held, because it had to.
