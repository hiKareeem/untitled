## Draft v1

*On the Statistical Correlation Between Nitro Throughput Volume and Void Coherence Index Readings in Urban Conduit Networks* — Dr. Maren Solberg, VEC Internal Review, 2170 (Classified)

> "See Appendix D."

---

The pod registered it before Sofia did.

A pulse in the haptic suit — not the familiar 0.7 Hz signal she'd been tracking for months, but something beneath it, something that used the 0.7 as a carrier wave and rode the infrastructure with a directionality her instruments had never recorded. The holographic field around her shifted from the standard blue-white of atmospheric monitoring to amber. Then deeper. The color of crude R0 viewed through first-generation conduit glass.

She was on her feet before the visual readout caught up. Frequency band theta-nine — the one Liang had called instrument drift, the one she'd recalibrated sixty-two sensors to verify, the one she'd written two papers about that nobody would publish — was no longer a signal. It was a floor. The baseline had stepped up and was not coming back down.

"Computer. Global coherence index. All strata. Real-time."

The holographic sphere expanded. Three meters across — the central display she'd always dismissed as decorative, too large for useful analysis, a showpiece for visiting administrators who wanted to feel briefed without being informed. Now it was the only display in the lab that could hold what she was seeing. Neo-Shanghai rendered in three dimensions: Deep Sump at the bottom, amber conduit lines threading upward through the Sump, branching into the Mid-Levels, thinning toward the Spires. The conduit network as cardiovascular system. She'd seen it a thousand times.

She had never seen it pulse.

The amber lines were brightening in sequence — not randomly, not in the stochastic pattern of standard throughput fluctuation, but in waves that propagated outward from a locus in the Deep Sump's Resonance District. The wave pattern was consistent with her convergence models. The vertical gradient she'd mapped in her held paper — Sump 0.96, Mid-Levels 0.91, Spires 0.87 — was inverting in real time. The Sump lines were no longer amber. They were white.

RCI readings populated the display margins. Numbers she'd spent months correlating with Stalker deployment records, with the atmospheric pulse data Liang had asked her to hold for methodology review, with the throughput calculations she'd stored in her encrypted archive because the institutional pipeline would classify them into silence. The numbers were climbing. Not climbing — stepping. Each stratum's coherence index jumping in discrete increments, the way a system behaves when external forcing overwhelms internal regulation.

Sump: 34.7. 38.2. 41.6.

Mid-Levels: 22.1. 24.8. 27.3.

Spires: 14.1. 14.3. 14.4.

The differential was wrong. Not wrong in the sense of instrument error — she'd killed that hypothesis months ago, recalibrated every sensor in her array, cross-referenced forty-seven years of archived data. Wrong in the sense of *engineered.* The Sump readings were climbing three times faster than the Mid-Levels. The Spires were barely moving. The infrastructure between the strata was not propagating the event uniformly. It was channeling it.

Someone had designed the conduit network to contain the coherence event below a specific altitude.

Her hands went still on the haptic interface.

The coupling events began appearing. Points of light on the holographic display — each one a location where the void's coherence index had exceeded the threshold she'd identified in her emergency paper, the paper VEC had classified as "potential strategic sensitivity" before she could submit it for peer review. The points were appearing in clusters. Not randomly distributed. Following the conduit lines. Following the infrastructure she'd mapped, the same infrastructure whose density she'd correlated with atmospheric anomalies at 0.94.

The clusters were densest in the Resonance District. The oldest infrastructure. The highest conduit density. The neighborhoods where the RCI had been running anomalously high for months and the tolerance band had been widened three times to accommodate it. She had written about the tolerance band in the classified paper. The tolerance band was now a kill radius.

She pulled off the haptic gloves. Left the pod. Crossed the darkened monitoring lab at a pace that was not running but was faster than the lab's geography required.

---

The corridor between the thirty-fifth floor and the forty-seventh was twelve flights of emergency stairs. The elevator system had defaulted to security lockdown — red indicator lights on every call panel, VEC's crisis protocol routing all vertical transit to authorized personnel. Sofia's access badge — Senior Research Fellow, Atmospheric Anomaly Group — carried clearance for the monitoring lab, the immersion pods, the residential level, the cafeteria. Not the administrative stratum. Not today.

She took the stairs two at a time. The stairwell hummed. Not the building's climate system — the conduit infrastructure running through the tower's core, the same trunk-line network that connected VEC HQ to the Deep Sump processing stations three hundred meters below. The hum had been a background constant for nine years. A colleague had told her once that you stopped hearing it after the first month. She never had. The hum was data. It was always data.

Now the data was screaming.

Forty-seventh floor. The corridor was empty — the kind of empty that meant everyone was in the same room. Overhead lights at full brightness, the emergency protocol flooding the floor with clinical white that made the corridor look longer than it was. Liang's office at the far end. The door closed. Through the glass partition beside it, the secured briefing room — Liang, two people she didn't recognize in VEC executive identification lanyards, a holographic display running a feed she couldn't read from this distance.

She walked closer. The display resolved.

**VEC EMERGENCY ALERT — CLASSIFICATION: APEX**

**Confluence confirmed. VPI: 87.3%.**

**AEGIS targeting calculated. Countermand window: 15:00.**

**All monitoring personnel: shelter in place. Do not access external communications.**

VPI. Void Propagation Index. She'd proposed that metric in a paper three years ago. The methodology committee had rejected it as "premature parameterization of a poorly understood phenomenon." Now it was on an Apex-level alert, calibrated to a tenth of a percent, and nobody had told her.

She pressed her badge against the biometric panel beside the briefing room door. The panel glowed red. Did not change.

Through the glass: Liang sitting with his hands flat on the table. The posture she recognized from his office — the day she'd brought him the correlation data, the day he'd asked her to hold publication for methodology review. The posture of a man who understood the numbers and was choosing the institutional response over the scientific one. His face was turned toward the executive display. Away from the glass. He might have known she was there.

He did not look.

Sofia stood in the corridor. The hum in the building's core was louder here — twelve floors closer to the administrative systems, the secured communication infrastructure, the classified networks that carried the data she was not cleared to see. She pressed her palm flat against the glass partition. The vibration traveled through her hand, her wrist, the bones of her forearm. The building was shaking. Not enough to notice without contact. Enough to measure.

She went back down.

---

The monitoring lab was dark except for the displays. The overhead lights had cycled to emergency spectrum — deep amber, the same frequency the conduit network was throwing, as if the building's systems were harmonizing with the event they were recording. Dr. Yun's workstation — station four, throughput modeling — was cycling through standard diagnostics that were no longer measuring anything standard. Farid's station was empty. The chair pushed back at an angle that suggested departure without return.

The immersion pod was where she'd left it. She climbed back in. Sealed the door. The haptic suit was still hanging on its rack — she didn't put it on. Didn't need the tactile resolution. The holographic field rebuilt around her from the projection arrays, the three-dimensional coherence map still rendering, still updating, still recording.

The Confluence was playing out exactly as her models had predicted.

She had run the convergence simulation eleven times over the past months — the atmospheric pulse data overlaid on the vertical gradient, the throughput rates modeled against coherence thresholds, the propagation vectors mapped against infrastructure density. Eleven simulations. Eleven results. The same result. If throughput in the Deep Sump conduit network exceeded a critical threshold, the coherence response would cascade through the infrastructure in a wave pattern that followed the conduit architecture — strongest where density was highest, weakest where it was lowest.

The model had predicted the wave. The model had predicted the gradient. The model had predicted the differential between strata.

What the model had not predicted — what no atmospheric simulation could predict, because the variable was not atmospheric — was that someone would increase the throughput on purpose.

The RCI readings were still climbing. Sump: 52.3. The number had passed the upper bound of her sensor calibration twenty minutes ago. Everything above 40 was extrapolation. The instruments were doing their best with a magnitude they'd never been designed to record.

She watched the coupling events populate the field. Each point of light — a location where the boundary between infrastructure and void had thinned past the threshold. The points were appearing in sequences that followed the conduit lines, the flood valves, the pressure door perimeters, the trunk-line junctions. The pattern was not chaotic. The pattern was architectural.

Each point was a person.

The thought arrived without qualifiers. No hedging. No "the correlation suggests." No "consistent with." Each coupling event was a human being at a specific location being absorbed into the infrastructure at a specific time, and her instruments were recording every one of them with the precision and indifference of equipment that did not know what it was measuring.

Then the spike hit.

Not a gradual intensification. Not a ratcheting escalation. A step-function — the global coherence index jumping to a value the holographic display rendered as overflow, the projection arrays flooding white, every sensor in her sub-strata array reporting maximum simultaneously. The pod shuddered. The holographic field destabilized — the three-dimensional city collapsing into a flat plane of overlapping light as the data volume exceeded rendering capacity. A broadband coherence event. Every frequency at once. Every signal overlapping. Structured. Repeating.

Eleven seconds.

The sound was below hearing. The pod's acoustic insulation filtered the audible spectrum, but the sub-sonic frequencies passed through the walls, through the chair, through the floor, through her skeleton. She felt it in her sternum. In her teeth. A pressure that was not air pressure but *coherence* pressure — the entire conduit network beneath the tower carrying a signal that was too large for any single instrument to resolve.

Then it stopped. The readings dropped — not to baseline, not to anything she recognized, but to a level the instruments could process. The holographic field rebuilt. The city was still there. The conduit lines were still glowing. The coupling events had stopped appearing.

Not because the event had ended. Because the substrate was saturated.

Sofia sat in the pod. Her hands were shaking — a high-frequency tremor in her fingers, her wrists, the muscles of her forearms. The physiological data scrolled at the edge of her display: heart rate elevated, respiration shallow, skin conductance spiked. The instruments reported her body's honest response to what her mind had just witnessed.

Her private terminal chimed.

The sound was wrong for the lab — a notification on the encrypted relay, the channel she'd configured when she'd first contacted Black Babel's network, the system that existed outside VEC's infrastructure because existing inside it meant existing inside the classification pipeline. She hadn't received anything through it in weeks. The last transmission had been an acknowledgment of her atmospheric convergence data — a timestamp and a node identifier. No commentary. No name. The architecture of anonymity.

She opened the channel.

Fragments. Not a coherent document — raw data packets, timestamped and unedited, arriving out of sequence with gaps between them. Text rendered by an implant's thought-dictation protocol, the compressed, article-sparse syntax of neural-to-text translation under cognitive load. The first fragment was mid-sentence:

*—readings from forty-one sites across six sectors. Baseline readings in the Sump proper range between eight and nineteen — within NitroCore's published tolerance band. Deep Sump readings range between twenty-two and thirty-four—*

She read it. Read the next fragment. And the next.

RCI field measurements. Forty-one sites. Six sectors. The correlation with disappearance locations explicit, documented, measured from the ground with a handheld sensor by someone who had walked the corridors she'd only seen as data points on a holographic display. The field measurements matched her atmospheric readings. The correlation was the same. The conclusion was the same. But the evidence was physical — boots on conduit floors, a sensor pressed to trunk-line housings, proximity to the infrastructure she'd studied from thirty-five floors above it.

More fragments arrived. Flood valve designations — FV-1 through FV-7. Photographs: low-resolution, implant-captured, the image quality of recording hardware working at the edge of its operational envelope. Infrastructure maps hand-annotated with throughput calculations, valve positions, pressure door locations. NitroCore procurement records showing installation dates that preceded the current crisis by years. And a protocol designation she had never seen in any VEC document, any classified briefing summary, any institutional communication:

*BLACKWEIR.*

*The protocol predates the current crisis by a minimum of thirty-six months. BLACKWEIR is not a response to void breach escalation. BLACKWEIR is the planned activation of pre-installed infrastructure—*

*Pre-installed.* The flood valves were not emergency equipment. They were design features. The infrastructure channeling the Confluence event through the Sump — the same infrastructure whose differential throughput she'd just watched on her instruments, the engineered gradient that protected the Spires by saturating everything below — had been built to do this. The procurement records showed dates. The dates showed planning. The planning showed intent.

The fragments kept arriving. Thirty-one confirmed disappearances across six sectors. A co-origin pattern — nitro throughput increases and void breach incursions sharing geographic source points. *The infrastructure is the delivery mechanism. The breaches are the product. The disappearances are the cost.* The language was precise, urgent, the syntax degrading in the later packets as the gaps widened and the articles dropped and the cognitive architecture behind the dictation fragmented under a strain Sofia could infer but not observe.

The last fragment was shorter than the others. The syntax broken in a way that was not degradation but interruption:

*The infrastructure was always designed to—*

Nothing after.

The timestamp was forty-one minutes ago. No continuation. No completion. The thought-dictation channel had stopped transmitting — not a clean termination, not a signed-off session. An absence. The kind of absence her instruments had been recording all morning as coupling events on the holographic display. Points of light that had been people.

Sofia sat in the immersion pod. The holographic field displayed the Confluence's aftermath — the coupling events frozen in their final positions, a constellation of points tracing the conduit architecture, densest in the Resonance District, thinning where the infrastructure thinned, absent where the infrastructure was absent. The anonymous source had documented the same pattern from below. The same correlation. The same engineered gradient. The same conclusion.

She switched the terminal to her personal archive. The encrypted partition outside VEC's network — the one she'd started using when Liang had asked her to hold the correlation paper, the one that held every anomalous reading the institutional process had recalibrated into silence. She opened a new entry. Began transferring: the real-time Confluence recordings, every second timestamped and calibrated. The anonymous cache fragments, unedited, gaps preserved. The atmospheric convergence data. The vertical gradient analysis. The correlation paper Liang had never approved.

Everything. The complete dataset. Her instruments and someone else's evidence in the same archive for the first time.

She looked at the holographic display. The conduit lines had dimmed from white to amber to a color the rendering system labeled *post-event residual coherence* — the infrastructure carrying the memory of what had passed through it. The Sump was quiet on the display. Not unpowered — the backup systems were operational, the emergency lighting active, the infrastructure intact. Quiet because there were no more coupling events to render. The substrate was saturated. The infrastructure had done what it was designed to do.

Upstairs, behind the glass on the forty-seventh floor, Liang was sitting in a briefing she could not enter, reading data from instruments she had calibrated, using a metric she had invented, processing an event her models had predicted. The institution had classified the scientist and operationalized the science. The distance between identifying a pattern and weaponizing it was twelve flights of stairs and a biometric lock.

She thought about the anonymous source. The fragments sitting in her archive — field measurements, photographs, procurement records, the unfinished sentence. Someone had walked the Sump with a handheld sensor and documented the engineering from inside the machine while the machine was running. The transmission had stopped forty-one minutes ago and had not resumed. The data had arrived through a network designed to hold evidence that outlived its source.

She did not know the source's name. She did not know whether the source was alive. She knew the data was clean. Defensible. Corroborated by her own instruments. The field measurements matched the atmospheric readings at every point of overlap. The correlation held from both ends — from the thirty-fifth floor looking down and from the Sump looking up. The complete picture.

The tremor in her hands had stopped. Her breathing was even. The pod's sensors reported physiological baseline — heart rate, respiration, skin conductance all within normal parameters. The instruments said she was calm.

She saved the archive entry. The encryption engaged. The data was outside VEC's network, outside the classification pipeline, outside the institutional architecture that had absorbed forty-seven years of inconvenient findings into folders nobody opened.

The data was complete.

The data had prevented nothing.
