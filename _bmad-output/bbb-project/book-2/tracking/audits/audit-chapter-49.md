## 2. Character Audit

### ✓ PASS — Nikolai voice consistency

**Contractions:** ZERO in narration and dialogue. Verified throughout.
- "did not," "was not," "could not," "had not," "do not," "I filed," "I did not," "I reported"

**Operational register:** Maintained. Testimony delivered in after-action report cadence. "The after-action report of October 21st accurately describes observed phenomena" — the same voice he used in briefings, the same voice he used when the Sump was being flooded.

**Jaw:** Transferred to Dekker (intentional inversion). "Dekker's jaw moved. One millimeter." Across B2 Ch 5, 15, 25, 41, the jaw clench has been Nikolai's — the body registering what the register suppressed. Here, in the terminal chapter, the jaw recognition transfers to the man convicting him. Dekker has the same body. Dekker knows. Dekker convicts. Nikolai's jaw does not clench because Nikolai is no longer suppressing — he said the accurate thing and the body has no discrepancy to register. The suppression has ended. The jaw's absence from Nikolai is the arc's completion.

**Hands-check ritual:** Present. "He checked his hands. Left index finger. Four seconds. / Still." Placed during the recess. The reflection tracks the full arc explicitly: "before Vladivostok, before the Baltic, before Accra, before Lagos, before the commission and the honest report and the second honest report." The steadiness reframed: "The steadiness was not composure. The steadiness was the instrument. The instrument continued." Terminal framing: "The hands would be still tomorrow and the day after." The ritual outlasts the institution.

**Hum discrepancy:** Present throughout. Tribunal interior: 3.8 (body) / 4.0 (instruments). Lagos exterior: 4.2 (body) / 4.4 (instruments). The interior readings are lower than Ch 41's perimeter baseline (4.2/4.4) — the judicial facility is more filtered, further from the conduit cluster. The exterior readings match Ch 41 and Ch 25's established Lagos baseline. The gap's persistent presence — even in the tribunal room, even in an institutional facility designed to filter — carries the chapter's argument: the gap between institutional measurement and bodily reality exists everywhere.

**"The instrument did not differentiate":** Three deployments (lines 47, 103, 131). One more than Ch 41's two. Terminal chapter justification: the refrain accumulates toward the instrument being decommissioned. First use: instrument doesn't differentiate between commendation and tribunal (institutional events). Second: instrument doesn't differentiate between the officer who lied and the officer who told the truth (moral states). Third: instrument doesn't differentiate while signing — the same hand that signed thirty-eight wrong forms and two right ones (the full arc). The escalation produces the decommission: "The instrument had been decommissioned. The markers were returned." The refrain is retired by the chapter's end. Author approved three deployments.

**Field log:** Referenced but not opened. "The field log was in his kit. Fifteen years." The field log is not written in during this chapter — Ch 41's closing entry was "This is the last entry written by a serving officer." He is no longer a serving officer. He does not write. The field log exists as an object in his possession — the Chekhov's gun. The chapter's closing paragraphs frame it: "a field log that contained fifteen years of the truth and no destination and no institution to deliver the truth to and no form to write it on." The field log's future is Book 3's question.

**Window:** Absent. Intentional. The window ritual (standing at a window in quarters, Lagos at night) appeared in Ch 25 and Ch 41. Here, Nikolai is not in quarters. He has no quarters. The institutional infrastructure that provided the window is gone. The closing is him standing on a street — no window between him and the city. The hum replaces the window. He listens directly, not through glass. The ritual's absence is the arc's structural marker: the institutional frame (quarters, window, hum filtered through building) has been removed. He is outside.

**Dual-register processing:** Present implicitly. Nikolai reads the charges in two registers: the institutional vocabulary and its actual meaning ("Unauthorized disclosure meant he had written what happened. Insubordination meant he had written it again."). The translation is not two-column anymore — it's embedded, immediate. The registers have collapsed toward convergence (first tracked in Ch 25).

## 6. Flags & Notes

### Structural notes:

**Four-act structure:** Tribunal convening/charges → Prosecution/defense → Recess/verdict/stripping → Exit/field log/close. Consistent with preceding Nikolai chapters' pattern (deployment → event → report → reflection/close) adapted to institutional-judicial setting.

**Testimony as in-chapter document:** Nikolai's defense statement is presented as extended quoted speech — the operational register applied to the truth, paralleling Ch 41's VS-7 free-text override displayed as in-chapter text. The reader hears Nikolai say in a tribunal room what he wrote on the form. The voice does not change.

**Closing rhythm:** The final paragraphs build through accumulating clauses ("with no rank and no commission and no orders and a field log...and no destination and no institution...and no form") then drop to the two shortest possible lines: "The hum was 4.2. / He listened." The rhythm mirrors Ch 41's close ("He stood at the window until the hum was all there was") — both end on the hum, both end on Nikolai alone with the frequency. Ch 41: listening through glass. Ch 49: listening directly. No window. No institution. Just the hum.

**Epigraph effectiveness:** "Accurate: conforming to fact. The form does not require accuracy. The form requires conformity. I confused the two for twenty years." Three sentences. Field-log register. Ties directly to the chapter title and the defense statement. Resolves on reread: the field log entry describes what took twenty years to understand and one tribunal to demonstrate.
