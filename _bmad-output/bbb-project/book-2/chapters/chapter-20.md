## Draft v1

The Feed had moved on.

Sofia sat at the desk — the same desk, the same apartment, the field kit pulsing green at the edge of the surface where Mirelle's printouts had been stacked ten days ago. The printouts were still there. She had not moved them. The correlation data occupied the same footprint it had occupied when she'd pressed confirm and the document had entered the world. The world had received it. The world had processed it. The world was now processing other things.

The terminal displayed Black Babel's propagation tracker — a monitoring overlay the network ran for high-priority distributions, measuring node-to-node spread, download velocity, secondary redistribution, media derivative citations. The tracker was not designed for her; it was designed for the network's operational assessment of whether a release had achieved critical propagation. Her release had. The metrics confirmed it. The document had reached 340,000 endpoints in the first hour, 2.1 million in the first day, derivative coverage on forty-seven of fifty global news feeds within four hours. By every metric Black Babel used to define a successful distribution, the Reyes publication was the most successful distribution in the network's history.

The propagation curve was a spike followed by a plateau followed by a decline. The decline had begun on day three.

She switched to the public Feed.

"—findings remain unverified by any institutional body, and critics have raised significant questions about the methodology—"

A woman in a studio. Professional lighting. The NitroCore logo was not displayed but the studio's aesthetic — clean angles, amber accent lighting, the particular shade of institutional warmth that NitroCore's media subsidiaries used across all platforms — was recognizable. Sofia had spent nine years inside an institution that shared the same corporate parent. She knew the palette.

She switched channels.

"—Dr. Reyes, a suspended VEC researcher, published through Black Babel, a known data-brokerage network with no institutional oversight or peer-review process. The question isn't whether the data is interesting — it's whether the methodology meets the standard we'd expect from—"

She switched again.

"—what we're looking at is a former employee with an axe to grind and a distribution channel that will publish anything for the right price. Black Babel has no editorial standards. No fact-checking. No—"

Again.

"—NitroCore has released a statement. Let me read the relevant portion: 'We take these findings seriously and have commissioned an independent review panel to assess the claims made in the Reyes publication. NitroCore remains committed to transparency and to the rigorous scientific standards that have guided our operations for over a century.' The independent review panel, we should note, includes Dr. Petra Lindqvist, formerly of VEC's own Classification Directorate, and Dr. Harlan Goss, who served as a senior consultant to NitroCore's atmospheric monitoring division for—"

Sofia paused. The anchor — a man, mid-forties, the practiced neutrality of someone who had been trained to present all positions with equal weight regardless of whether they warranted it — continued reading the review panel's composition. Six names. She recognized four. Two had co-signed classification orders on her own research. One had written the methodology framework that VEC used to justify holding her correlation paper for review. The fourth had published a paper in 2171 arguing that atmospheric resonance data was insufficiently calibrated for causal inference — a paper funded by a NitroCore research grant, a detail that appeared in the funding disclosure section that nobody read.

The independent review was not independent. This was not a revelation. It was a confirmation. She had predicted it on the night she'd pressed confirm — had sat at the window with cold coffee and told nobody that the methodology objections would arrive first, then the legal filings, then the political positioning. The objections had arrived at hour thirty-six. The legal filings at hour fifty-two. NitroCore's statement at hour sixty-eight. The timeline was faster than institutional suppression and slower than genuine concern. It was the precise speed of an organization calibrating its response to the interval between "the public is angry" and "the public is distracted."

She closed the Feed.

---

The terminal chimed. An automated notification — VEC's institutional messaging system, the same pipeline that had delivered her reassignment to remote monitoring, the same clean typography that had accompanied every directive, every classification order, every methodology review request that had held her work in institutional suspension for three years before she'd encrypted it and routed it through a channel the institution couldn't reach.

*Employment Status Update: Dr. Sofia Reyes, Senior Research Fellow, Atmospheric Anomaly Group. Status: TERMINATED. Effective: Immediately. Reason: Violation of Employment Terms §12.4 (Unauthorized Disclosure of Classified Research), §14.2 (Use of Institutional Data Without Authorization), §18.1 (Breach of Non-Distribution Agreement). Severance: Per institutional policy, housing access will be maintained for 30 days following termination. Personal effects may be retrieved from Monitoring Station 9 during scheduled access hours.*

She read it once. The font was the same font as the strategic sensitivity directive that had buried her emergency paper. The same font as Liang's reassignment memo. The same font as the Classification Directorate's countersignature on Solberg's suppression order five years before Sofia had found the same signal and been processed by the same machine. The institution's typography was consistent. The institution's typography would survive the institution.

Thirty days of housing. The apartment she was sitting in — the sixty-first floor, the conduit hum through the floor, the window facing the Anchor Zone — was hers for thirty more days. Then it was VEC's again. The institution that had terminated her employment would reclaim the space where she had assembled the evidence that the institution existed to suppress. The architecture processed her the way it processed everything: completely, administratively, without altering shape.

She did not re-read the notice. She set the terminal to the Black Babel overlay and checked the propagation tracker. The curve was still declining. The document was still in the world. The institution had terminated her. The document had not been terminated. These were different systems operating at different speeds, and the document was faster than the institution in the same way that the institution was faster than the truth: not by being right, but by being distributed.

---

The field kit pulsed green on the desk. She picked it up — the ruggedized sensor package she'd carried since Liang had asked her to hold the correlation paper, the instrument that recorded data outside VEC's pipeline, the parallel archive that had outlasted her institutional access and would outlast her institutional housing. The indicator light was steady. The kit was recording the hum through the building's conduit lines at the same frequency it had recorded for nine years — 0.7 Hz, the structured pulse in the sub-strata band, the signal that had started everything.

She set it down. Opened the Feed again.

The coverage had stratified. Ten days in, the document occupied three distinct positions in the media architecture. Position one: the original breaking story, now archived, available but not featured, the headlines from day one preserved in the Feed's historical cache like insects in amber. Position two: the institutional response layer — NitroCore's statement, VEC's no-comment, the independent review's announced timeline, the legal filings. Position three: the analysis, the debate, the methodology concerns, the "both sides" framing that treated a 0.94 correlation coefficient and a corporate PR statement as equivalent claims requiring equal airtime.

She cycled through the feeds. Each one had settled into its register.

"—the broader question, of course, is whether this kind of publication — outside institutional channels, without peer review — actually advances scientific understanding or merely creates confusion in an already complex—"

Switch.

"—Kindling has issued what they're calling an annotated edition. The full Reyes publication with margin notes. Let me quote: 'You now know what the system runs on. Act accordingly.' That's the entirety of the annotation on page one. Security analysts are calling this the most widely distributed piece of dissident literature since—"

Switch.

"—the Church of the Blessed Nitro released a formal encyclical this morning. Arch-Prelate Malachi of the European See authored what's being called 'The Voice Is Not Wounded' — a theological response to the Reyes publication asserting that the claims, quote, 'remain unverified by any authority recognized under the Five Harmonies and do not alter the Church's understanding of the Voice as divine provision.' The encyclical has drawn immediate criticism from the Cathedral movement in Neo-Shanghai, whose leader, the former Prelate known as Nephthys—"

Sofia paused. She did not know Nephthys. She did not follow the Church. The theological dimension of the nitro crisis existed in a register she had never occupied — the liturgical vocabulary, the sacramental framework, the institutional architecture of faith built on the same infrastructure she had spent three years measuring. But the anchor was describing a fracture. The Church's official position — denial dressed as theological clarity — and a counter-movement that was citing the Reyes publication as evidence that the Voice was not provision but pain. Two institutional positions generated from the same data, arriving at opposite conclusions, both using her numbers.

"—the Threshold faction within the Cathedral movement has issued a direct response contradicting the encyclical, citing atmospheric data from the Reyes publication to argue that the Voice, as described in Church doctrine, is consistent with a consciousness signal generated by human suffering. This is the first time an internal Church faction has publicly used scientific evidence to challenge an official theological position—"

She switched the Feed off. The terminal went dark.

The data was in the world. The world was doing what the world does with data: sorting it into positions, aligning it with existing frameworks, metabolizing it into the vocabulary of whatever institution needed it to say whatever the institution needed said. NitroCore used her data to announce a review. The Church used her data to affirm its doctrine. The Threshold used her data to challenge the doctrine. Kindling used her data to radicalize. Every institution that touched the document converted it from evidence into ammunition, and the conversion was so efficient that within ten days the document's content — what nitro is, what breaches are, what the absorbed experience — had become secondary to the document's position in a series of institutional arguments that predated it and would outlast it.

She had published the most important scientific document in human history. The document was now a data point in someone else's debate.

---

The Feed's historical cache was accessible through the terminal's archive function — the stored coverage from days one through three, the breaking-news cycle preserved in its original timestamps. She did not open it often. She had opened it twice. Both times she had been looking for the same thing.

Mirelle's name.

The attribution section had been published exactly as Sofia had written it. *Primary evidence: Mirelle Dubois, independent journalist, The Wire. Posthumous. Field data collected during active investigation of NitroCore infrastructure in the Neo-Shanghai Sump and Deep Sump, 2174-2175. Absorbed during BLACKWEIR Confluence event.*

The name had appeared in the coverage. Day one: "The publication credits evidence to a journalist, Mirelle Dubois, who was reportedly absorbed during the BLACKWEIR event." Day two: "Dubois, a former Wire correspondent, compiled what appears to be an extensive field investigation of NitroCore's Sump infrastructure prior to her absorption." Day three: "The methodology of the Dubois evidence has been questioned by analysts who note that field data collected by an independent journalist without institutional affiliation—"

By day four, the name had receded. The coverage that remained was about Sofia — "the Reyes publication," "the Reyes claims," "Dr. Reyes's methodology." The institutional identity attached to the living author, not the dead one. Mirelle's evidence was the foundation of the document. Mirelle's name was a paragraph in the attribution section. The coverage had metabolized her the way it metabolized everything — the person became a detail, the detail became a reference, the reference became a footnote in the institutional debate that had consumed the document whole.

Sofia had written the attribution with care. Every word deliberate. The name first. The affiliation. The method. The death — *absorbed,* not killed, because the distinction was the entire point of the document. The dead-hand cache had arrived with a metadata header: *Do not wait.* Mirelle had not waited. Had walked the Sump. Had pressed a sensor against conduit housings. Had documented the engineering from inside the machine. The dead-hand protocol had activated because the journalist who built it was inside the infrastructure she'd been investigating, and the infrastructure had not distinguished between the investigator and the eight million others.

Sofia had written the document. Mirelle had built the evidence. The document was circulating under Sofia's name because Sofia was alive and Mirelle was not. The attribution was correct. The attribution was also insufficient. A name on a page was not the same as a byline on a story, and Mirelle would have known the difference because Mirelle had been a journalist and journalists understood that evidence does not move people — stories move people. Evidence supports. Stories compel. The publication was evidence. It was not a story. It was a correlation coefficient and a methodology and forty-three pages of appendices and a conclusion that was irrefutable and completely inert.

Mirelle would have made it a better story. Sofia had thought this on the night she'd pressed confirm, sitting at the window with cold coffee, and the thought had not changed in ten days because the thought was not a feeling. It was an assessment. The instrument she had used — scientific publication — was the wrong instrument for what the moment required. She did not have the right one. She did not know how to acquire it. She was a scientist. She had done the science. The science was complete and the world had responded with methodology concerns.

---

She went to the kitchen. Made coffee. The kettle boiled. The heating element drew power from the conduit grid — the same infrastructure, the same system, the same trunk-line network whose throughput-coherence dynamics she had documented in fifty-five pages that the world was metabolizing into institutional positions while the grid continued to run and the hum continued through the floor.

She poured the coffee. Drank it standing at the counter. The ceramic was warm in her hands. Outside the window, the city's conduit glow traced the vertical stratification she could draw from memory — amber lines thickening as they descended from the Spires to the Mid-Levels, brightening into the dense lattice of the lower districts where the infrastructure was heaviest and the people were closest and the throughput-coherence gradient did exactly what her paper said it did.

The apartment was paid for. Thirty days. After that, the institution's architecture would reclaim the space and she would be somewhere else — a place she had not identified, in a life she had not planned, with credentials that no longer carried institutional weight and a publication record that was simultaneously the most cited atmospheric study in history and the most contested. Her name was in the Feed's archive. Her data was in the world. Her lab access was revoked, her immersion pod credentials suspended, her employment terminated, her housing temporary, her field kit the only instrument she still owned that was not subject to institutional recall.

The field kit was green. Operational. Recording.

She walked to the window. Neo-Shanghai at night — the same view she had looked at ten days ago when the document had entered the world and she had waited for the world to notice what it was standing on. The Anchor Zone shimmer was faint violet above the Resonance District. The grid was running. The amber glow pulsed in the conduit lines with the rhythm she'd measured for nine years — steady, structured, the infrastructure's heartbeat visible from sixty-one floors up. The city had not changed shape. It would not change shape. The document was in the world and the world had noted it and the world had filed it and the world would reference it in the arguments the world was already having, and the grid would continue because the grid was not an argument. The grid was infrastructure. Infrastructure does not pause for evidence. Infrastructure runs.

The hum was steady through the floor. Through the counter. Through her feet. The same 0.7 Hz pulse. The same structured signal. The same sound she had measured and documented and published and watched the world convert into positions and debates and encyclicals and annotations and methodology concerns. The signal had not changed. The signal was not listening to the coverage. The signal was not affected by the NitroCore review or the VEC termination or the Church's assertion that the Voice was not wounded. The signal continued because the infrastructure continued because the grid continued because the world continued to run on exactly what she had told them it ran on, and they had heard her, and they were still running.

The coffee cooled in her hands.

She stood at the window and listened to the hum and did not look away from the city that had not changed shape and would not change shape and had absorbed the truth about itself the way it absorbed everything — completely, structurally, without stopping.
