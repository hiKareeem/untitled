## Detailed Thematic Analysis

### Truth as Weapon — Strong

The chapter's central act — depositing a dead-hand cache — reframes truth from information to armament. Mirelle doesn't publish; she *arms* the data. The dead-hand protocol is truth weaponized through absence: it fires not when Mirelle acts but when she stops acting. The 72-hour check-in makes her survival the safety on a loaded weapon.

Key moments:
- "One copy was not enough. One copy meant that removing her removed the story."
- "That's the story" — Mirelle's response to Talia's "That's a pattern"
- The transaction log as rhetoric reference: truth reduced to automated protocol

The epigraph — "The difference between a rumor and a report is one surviving source" — is Mirelle's own words. The dead-hand is her answer to the vulnerability the epigraph describes.

### Systemic Complicity — Strong

Every institution in this chapter accommodates the disappearances:
- **The Feed:** "All affected personnel reassigned. No disruption to service expected." The bureaucratic grammar of erasure.
- **NitroCore:** Tolerance bands widened. Junctions reclassified. The definition of "wrong" adjusted.
- **Gauthier/The Wire:** Demands evidence standard that the institutional suppression makes impossible to meet.
- **Even Black Babel:** Sells truth to anyone, including the institutions suppressing it. Talia profits from the chaos without taking sides.

The Feed notice expanding from "Junction 7" to "five sectors" — the reclassification language outpacing Mirelle's ability to count — is the system absorbing the evidence before it can become a story.

### Commodification of Suffering — Moderate

The Babel price literalizes this theme: Mirelle trades her source network metadata for the dead-hand service. Talia explains the economy explicitly: "You publish, markets move, my other clients get nervous, prices go up. The chaos you create is good for business."

Talia profits from Mirelle's courage. NitroCore profits from the infrastructure that kills its workers. The information economy and the nitro economy run on the same fuel: other people's exposure.

### Architecture of Power — Moderate

NitroCore restructuring access to the disappearance-adjacent junctions is power expressed through classification architecture. The reclassification from municipal to restricted-corporate doesn't acknowledge any problem — it simply redraws the boundaries of who can see the problem. The tolerance bands widen. The definition changes. The infrastructure remains.

### What We Owe — Moderate

The dead-hand is a machine built from obligation. Mirelle can't protect her sources by being present (she might not be); she protects them by building an automated system that speaks for her in her absence. The question: is a machine an adequate substitute for a person's responsibility?

Talia's refusal — "I'm a node, not a mourner" — is the explicit rejection of obligation. She stores but doesn't care. She profits but doesn't grieve. The infrastructure of truth-preservation stripped of all human weight.
