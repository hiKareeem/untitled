# Mirelle Dubois

*Character dossier created via Build Characters workflow — Autonomous Generation*

## Basic Information

**Name:** Mirelle Dubois

**Age:** 34

**Profession/Status:** Independent investigative journalist. No corporate affiliation — which in 2175 means no safety net, no evacuation priority, no legal standing in corporate territory. She operates outside every institutional framework that might protect her. This is both her methodology and her vulnerability.

**Social origin:** Franco-Maghreb heritage, raised in the mid-tier districts of the European Outlands — not Sump-poor but not Spire-comfortable. Her father was a logistics clerk for a regional shipping firm; her mother taught primary school in a settlement that lost its nitro allocation when the regional grid was rerouted. She grew up watching institutions fail the people they were supposed to serve, which she internalized not as cynicism but as mission: someone has to write it down.

## Physical appearance

**General description:**
Lean, angular, built for endurance rather than comfort. She moves through crowds like water — not invisible, but hard to fix in place. Her body language shifts with context: in the Sump she compresses, shoulders forward, eye contact minimal, blending into the noise. In corporate offices she expands — sits forward, holds silence like a weapon, takes up space she hasn't been offered. She dresses functionally: layered clothing that works across climate zones and social strata, nothing that marks her as belonging to any particular district or class. Her hands are her most distinctive feature — long-fingered, always moving: adjusting equipment, tapping notes into her wrist display, turning a stylus end over end while she listens.

**Distinctive features:**
- An ocular implant in her left eye — not top-shelf corporate, but well-maintained and heavily customized. She uses it for recording, signal filtering, AR overlay, and low-light enhancement. When she adjusts its gain, there's a subtle shift in the iris — a tell that experienced interviewees learn to watch for.
- A thin scar across the bridge of her nose from a shrapnel graze covering the Caracas supply riots in 2168. She's never had it repaired. She says it reminds her to duck. It actually reminds her of the cameraman who didn't.
- Callused fingertips from years of manual data entry — she keeps physical backup notes in shorthand on actual paper, a habit from covering conflict zones where electronics get confiscated or fried.

**Story adaptation:**
Mirelle's physical presence should feel adaptable — a person who becomes what the room requires. In the Sump, she's another face in the crush. In a corporate tower, she's composed enough to pass but never quite comfortable — always slightly wrong for the furniture. Her exhaustion accumulates visibly across her arc: the bags deepen, the hands move faster, the pauses between sentences get shorter. By Phase 3, she looks like a woman running on adrenaline and caffeine and the conviction that stopping means dying.

## Personality

**Dominant traits:**
- **Relentless:** She does not stop. Sources dry up, she finds new ones. Leads go cold, she crosses them against other data. She is a compiler, a pattern-matcher, a person who treats scattered fragments as puzzle pieces and will not rest until the picture resolves.
- **Paranoid (functionally):** Copies everything. Dead-drops evidence in multiple locations. Encrypts communications across redundant channels. Assumes every system will betray her. This is not pathology — it is twelve years of watching systems betray her, distilled into method.
- **Socially fluid:** Can sit with Frame Runners in a shipping-container bar and interview a CEO in a mahogany-paneled office on the same day. She adjusts register, body language, and approach to match her subject — not as deception, but as professional competence. She knows how people work.
- **Intellectually honest (to a fault):** She doesn't embellish. She doesn't speculate beyond data. She follows evidence where it leads, even when it leads somewhere she doesn't want to go. This is her strongest professional quality and her greatest personal vulnerability — she cannot look away.
- **Emotionally contained:** Not cold, but disciplined. She has learned to partition feeling from function in the field. The grief, the rage, the horror — they get processed later, alone, in a hotel room with the lights off. Or they don't get processed at all.

**Strengths:**
- Extraordinary ability to read people — she catches deflections, notes micro-expressions, uses silence as a tool. She knows when an interview is becoming a relationship and when to push.
- Pattern recognition across disparate data sets — she sees correlations that specialists miss because they're siloed. She's a generalist in a world of specialists, and that's exactly what the story requires.
- Operational tradecraft learned from a decade in conflict zones — encrypted communications, dead-drops, counter-surveillance awareness, source protection protocols. She operates like a low-grade intelligence officer.

**Weaknesses:**
- She doesn't ask for help. She doesn't trust institutions, partners, or backup plans. She pushes alone into situations that require a team.
- She cannot separate herself from the story. She says "the story" when she means "the truth" and "the truth" when she means "the thing I need to be right about." Her professional identity and her moral identity are fused.
- She is physically running herself into the ground. Irregular sleep, irregular meals, chronic stimulant use. She treats her body as equipment to be maintained at minimum operational capacity.

**Internal contradictions:**
1. **Treats the story as more real than the people in it** — she will protect a source's identity but will not hesitate to publish information that destroys a source's life, because the truth matters more than any individual. She tells herself this is principle. It is also armor.
2. **Fundamentally unable to trust institutions, yet her entire project depends on institutions responding to truth** — her Lie ("if people know, they'll act") requires faith in collective response that her experience has systematically demolished. She publishes anyway.
3. **Paranoid tradecraft masks a deep need to be believed** — she maintains dead-drops and evidence caches not just for safety but because she is terrified that her work will disappear without trace. The paranoia is real. The wound underneath it is the fear of irrelevance.
4. **Fluid across social strata, belonging to none** — she can read any room but has no room of her own. No corporate citizenship, no faction affiliation, no community. Her mobility is her loneliness repackaged as freedom.
5. **Emotionally disciplined on the surface, accumulating unprocessed damage underneath** — she partitions grief and horror into "later" and "later" never arrives. This isn't stoicism; it's debt. By the time she's absorbed, she is carrying twelve years of unprocessed witness-trauma.
6. **Chasing a truth she suspects will be useless** — she already knows, in her gut, that publication changes nothing. The Mediterranean refugee corridor piece "didn't change anything." She publishes anyway because the alternative — that truth is irrelevant — is a conclusion she cannot survive.
7. **Her recklessness reads as courage but is partly self-destruction** — she pushes too far, too fast, without backup, not only because she believes no one else will carry the truth, but because she has quietly stopped valuing her own survival as much as she values the story's survival.

## Desires and fears

**What they want (conscious):**
Publish the suppressed 2170 VEC report linking nitro infrastructure density to breach probability. Expose the Sump sacrifice — the engineered geography that routes void risk downward onto expendable populations. Break the story before the story breaks everything.

**What they want (unconscious):**
Prove that truth matters. That revealing it changes something. That the world she lives in is responsive to evidence, that the systems she despises are at least capable of shame. If this is wrong — if truth is just noise — then her entire life has been a mistake.

**Biggest fears:**
- That she will uncover the truth and no one will care. Not suppression — indifference. The world will shrug.
- That she has become the thing she investigates: a system that processes human suffering into a product (the story) and calls it public service.
- That she will die without the story getting out — that the dead-drops will fail, the evidence will decay, and no one will know what she found.
- That the absorbed are still in there, aware, and she'll become one of them.

**Blind spots:**
- She cannot see that her obsession with the story is itself a form of consumption — she is mining human misery for narrative the same way the corporations mine nitro for power. The parallel is invisible to her.
- She underestimates institutional inertia. She believes that sufficient evidence overcomes sufficient power. It doesn't. Power doesn't need to disprove truth — it only needs to outlast attention.
- She assumes her sources are as committed to the truth as she is. They aren't. They have families, debts, fears. When she pushes them, some of them break in ways she doesn't anticipate.
- She does not see that her relationship with Zeyad is becoming personally important to her, not just professionally useful. She would call this a tactical assessment. It isn't.

## Background and history

**Childhood:**
Grew up in the European mid-tier — the buffer zone between corporate comfort and Outland scarcity. Her mother's school lost funding when the regional grid was rerouted; the settlement shrank. Mirelle watched neighbors leave, services collapse, the community contract dissolve — not through violence but through administrative neglect. No one wrote about it. That absence — the silence where a record should have been — became the hole she has spent her adult life trying to fill.

**Key figures:**
- **Her mother (Layla):** Teacher who stayed in the settlement after the grid reroute. Taught Mirelle that institutions are only as good as the people willing to hold them accountable. Died of chronic nitro-exposure illness when Mirelle was 22 — a recognized occupational condition for residents near degraded conduit infrastructure. The death was unremarkable. Statistically expected. Mirelle has never forgiven the statistic.
- **Djamel Harouni:** Her first editor. Algiers-based independent outlet. Taught her source protection, encryption, the ethics of publishing under threat. Killed by a car bomb in 2166 while covering a refinement-worker strike. The bombing was attributed to "criminal elements." Mirelle knows it was corporate security. She can't prove it.
- **The cameraman in Caracas (unnamed in her memory — she's blocked his name):** Died in the supply riots while she survived. The scar on her nose is his legacy. She carries the guilt of living through something someone else didn't.

**Formative experiences:**
1. **The grid reroute (childhood):** Watching her community dissolve through administrative neglect. Taught her: institutions don't fail — they choose who to abandon.
2. **Djamel's death (age 26):** Taught her: truth-telling is lethal. People die for asking questions. She either stops or accepts the cost. She accepted.
3. **The VEC circuit purge exposé (age 32):** Her biggest success. Leaked internal memos proving VEC was clearing Frame Runner tunnels for a classified NitroCore project, not for "infrastructure safety." The story forced VEC to back off. But nothing changed structurally. The tunnels were cleared six months later under a different pretext. Taught her: individual stories can win individual battles; systemic truths are harder to kill because they're harder to see.

**Baggage and wounds:**
- Unprocessed grief for her mother, Djamel, and the cameraman in Caracas — never properly mourned, filed under "the cost of the work."
- A quiet, corrosive suspicion that her work doesn't matter — that every exposé is a pebble thrown at a cliff.
- Physical toll: chronic sleep debt, stimulant dependency, early-stage nitro ear from sustained Sump fieldwork, a body treated as a tool rather than a home.

## Transformation arc

**Starting point:**
Professional, driven, paranoid, effective. Working the Sump demographics story — sources disappearing, editor dismissing her work as "correlation not causation." She is at the top of her investigative form: sharp, connected, meticulous. She is also running on fumes and held together by conviction rather than health. Her Lie — "if people know, they'll act" — is intact but strained.

**Catalysts of change:**
1. Evidence trail strengthens: matched-district datasets confirm that Sump demographics correlate with breach probability at rates impossible to dismiss. The story becomes undeniable.
2. Black Babel contact and Zeyad exchange create information bridges that let her triangulate corporate, governmental, and scientific data streams. For the first time, she can see the full pattern.
3. Deep Sump investigation: she finds pre-installed BLACKWEIR flood valves — infrastructure designed for sacrifice that predates the crisis it's supposed to respond to. The architecture of genocide was built before the justification.
4. Photographs the flood valves. Plants her final evidence cache. Begins transmission to Black Babel: "The infrastructure was always designed to—"

**Transformation (Arc Type: Tragic / Disillusionment → Transcendence-by-evidence):**
Mirelle's arc is not a transformation of character but a fulfillment of it. She does not change — she becomes what she always was, pushed to its logical terminus. The hunter becomes the evidence. The truth-seeker becomes the truth. Her Lie is tested posthumously: she wanted the world to know. The world knows. The world shrugs. But the evidence persists — her dead-hand packages, her fragmented transmissions from inside the breach, her patterns appearing in hum recordings and comm static. She becomes proof that the truth exists, even if the truth doesn't save anyone.

**Ending point:**
Absorbed mid-sentence during BLACKWEIR. Her transmission to Black Babel cut mid-word. Her body becomes a void-analog silhouette — maintaining shape, changing substance. Her recording equipment continues transmitting audio fragments: her voice speaking words she never recorded, assembled into partial observations, as if someone is trying to describe what they're seeing from inside using only the words available to them. She is not dead. She is not alive. She is proof. Her Lie — "if people know, they'll act" — is answered: they know. They don't act. The dead-hand evidence releases. The world reads it. The world files it. Mirelle Dubois becomes the most important footnote in a report no one finishes.

## Voice and mannerisms

**How they speak:**
Direct, economical, precise. She uses short declarative sentences in professional contexts and loosens into dryer, more sardonic phrasing in private or with people she trusts. She doesn't waste words on preamble. She asks questions that sound simple and aren't.

- To a source: "I'm tracking the outages. The official news says it's maintenance. But maintenance doesn't leave frost on the conduits."
- To a diplomat: "I don't need someone with power. I need someone who cares."
- To herself: "That's the story."
- Correcting an assumption: "Independent. I don't work for the corps. I don't work for the UGC. I pay in hard creds."

She uses silence the way other journalists use follow-up questions — she lets it sit, lets the other person fill it with words they hadn't planned to say. She notices when it doesn't work ("Aurielle just watched her. Waiting.") and adjusts.

She does not monologue. She does not explain herself. She states what she knows, asks what she doesn't, and moves.

**How they think:**
In correlations and patterns. Her internal monologue is data-forward: she maps, cross-references, overlays. "Too many stories packed into too little space, all of them fighting to be heard" — she perceives the world as information competing for attention, and her job is triage.

She also thinks in assessments of people — reading them the way an intelligence officer reads assets. *She's good. Very good. Or very practiced. Maybe both.* She evaluates honesty, motivation, and utility simultaneously. This is not manipulative — it's professional. She doesn't use people cynically; she understands them instrumentally.

Her deepest thoughts are questions she doesn't ask aloud: *Did mine look like speculation?* She tests her own work constantly, holding it to the standard she applies to everyone else. The self-doubt is quiet but structural.

**Tics, habits, quirks:**
- Turns a stylus end over end while listening — faster when she's processing, still when she's about to ask the question that matters.
- Adjusts the gain on her ocular implant when shifting between environments — a subtle iris shift that experienced subjects learn to watch for.
- Maintains physical shorthand notes on actual paper — a conflict-zone habit from covering places where electronics get confiscated. She carries a small weatherproof notebook.
- Uses the phrase "That's the story" as both professional assessment and personal mantra — it means "this is what matters" and "this is what I'm willing to die for" and she doesn't always distinguish between the two.
- Books transport before she's sure she'll need it. She's always moving toward the next location before the current one is finished. Forward momentum as survival strategy.

## Themes explored

**Core contradictions:**
- She mines human suffering for narrative and calls it public service — the same extraction logic she's investigating in the corporations. The journalist and the corporation both convert invisible anguish into a product. The difference is she believes her product serves the public good. The similarity is she doesn't ask the suffering if they agree.
- Her Lie — "if people know, they'll act" — requires faith in collective moral response that her experience has systematically demolished. She publishes anyway, because the alternative is nihilism, and nihilism is a conclusion she cannot metabolize.
- She is the freest character in the story (no faction, no institution, no corporate citizenship) and the most vulnerable (no safety net, no evacuation priority, no one coming if she's caught). Her freedom and her expendability are the same thing.
- Her absorption transforms her from subject to object — from the person who uncovers truth to the truth that needs uncovering. The hunter becomes evidence. The journalist becomes the story.

**Central question:**
What happens to the truth-seeker when the truth consumes them? And afterward: does truth that no one acts on still matter?

**The Lie:** "If people know, they'll act."

**How the Lie is tested:** She gets the truth out. The dead-hand packages release. The world reads her evidence. The world files it. The shrug is the answer. Her Lie is not refuted — it's made irrelevant. Truth without consequence is just noise. But the noise persists. And maybe, eventually, someone listens.
