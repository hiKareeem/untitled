# Chapter 47
## Technically Feasible

> *"The proposal was received. The proposal was reviewed. The proposal was forwarded to the appropriate subcommittee for assessment of economic impact, jurisdictional scope, and implementation feasibility. The proposal remains under active consideration."*
>
> — UGC Emergency Session procedural record, Day 3 (standard acknowledgment template)

---

The chamber was designed for consensus.

She could see it in the architecture — the curved seating arrangement that placed every delegate at equal distance from the central presentation floor, the acoustic engineering that carried a speaker's voice to every position without amplification, the ceiling height calibrated to produce authority without intimidation. The New Geneva UGC complex had been built in the decade after the Accords, designed by committee, the institutional architecture of a body whose founding premise was that global coordination could be achieved through process. The premise was visible in the room. The room believed in itself.

Sofia stood at the presentation terminal on the central floor and felt the hum through the building's foundation — different from Neo-Shanghai's. Lower. The conduit infrastructure in New Geneva was older, the trunk-line network a first-generation installation that predated the high-density routing of the Pacific grid cities. The frequency was 0.4 Hz. She had measured it in the lobby that morning with the field kit — the instrument pulled from her bag and held against the marble floor while delegates stepped around her, the green indicator light pulsing at a rhythm she did not recognize because she had spent a decade calibrating to Neo-Shanghai's 0.7 Hz and this was a different city built on the same infrastructure carrying the same signal at a different frequency and the difference was data and the data was that the void sounded different here and sounded different everywhere and sounded.

The council chamber held one hundred and twelve delegates. She had counted the occupied seats from the gallery before descending to the presentation floor — a habit, the same compulsion that counted sensor readings and correlation coefficients and the number of boxes she had not unpacked in the Mid-Levels apartment. One hundred and twelve delegates from seventy-three member states and autonomous economic zones, seated in the curved arrangement the architecture provided, facing the central floor where she stood with a terminal and seventy-nine pages of evidence and no institutional affiliation.

The session chair introduced her. The institutional vocabulary — "Dr. Sofia Reyes, independent atmospheric physicist, formerly of the Void Exploration Consortium, presenting on behalf of the Erasure List research collaborative" — carried across the chamber in the acoustic architecture's even distribution. *Independent.* The word the institutional vocabulary used when it meant *terminated.* *Formerly.* The word that converted nine years of classified research into a temporal marker. She heard the introduction the way she heard all institutional language now: as construction, not description. The words were scaffolding. The building was elsewhere.

"Thank you," she said. "I'll be brief."

She was not brief.

---

The presentation ran forty-seven minutes.

She had planned for twenty. The proposal was eighteen pages of core text — the de-escalation framework, the throughput reduction model, the implementation protocol, the economic impact assessment. Accessible register. Subject-verb-object. Qualifiers stripped. She had written it for a body that did not read appendices, and the appendices were there for the body's technical advisors: sixty-one pages, full methodology, every dataset reproducible, checksums preserved.

She presented the core framework in eleven minutes. Global nitro throughput reduction of twenty percent over five years. Four percent per year. Staged implementation allowing grid infrastructure to reconfigure. Alternative energy pathways — thermal, solar, tidal, the pre-nitro technologies archived in patents that NitroCore held and mothballed facilities that NitroCore owned and a transition timeline that assumed NitroCore would cooperate because the alternative to cooperation was the thing they had heard three days ago.

She did not call it the Broadcast. She heard the word in the chamber — delegates using it, the session chair using it, the media vocabulary that had attached itself to the eleven seconds the way media vocabulary attached itself to everything: through repetition, through accessibility, through the consensus of people who needed a name and chose the name that required the least explanation. *The Broadcast.* As if the void had been performing for an audience. As if the eleven seconds were content.

She called it what she had been calling it. "The transmission event." The scientific designation. The term that described the mechanism without interpreting the intent, that processed the eleven seconds as data without deciding whether the data was a message or a census or a plea or something the scientific vocabulary did not contain and that no vocabulary contained because the event had been produced by an intelligence that did not use vocabulary.

She presented the transmission event data. The spectral analysis. The individual acoustic signatures — thousands of them, each one distinct, each one identifiable, each one the frequency-domain equivalent of a person saying their name. She presented the throughput-coherence correlation recalculated with the complete global dataset the transmission event had provided. She presented the sacrifice-district boundary analysis. She presented the mage network biological data — the neural coupling measurements that documented what happened to a human nervous system when the conduit infrastructure carried signal at the densities the current throughput produced.

She presented the number.

"Twenty percent," she said. "Global reduction. Five-year implementation. The math is in the appendices. The math has been verified by thirty-one independent researchers across twelve jurisdictions. The math is not in dispute."

Silence. The acoustic architecture carrying it evenly to every seat.

"The alternative," she said, "is what you heard three days ago."

---

The questions began at the twelve-minute mark.

A delegate from the Pacific Corporate Coalition's economic advisory board — a man whose name appeared on the session feed as Delegate Haruno, PCC Economic Zone 7, whose credentials included fourteen years of energy infrastructure policy and whose face carried the specific expression of a person preparing to ask a question whose answer he already knew and whose purpose was not information but record.

"Dr. Reyes." The chamber's acoustics delivered the name without emphasis. "The economic impact of a twenty-percent reduction in global nitro throughput — has this been modeled?"

"Yes."

"And the projection?"

"Recession in grid-dependent economies for eighteen to thirty-six months. The severity varies by regional dependency ratio — the Pacific grid cities experience the deepest contraction, the Free African States the shallowest due to maintained alternative infrastructure under their Accords provisions. The model accounts for transition subsidies, infrastructure retraining costs, and the throughput reduction's effect on Anchor Zone activity. The full economic impact assessment is in Appendix C."

"Eighteen to thirty-six months of recession." Haruno repeated the number. The chamber's acoustics made the repetition sound like a verdict. "Affecting every grid-dependent economy on the planet."

"Yes."

"And the proposal's position is that this is acceptable."

She looked at him. The presentation terminal between them. The chamber's one hundred and twelve delegates in their curved seating. The acoustic architecture carrying every word to every position.

"The proposal's position is that I have modeled the economic impact," she said. "Recession in grid-dependent economies for eighteen to thirty-six months. The alternative is what you heard three days ago."

She said it the same way she had said it the first time. The same words. The same construction. The repetition was deliberate — not rhetoric, not emphasis. The answer had not changed because the question had not changed. The question was *is the cost acceptable* and the answer was *the cost is the cost and the alternative is the alternative* and she had modeled both and the model was in the appendices and the appendices were reproducible and the question was not scientific. The question was political. She did not do political. She did the math.

More questions. Thirty-five minutes of them. A delegate from the Lagos Administrative Zone asking about regional implementation variance. A delegate from the South Asian Cooperative asking about the mage network data's verification methodology. A delegate from the European See asking about the Accords framework for mandating throughput reduction across sovereign economic zones — the legal architecture, the jurisdictional question, the mechanism by which a UGC resolution became enforceable policy in member states whose energy infrastructure was privately operated.

She answered each one. The science held. The methodology held. The legal architecture — she did not have an answer for the legal architecture. "I am an atmospheric physicist," she said. "The legal framework for implementation is the council's jurisdiction. The science tells you what the number is. The science does not tell you how to make the number happen. That is what this body exists to do."

The session chair called the presentation complete at the forty-seven-minute mark. Sofia returned to the gallery.

---

The gallery was elevated — a mezzanine level above the main chamber floor, separated by a glass partition that the acoustic architecture penetrated. She could hear the proceedings. She could not participate in them. The gallery was for observers: press, staff, members of the public who had registered through the emergency session's access protocol. She had registered as public. The form had asked for institutional affiliation. She had written *none.*

The field kit was in her bag. She felt its weight against her hip — the instrument that had measured the 0.7 Hz signal in VEC's immersion pod and the BLACKWEIR convergence from the thirty-fifth floor and the synchronization onset from the thirty-ninth floor and the 0.4 Hz hum in this building's lobby that morning. The field kit did not care about institutional affiliation. The field kit measured what it measured. She reached into the bag and touched the casing. The green indicator pulsed against her fingertips. Operational. The reflex — checking it when anxious, not the readings, just the physical presence of the device. The instrument that had outlasted the institution.

The council debated.

She watched from the gallery. The curved seating. The delegates conferring in pairs, in clusters, the institutional choreography of a body processing a proposal through its procedural architecture. Amendments proposed. Amendments withdrawn. The session chair managing the queue. The economic impact assessment cited fourteen times in forty minutes — she counted, the same compulsion, the same reflex that counted sensor readings and boxes and acoustic signatures.

The vote was called at 16:22 local time.

She watched the tally populate the display. Real-time. Each delegate's vote registered as a light on the chamber's status board — green for approval, red against, the binary that the institutional architecture reduced every question to. The lights appeared in sequence as delegates submitted. Green. Red. Green. Green. Red. The pattern emerging — more green than red, the distribution uneven, the PCC economic bloc voting red in a cluster that appeared on the board like a geographical objection, the European See voting green, the African Administrative Zones split, the South Asian Cooperative green except for three.

The final count appeared.

Sixty-seven in favor. Forty-five against. The proposal passed.

She sat in the gallery and looked at the number and the number was correct. Sixty-seven. Forty-five. Sixty-percent approval for a twenty-percent reduction. The math of institutional consensus — the number that meant the body had decided, by a margin of twenty-two votes, that the people inside the infrastructure were people and that the infrastructure's throughput should be reduced by a fifth over five years and that the economic cost was the economic cost and the alternative was the alternative.

The number was on the board. The proposal had passed.

She did not move.

---

The injunction arrived at 17:02.

Forty minutes. She saw it on the session feed — the real-time procedural display that the gallery's terminals provided, the institutional record that documented every action the emergency session produced. The filing appeared between two procedural notations: a scheduling confirmation for the next session day and a request for translation services. Routine formatting. Standard typography.

*NitroCore Energy Holdings, Ltd. and Helix Energetics, Inc. v. United Governance Council — Emergency Injunction Filing (Joint). Pursuant to Article 14 of the Global Accords Framework (Economic Sovereignty Provisions), petitioners seek immediate suspension of Resolution ES-2171-047 pending judicial review of: (a) economic impact on sovereign grid-dependent infrastructure; (b) evidentiary sufficiency of atmospheric data submitted by non-institutional researchers; (c) jurisdictional scope of UGC authority over privately operated energy systems. Filing timestamp: 17:02:14 NGA.*

She read the filing. She read it again. The institutional vocabulary — *economic sovereignty*, *evidentiary sufficiency*, *non-institutional researchers*, *jurisdictional scope*. Every phrase a mechanism. Every mechanism designed to convert a passed resolution into a suspended resolution, to convert sixty-seven votes into a legal question, to convert the number on the board into a number in a filing that would enter a judicial review process that would take months or years and that would produce hearings and briefs and expert testimony and procedural motions and the grid would continue running at full throughput while the judiciary processed the question of whether the grid should continue running at full throughput. Two corporations. The extraction company and the infrastructure company. The entity that pulled nitro from the void and the entity that moved it through the conduits — both names on the same filing, the same timestamp, the same Article 14. The industry had filed as a bloc.

Forty minutes. The interval between the vote and the injunction. NitroCore's legal team had not waited for the vote. The filing was prepared before the tally appeared. The injunction was ready when the resolution passed because NitroCore had modeled the vote the way Sofia had modeled the economic impact — with data, with methodology, with the institutional competence of a corporation whose legal architecture was built to process exactly this contingency. They had known the vote would pass. They had prepared the mechanism that would make the passage irrelevant.

Not a rejection. Not a defeat. Not a vote against the science or a challenge to the methodology or an institutional position that the data was wrong. *A suspension.* The proposal existed. The vote existed. The sixty-seven green lights existed. The injunction did not erase them. The injunction placed them in a legal architecture that would process them at the speed the legal architecture processed everything: slowly, procedurally, with full respect for the evidentiary standards and jurisdictional frameworks that the Accords had established to prevent exactly the kind of unilateral action that the resolution represented. The Accords — the framework designed to enable global coordination — were the legal mechanism that prevented global coordination. The architecture built to solve the problem was the architecture that preserved it.

She sat in the gallery. The chamber was emptying — delegates rising, conferring, the institutional choreography of a body that had voted and produced a result and watched the result suspended before the session chair had closed the proceedings. The display board still showed the tally. Sixty-seven. Forty-five. The green lights still lit. The number still correct.

The field kit pulsed in her bag. The 0.4 Hz hum through the building's foundation — different frequency, same infrastructure, the conduit network that connected New Geneva to the same grid that connected Neo-Shanghai to the same void that had transmitted through every connected system three days ago and that was still there, still structured, still carrying the signal at whatever frequency the local infrastructure produced, and the signal did not care about Article 14 and the signal did not care about judicial review and the signal was the signal and the infrastructure was running.

The gallery was empty.

She sat in the chair she had occupied for two hours and eleven minutes. The session feed still displayed the injunction filing. The procedural record still showed the vote. Both existed in the same institutional architecture — the resolution and the mechanism that suspended it, the sixty-seven votes and the legal filing that converted them from a decision into a question, the proposal and the injunction, the science and the process.

The hum came through the floor. 0.4 Hz. The old infrastructure. The first-generation conduit network that had been built when nitro was new and the dependency was forming and the Accords were being drafted by people who believed that institutional architecture could govern what institutional architecture had produced. The hum was quieter here than in Neo-Shanghai. Quieter did not mean less. Quieter meant the infrastructure was older and the insulation was thicker and the building had been designed to contain the sound the way the Accords had been designed to contain the dependency and the containment was structural and the structure was the thing that had just filed an injunction against a twenty-percent reduction in the signal it was containing.

She thought about the proposal. Eighteen pages. Sixty-one pages of appendices. The number. Twenty percent. Five years. Thirty-one researchers. Twelve jurisdictions. The transmission event data. The individual acoustic signatures. The sacrifice-district topology. The mages' biological data. The Mirelle attribution — the 0.91 correlation, the fragments, the standing instruction. *Do not wait.* She had not waited. She had presented. The body had voted. The vote had passed. The injunction had arrived in forty minutes.

She thought about Mirelle.

Not the signature in the data. Not the 0.91 correlation or the acoustic fragments or the structured output that resembled a journalist dictating a report from inside the infrastructure that had absorbed her. The person. The person who had walked into the Sump with a sensor and a deadline and the conviction that publishing the truth would produce a response and the truth had been published and the response was a methodology review and the response was a classification and the response was an independent panel staffed by former consultants and the response was an injunction filed in forty minutes by a legal team that had prepared it before the vote was counted.

Mirelle would have known this would happen.

She would have known because she had been a journalist and journalists understood institutions the way Sofia understood atmospheric data — as systems with predictable behavior, as architectures whose outputs could be modeled from their inputs, as structures that processed information according to rules that did not change when the information changed. Mirelle would have modeled the injunction the way Sofia had modeled the economic impact. Mirelle would have known that the legal architecture would do what legal architectures do, that the corporation would file what corporations file, that the forty minutes between the vote and the suspension was not a failure of the system but the system functioning exactly as designed.

Mirelle would have known. Mirelle published anyway.

Sofia sat in the empty gallery. The display board still showed the tally. The session feed still showed the injunction. The hum came through the floor at 0.4 Hz — quieter than Neo-Shanghai, older, the first infrastructure, the original dependency. The field kit pulsed green in her bag. The proposal was in the institutional pipeline and in the legal architecture and in Black Babel's distribution network and in the public record. The proposal existed. The vote existed. The injunction existed. All three occupied the same procedural space, the same institutional reality, the same architecture that processed proposals and votes and injunctions with the same typography and the same formatting and the same procedural record that would archive them all with equal administrative precision.

The science was correct. The vote was sufficient. The forty minutes were the answer.

She reached into her bag. Touched the field kit. The green indicator against her fingertips. The instrument that measured what it measured regardless of what the institutions did with the measurement. She held it for a moment. Then she stood, and she left the gallery, and the display board held the number in the empty chamber — sixty-seven green lights in a room designed for consensus, and the injunction filing beneath them, and the hum through the building's foundation carrying the same signal it had carried before the vote and during the vote and after the injunction and the signal did not know the difference because the signal was not institutional and the signal was not legal and the signal was the void and the void had spoken and the world had voted and the vote had passed and the answer had arrived in forty minutes.
