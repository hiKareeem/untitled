# Chapter 53
## I Was There

> *"Re: Nuwa Lin — Sump topology data. Your junction maps correlate with readings from three other sites. Forwarding to the Lagos coordination group. If you have more, send it. — K.Osei, Mage Testimony Network, relay node 7"*
>
> — Mage Testimony Network, public channel

---

She counted before she opened her eyes.

Creds: twenty-three. Room: paid through today — nine committed, leaving fourteen for food. Rice: enough for this morning. Oil: gone. Greens: Shuang had left a bundle of water spinach outside her door last night, wrapped in the damp cloth, the cloth she used for greens and nothing else.

The frozen notification. Still there. Still the first thing the overlay rendered, before the transit map, before the Feed, before the permanent grey of the dead zone in the upper-right quadrant.

*Lin, Nuwa. Credential tier: Basic. Evacuation priority: None assigned.*

Beside it, a second notification. Newer. Green where the credential notice was grey.

*Mage Testimony Network — 3 new responses.*

She had not expected responses. She had sent the message days ago — after the Broadcast, after the forty-seven names, after the counting. She had typed her name into a network built for mages and researchers and people with institutional vocabularies and she had pressed send and the overlay had confirmed transmission and she had closed it and gone back to the topology map on the wall because the topology map was the work and the work was what she knew how to do.

She opened her eyes. The ceiling. The concrete. The hum through the walls — the Mid-Level frequency, steady, residential-grade. The note that meant the system continued.

She opened the overlay.

---

The first response was from a relay node operator in Lagos. The message was short — four lines, technical, the compressed format of someone who processed large volumes of data and did not have time for the social architecture of introduction.

*Your junction maps. Forwarding to Lagos coordination. Conduit density readings match patterns we're seeing in three West African sites. If you have the full thirteen years, send the complete dataset. Partial is useless for longitudinal correlation.*

She read it twice. Once for inventory. Once for structure.

Lagos. She did not know where Lagos was in relation to the network's geography — she did not know the network's geography, had never mapped it, had only read the entries as they appeared on the public channel the way she read everything that scrolled through her cracked overlay: tilted, partial, compensated for. Lagos was a word on her display. The word was asking for Fuxi's data.

The second response was from someone whose network handle she did not recognize. The message was longer. Less technical. The vocabulary shifted between institutional precision and something rawer — the register of a person who had been trained in formal language and was now using it to describe something the training had not covered.

*Nuwa Lin — I work with a group documenting absorption patterns across sacrifice districts. Your message is the first ground-level topology report we've received from Neo-Shanghai. The institutional data is modeled. Yours is measured. There's a difference. Can you describe the path-poor structures in more detail? Building materials, construction era, proximity to junction nodes. We're building a database. Your corridor-level knowledge is what the models don't have.*

She sat with the words. *Your corridor-level knowledge is what the models don't have.* The sentence described her survival intelligence — the body's map, the routing along masonry walls, the instinctive path selection the document had quantified as correlation 0.89 — as something the institutional systems could not produce. She had carried this knowledge the way she carried the chemical staining on her fingers: as a mark of where she had been, visible to anyone who recognized it, invisible to everyone else.

Someone recognized it.

The third response was a single line.

*I was in the Dharavi containment zone. I counted too.*

No name. No handle. No institutional affiliation. Just the line, and the past tense, and the verb that Nuwa had used in her message because the verb was the first language and the first language was counting.

She closed the overlay. Opened it. Closed it.

The room was quiet. The topology map glowed on the wall — blue zones, red zones, the geography of survival rendered in low resolution by the projector she had rigged from a discarded public terminal. The map had not changed. The responses had not changed the map. The map was the same map she had been showing to families for weeks — the same blue zones she had walked people toward, the same red zones she had told them to avoid, the same masonry structures she had identified from Fuxi's chip data and the body's memory of which walls carried the signal and which walls did not.

The responses changed what the map was for.

---

She made rice. Plain. The water spinach she set aside — if she split the bundle, it would last two days. She ate standing at the sink. The bowl, the chopsticks, the routine. The counting that happened between bites without her deciding to count: twenty-three creds, fourteen after rent, the adhesive tube on day ten of twelve, the projector's power draw against the building's unmetered outlet.

Shuang appeared in the doorway at mid-morning. Two children behind her — the older one holding the younger one's hand, the grip that children maintained in buildings where the adults were busy and the corridors were dim and the world outside the door was not designed for them.

"Three families today," Shuang said. "The couple on Level 6 — they came back. They want to see the map again."

"They saw it last week."

"They want to see it again. The woman said she couldn't sleep. She kept trying to remember which corridor you told them to use and she couldn't remember and she spent the night counting the steps from their apartment to the stairwell and she wasn't sure the stairwell was masonry."

"It is."

"She wants to see."

Nuwa nodded. The couple on Level 6. She remembered them — the woman's hands gripping the edge of the table, the man standing behind her with his arms crossed, the posture of a person who did not want to be in a room where someone was showing him a map of where he would die if the walls were wrong. They had looked at the topology map and they had listened to Nuwa describe the path-poor zones and they had asked the question everyone asked: *Is it going to happen here?*

She had answered the way she always answered. She did not know. She knew which walls were masonry. She knew which corridors carried conduit. She knew which buildings had junction nodes beneath them and which buildings did not. She knew the topology. She did not know the future. The topology was what she could give them. The future was the system's.

"Send them up at mid-morning," she said. "I'll have the map running."

Shuang nodded and left. The children's footsteps on the stairs — quick, light, the sound of bodies too small to register on the building's worn concrete. Nuwa listened until the sound faded. Then she turned to the projector.

The overlay flickered. The chip data populated — Fuxi's junction maps, the conduit density readings, the thirteen years that lived behind her left ear in a corroded data port beside the cracked housing and the frozen notification and the permanent itch. She opened the topology layer. Blue and red on the wall. The geography she had learned from a dead man's records and the body's memory of which walls carried death and which walls carried nothing.

She opened the network channel.

The three responses sat in the queue. Lagos. The documentation group. The single line from Dharavi.

She opened the message field. The cursor blinked in the dead zone's margin. She tilted her head.

She typed.

---

The message took her forty minutes.

She had never described the path-poor structures in writing. She had described them in rooms — standing at the table, the projector throwing the map onto the wall, Shuang or Fen or the couple from Level 6 sitting across from her while she pointed at blue zones and said *masonry, poured concrete, pre-grid construction, the walls are dense enough to dampen the propagation front.* The words had been adequate for rooms. They were not adequate for the network.

She wrote the way she counted — one item at a time, each one held and examined before the next. Building by building. Her building: converted warehouse, Level 4, masonry exterior walls, poured concrete floors, minimal conduit routing, pre-residential-grid construction. Built before the infrastructure expansion brought metal-framed polymer into the standard. Path-poor. Confirmed survival pocket — she had lived in it during and after BLACKWEIR. The transit substation: decommissioned, concrete-walled, publicly accessible. The ceramic-lined water treatment facility: operational, the ceramic lining a legacy of older filtration technology. Three residential blocks on Level 6: masonry cores, same construction era as her building.

Seven structures for twelve thousand people.

She wrote the number. She did not soften it. The number was what it was — seven path-poor structures in a sector with eighty-four residential buildings and a combined population she estimated at twelve thousand based on the transit density patterns. Fourteen hundred and forty at twelve percent. The arithmetic that ran without her deciding to run it.

She included Fuxi's junction coordinates. Not the full thirteen years — the Lagos operator had asked for the complete dataset and she would send it, but not yet. The coordinates for Sector 7M. The three trunk-lines converging at the distribution node beneath Level 5. The conduit density readings that mapped onto the sacrifice district designation the way the Sump's topology had mapped onto BLACKWEIR.

She included the names.

The forty-seven. She did not have institutional permission to share names, did not have credentials, did not have the authority the network's other contributors carried. But she had the names. She had heard them through the walls during the Broadcast. She had counted them. And the forty-seven names cross-referenced with Fuxi's junction data produced something the institutional models did not have: specific people absorbed at specific infrastructure coordinates.

Mrs. Huang. Block 14. Unit 7-C. Junction 4E-17.

Yuto. Block 11. Shimmer node near junction 4E-22.

Lin Wei. Converter Station B.

She typed them the way she had counted them — without deciding to, the first language running before the mind could intervene. Each name with its junction. Each junction with its conduit density reading from Fuxi's logs. Each reading a number that described the infrastructure that had absorbed the person the name belonged to.

She read the message. Once for inventory. Once for structure.

The words were hers and Fuxi's and the document's, braided together the way they had been braided since she opened the chip on the mattress in her four-by-three room and saw his handwriting in the metadata field. Clinical vocabulary. Body-knowledge. The arithmetic of who lived and who didn't based on the composition of the walls they slept beside.

She sent it.

The overlay confirmed. Green notification beside the grey.

*Message delivered. Recipient: Mage Testimony Network (public channel).*

---

The couple from Level 6 came at mid-morning. The woman sat at the table. The man stood behind her. The same positions as last week. The same grip on the table's edge. The same posture of a person who did not want to be here and could not afford not to be.

Nuwa showed them the map. Blue and red. Path-poor and path-rich. The stairwell — masonry, poured concrete, she pointed to the column on the schematic and traced the line from their floor to the ground level. The woman watched her hand move across the projected topology. The man watched the woman.

"The stairwell is safe," Nuwa said.

"How do you know?"

The question she was asked in every room. The question that meant: *why should I believe you?* And beneath it: *who are you to tell me where to stand?*

"I stood in a building like it," she said. "During BLACKWEIR. The walls were masonry. The signal did not pass through. I survived."

The woman looked at her. The assessment — the longer one, the one that recalculated weight.

"Were you alone?"

"Yes."

The woman's hands loosened on the table's edge. The man shifted his weight. The room was quiet. The projector hummed — a small mechanical sound, the motor that turned the salvaged display, unrelated to the infrastructure hum that lived in the walls and floors and carried the frequency that meant the system continued.

"Show me again," the woman said. "The route from our floor."

Nuwa showed her. Third time. The same route. The same masonry walls. The same poured-concrete stairwell. The same seven structures for twelve thousand people.

The woman asked if she could bring her sister. Her sister lived in the building east — the metal-framed polymer construction, the path-rich structure Nuwa had marked red on every map she projected. The sister needed to move. The sister did not know why.

"Bring her," Nuwa said.

---

By afternoon, the network had four more responses.

She read them between the families. Between the maps and the pointing and the questions that were always the same question — *is it going to happen here, and if it does, where do I stand* — she opened the overlay and tilted her head and read.

The Lagos coordinator had forwarded her data to a group she had not heard of — an informal network of infrastructure analysts, former conduit workers, municipal engineers who had left their positions after the document was published. The coordinator's message was brief. *Your topology data is being integrated. More sites are reporting similar patterns. Neo-Shanghai Sump is the most complete dataset we have. Keep sending.*

A mage in Mumbai — she recognized the handle from months of reading the testimony chain — had responded to her forty-seven names. The message was precise, clinical, the vocabulary of someone trained to measure what the implants received. *The names you documented correspond to discrete coherence signatures within the aggregate field. Each name is a resolvable pattern. Your count of 47 from personal recognition suggests the total resolvable count for the Neo-Shanghai zone is substantially larger. The Broadcast event was not random. It was a census.*

A census. She held the word the way she held cred calculations. A census: an official count of a population. The system counted populations. The system had counted her — *Lin, Nuwa. Credential tier: Basic.* The system had counted the Sump — 8.2 million, reclassified, archived, *formerly.* And now the absorbed were counting themselves. Through the infrastructure. Through the walls. Through the conduit network that had carried the suffering that created them.

She had counted forty-seven because forty-seven was the size of her inventory. The Broadcast had counted 8.2 million because 8.2 million was the size of the field.

Two more responses arrived while she was showing the map to a woman from Level 3 who had walked forty minutes to reach the organizing building because someone at the market had told her that a woman in this building knew which walls were safe. The woman from Level 3 had a child on her hip and a bag of rice in her hand and the expression of a person who had been carrying both for longer than either should require.

Nuwa showed her the map. Blue and red. She pointed to the masonry structures. She described the routes. She answered the question.

After the woman left, she sat at the table. The projector was still running. The topology map glowed on the wall. The overlay flickered — the cracked housing warm behind her ear, the chip data dormant, the frozen notification in its permanent position.

She opened the message field.

She did not type a report. She did not type topology data or junction coordinates or the forty-seven names. She typed what the woman from Level 3 had said when Nuwa showed her the blue zone closest to her apartment — the ceramic-lined water treatment facility on Level 5, the path-poor structure with public access.

*She asked me how far. I told her six corridors and one stairwell. She asked me if six corridors was enough time. I told her I didn't know. I told her in the Sump I had four meters. I told her the cloth. I told her the masonry. She asked me how I knew. I told her I was there.*

She sent it.

The green notification. The grey notification. Side by side in the damaged display.

*Lin, Nuwa. Credential tier: Basic. Evacuation priority: None assigned.*
*Message delivered. Recipient: Mage Testimony Network (public channel).*

The credential notice had been there for over a year. The system's last word on her institutional existence. Dead server, dead district, dead classification. It did not update. It did not expire. It occupied the edge of her vision the way the chemical staining occupied her fingertips — permanent, unremarked, the residue of a system that had processed her and moved on.

The network notifications were accumulating beside it. Green beside grey. The live system and the dead one, sharing the same cracked display, rendered through the same damaged housing, read through the same compensating tilt of her head.

She was in both systems now. The one that had archived her and the one that was asking for what she knew.

---

She cooked in the evening. Rice. The water spinach — half the bundle, sliced thin, fried in the last of someone's oil that Fen had left on the shared shelf in the corridor. The smell filled the room. Green. Sharp. The smell of a choice made in a kitchen that was not the annex and not the Sump and not the four-by-three room where she had first eaten spring onions at a folding table with a cardboard shim.

She ate at the table. The projector was off. The map was not on the wall. The room was a room — four meters by three, sink, cooking surface, window onto the ventilation shaft, the hum through the masonry walls faint and steady and carrying nothing she needed to count.

The overlay pulsed. Another response. She did not open it yet.

She ate. The rice and the greens and the quiet. The inventory ran — twenty-three creds, the adhesive on day ten, the projector's shelf bracket loosening, the families scheduled for tomorrow, the sister from the path-rich building east, the woman from Level 3 who would come back with the child. The inventory was larger now. It had always been creds and food and days until rent. Now it included corridors and junction nodes and the names of people who walked forty minutes to see a map and the messages from strangers in Lagos and Mumbai and Dharavi who recognized the shape of what she carried because they carried it too.

She washed the bowl. One bowl. One pair of chopsticks. One mug. The same count as the first morning in this room. The inventory of objects had not changed. Everything she owned could still be packed in four minutes.

What she carried could not be packed at all.

She sat on the mattress. Opened the overlay. The new response was from the single-line sender — the one from Dharavi who had written *I counted too.*

The message was longer this time. Still no name. Still no handle. But words — careful, precise, the vocabulary of someone who had learned to describe the indescribable by starting with what could be measured.

*In Dharavi the masonry was older. Pre-colonial foundation walls. The absorption front stopped at the brick-lime boundary the way yours stopped at the poured concrete. I survived in a textile workshop. The loom frames were wood and iron. The walls were brick. I counted nineteen. I have been sending the names to this network for three months. Nobody responded until you. I think it is because you used the word "counted." The others use "documented" or "recorded" or "catalogued." You counted. So did I. It is not the same verb.*

She read it. Once for inventory. Once for structure. Once more.

*It is not the same verb.*

No. It was not. Documented was the system's verb. Recorded was the institution's. Catalogued was the archive's. Counted was the body's — the first language, the one that ran before the mind could intervene, the one that held each name the way a hand held beads, not for the total but for the texture.

She typed.

*Nineteen. I had forty-seven. The loom frames — did the iron conduct? In the Sump the metal-framed structures were path-rich. If the iron was structural but not connected to the conduit network, it might have been inert. Can you describe the workshop layout?*

She sent it. The green notification appeared. She looked at it. She looked at the grey one beside it.

The grey notification was the system's inventory of her. The green notifications were her inventory of the system.

She closed the overlay. The room was dark. The hum through the walls was the note that meant the system continued, and she was inside it, and the system did not know what she was doing inside it because the system had never been designed to track what the expendable did after they survived.

Forty-seven names. Nineteen more from Dharavi. The topology data forwarded to Lagos. The junction coordinates being integrated into a database she had not built and did not control and could not have imagined when she sat on the floor of the masonry annex with a wet cloth on her face and counted the seconds until the sound stopped.

The inventory was open. She was not done.
