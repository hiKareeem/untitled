The memory inventory ran on its own schedule. She didn't initiate it. It just — surfaced, the way the filing system surfaced entries she hadn't requested. Twice a day now. Usually morning and mid-afternoon. The neurologist at UCSF had called it "consolidation behavior" and said it might continue indefinitely or it might stop and neither outcome would tell her anything about the underlying architecture.

Tuesday was gone. The neurologist had been direct about it: the consolidation window for Tuesday's memories had closed. Whatever she'd done, seen, said, streamed on Tuesday of that week — the week of the blackout stream, the week everything after 90mg became noise — was not stored in long-term memory and would not be recovered. The gap was permanent. Not damage. Absence. The filing system had a notation for it.

*Tuesday: —. Status: permanent gap. Content: unrecoverable. Assessment: accepted.*

Thursday had come back. Not all at once — in pieces, over weeks. The cafeteria at Chén. Dr. Osei's hands. The taste of the hospital's protein drink, chalky, wrong temperature. A conversation with Paz she couldn't place in sequence but could hear clearly — Paz saying "you scared me" and meaning it in the way Paz meant things, which was completely. Thursday had reassembled itself from fragments into something that felt like a day. Not sharp. Not the way pre-Lumina memories were sharp. But present.

Friday was fuzzy. The neurologist said fuzzy was a category — neither gone nor consolidated, existing in a middle state that could resolve in either direction. Friday might come back. Friday might thin further and vanish. She would not know which until it happened or didn't.
