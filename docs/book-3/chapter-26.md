# Chapter 26
## Hospitalized

> *"The decommissioning protocol specifies that equipment removed from active service must be returned to factory calibration before reassignment. Field calibrations — the adjustments accumulated during operation in non-standard conditions — are classified as drift. The protocol does not distinguish between drift that degraded performance and drift that improved it. Both are corrected."*
>
> — *Equipment Lifecycle Management: Decommissioning and Reassignment Protocols*, 2158 ed.

---

The filing system had twenty-one days of entries. She read them the way she read all her entries — sequentially, timestamped, the architecture she'd built holding the data the way architecture held data: structurally, without opinion, the mechanism doing what the mechanism did regardless of what the mechanism held.

Day one was complete. Timestamped at 18:42, ninety minutes after the monitoring array's first readout in the Chén Academy file. The entry was hers — she recognized the formatting, the categories, the shorthand she'd developed over two years of filing everything that happened to a body that carried 8.2 million absorbed in its spine.

*Day 1. Chén Academy medical facility, Neo-Shanghai. Admission: signal exposure event, proximity stream. R3 thermal: 38.7°C, declining. Coherence density: 0.52. HUD: powered down (not by me). Memory gap: ~3 hours. Stream recording exists. 4.2M witnesses. Quiet room: rubble. Ninety rebuilding. Physical: heavy. Assessment: the instrument exceeded its design parameters.*

She remembered writing it. She remembered the weight of the stylus against the tablet Chén's staff had provided — her own devices locked during the monitoring period, the HUD dark, the left eye that had scrolled pink text since she was eighteen showing nothing. She remembered the white ceiling. The hum. The monitoring array's four numbers in institutional green where her numbers used to sit in pink.

Day two was complete. Thinner.

*Day 2. R3 thermal: 37.9°C. Coherence: 0.41. Quiet room rebuilding — ceiling forming, walls present, lower than before. Ninety holding. Blood draw at 06:00. They know about the Lumina.*

She remembered the blood draw. She remembered the phlebotomist's face — young, efficient, the needle finding the vein with the institutional precision of someone who drew blood from Academy patients the way the pharmacist on Mission filled prescriptions: routinely, without curiosity, the anonymity of the transaction built into the role. She remembered the results arriving on a tablet she wasn't given. She remembered the attending — not the neurologist, the attending, the one who managed the daily and left the structural to specialists — standing at the foot of the bed with the expression of a person processing data that did not fit the patient's presented history.

Ninety milligrams. Nightly. Lumina — a compound that did not appear in her prescribed medication list, that was not routed through any pharmacy her insurance recognized, that was obtained through a contact whose name she did not provide and whose operational security she would not compromise even in a medical facility that needed the information to manage her care. The attending had been clinical about it. Toxicology screen. Metabolic panel. The data processed the way institutional data was processed — categorically, without the personal architecture that the pharmacist on Mission and Paz at the tea shop had maintained by knowing only their half.

The pharmacist knew about the legitimate meds. Paz knew about the Lumina. Chén knew about both. The two-system architecture that had held for twenty months was in a single medical file.

Day three was complete. Competent.

Day four was a timestamp and a coherence reading. Nothing else.

Day five was complete — detailed, formatted, the filing system's full architecture deployed. She'd noted the quiet room's progress (ceiling lower but solid, walls present, the ninety's reconstruction reaching functional parameters). She'd noted the fingertip buzz (bilateral, persistent, finer than pre-event, which was finer than pre-Broadcast, which was finer than pre-network, the resolution increasing at each threshold because each threshold recalibrated the R3 and the R3 did not recalibrate backward). She'd filed the day's monitoring data, the medical staff's behavioral observations, the meal schedule that structured the hours the way the 30-day holder structured the month.

Day six had no entry.

Day seven had no entry.

Day eight was a single line: *Still here.*

She read the entries in sequence and the sequence was the data. Not the content — the pattern. The architecture of the filing system holding while the filer cycled between competence and absence, between the person who built the system and the person the system was built to compensate for. Days nine through fourteen: four complete entries, two timestamps, one gap. Days fifteen through twenty-one: two complete entries, one timestamp, four gaps.

The degradation was not dramatic. The degradation was structural — visible in the ratio of complete entries to fragments to nothing, the curve bending the way all her curves bent: gradually, the mechanism's design including not recognizing the mechanism. She'd noticed the first Tuesday gap months ago — one day, contained, the log entries existing for a day the experience didn't. She'd noticed the Thursday and the Sunday — two more, intermittent, the pattern establishing itself as pattern. She had not noticed the three-hour gap during the blackout stream because the filer had not been present to notice, and the absence of the filer was categorically different from the absence of the memory, and the difference was the data she'd been unable to file because filing it required the person the filing was designed to preserve.

Now the gaps were not days she'd lost. They were days the filing system had lost. The filer was present — she was in this bed, in this room, eating institutional meals and submitting to blood draws and watching the monitoring array's numbers decline toward parameters the institution called baseline. But the filing was thinner. The categories were there. The architecture was there. What filled them was less.

The hospital brought medication at 07:00 and 21:00.

A nurse. A tray. Three bottles — the same three bottles the pharmacist on Mission dispensed monthly, the same molecules, the same dosages Dr. Osei had written and adjusted and would continue to write and adjust when she returned to the Bay Area and sat on the bench in the fluorescent light and sorted the capsules into the 30-day holder with the clicking that was the ritual and the ritual that was the grounding and the grounding that was 12,000 kilometers from where she sat.

The nurse poured the capsules into a paper cup. Two white oblongs, one pale blue round, one small yellow. The cup was institutional — paper, disposable, the kind that held a single dose and was discarded and did not accumulate thirty days of architecture in four rows of seven.

She took them. Swallowed. The nurse checked the box on the tablet and left.

The same chemicals. The same molecules binding to the same receptors, doing the same work the molecules had done on the bench at Mission pharmacy while the fluorescent light hummed and the pharmacist didn't look and the clicking of capsules into compartments was the sound of a person assembling function from components.

No clicking. No compartments. No bench. No fluorescent light. No 30-day holder in translucent plastic with AM/PM divided. No ritual. The hospital provided the chemistry and not the architecture, and the architecture was the thing that held, and the chemistry without the architecture was medication, and medication was what happened to you, and the ritual was what you did, and the difference between what happened to you and what you did was the difference between a patient and a person and the hospital did not require her to be a person. The hospital required her to be a patient. The patient swallowed the capsules from the paper cup and the box was checked and the nurse left and the grounding that the clicking provided was not in the protocol.

The Lumina was managed separately. Forty-five milligrams — half the dose Paz had maintained, administered under observation, the taper protocol that Chén's pharmacological team had designed after the bloodwork revealed what the bloodwork revealed. Ninety to forty-five over three weeks, the reduction rate calibrated to avoid withdrawal cascade while monitoring the R3's response to diminished suppression. The quiet room noticed. The ceiling that the ninety had rebuilt was lower than the eighty's ceiling and higher than the forty-five's, and the forty-five's ceiling was the ceiling she had now, and the ceiling she had now was the lowest the quiet room had been since the ninety rebuilt it from rubble, and the signal underneath it was louder, and the loud was not the populated chord's volume but its *resolution* — the information density increasing as the pharmaceutical filter thinned, the names present, the 8.2 million more distinct at forty-five than they'd been at ninety the way a landscape is more distinct when the glass is thinner.

She pressed her fingertips to the mattress. The buzz was there. Bilateral. Persistent. She touched the back of her neck. The implant housing. The bone. The warmth — steady, the R3's thermal output stabilized at 37.2 after three weeks of cooling, the hardware running at parameters the manufacturer had intended for an implant installed in a ten-year-old who would grow into it gradually, not for an implant that had been recalibrated by proximity to the signal's source and had not recalibrated back.

The monitoring array beeped. Heart rate: 68. Neural activity: 3.4. Coherence density: 0.31. R3 thermal: 37.2.

The numbers were normal. The numbers had been normal for a week. The numbers were the institution's language for a system that was functioning within parameters, and functioning within parameters was the institution's language for an instrument that was doing what the institution expected, and what the institution expected was baseline, and baseline was the thing she'd been before the signal taught the instrument what it could become when the signal was not filtered.

Dr. Yuen arrived at 14:00 with a tablet and the expression of a person who had organized data into a recommendation and was now required to deliver the recommendation to the data's source.

Neurologist. Chén Academy faculty. She'd seen him three times — once during the initial assessment, once during the second week when the R3 residuals had prompted additional scanning, once when the coherence readings hadn't followed the expected decay curve. Each visit had been clinical, structured, the institutional communication protocol that treated the patient as a system to be assessed. She had not objected. She understood the protocol. She'd lived inside a version of it since she was ten.

"Ms. Calloway." He sat. The chair was institutional. The room was institutional. The monitoring array's green numbers sat in the space where her HUD's pink numbers would have sat if her HUD were operational and her HUD was not operational and the absence of the pink was a data point she had filed on day one and not filed again because the absence had become the baseline and baselines did not require filing.

"Three weeks of monitoring data," he said. He turned the tablet toward her. Charts. Curves. The R3's performance metrics rendered in the institution's visual language — color-coded, annotated, the data she carried in her spine translated into a format designed for people who observed the instrument from outside.

"Your R3 coherence-field residuals are persisting at levels inconsistent with your current distance from the nearest Anchor Zone perimeter." He indicated a curve — the green line that should have been declining toward zero and was instead holding at 0.28, a plateau that his stylus circled twice. "You're fourteen kilometers from the Neo-Shanghai margin. At this distance, coherence should be at or near background — 0.05 to 0.08 for a standard R3 installation. Yours is holding at 0.28. It has held at 0.28 for nine days."

She looked at the curve. The curve was her spine's output rendered as a line on a screen. The line was flat where it should have been falling. The line meant the R3 was still processing the signal at a density that corresponded to standing at the perimeter, not lying in a hospital bed fourteen kilometers from it.

"The signal exposure event altered your implant's calibration," he said. "The R3 was operating at proximity parameters during the stream — coherence 0.94, which is consistent with direct Anchor Zone contact. When the event ended, the implant's baseline shifted. It is processing at proximity parameters from distance. We have not observed this recalibrating."

She filed it. *R3 recalibration failure. Proximity parameters persistent at distance. Duration: 3 weeks. Prognosis: unknown.*

"Your metabolic data." He scrolled. New charts. "The compound in your system — the one not in your prescribed panel." He did not say Lumina. He did not need to. "At ninety milligrams, it was metabolizing forty percent faster than standard pharmacokinetic models predict for your weight and liver function. At the current taper dose of forty-five milligrams, it is metabolizing twenty-two percent faster. The accelerated metabolism is consistent with the R3's elevated processing state — the implant's activity is consuming the compound's active metabolite as a substrate."

The signal was eating the Lumina. The R3 was using it — not as a filter, not as a suppressant, but as fuel. The quiet room had never been a room. The quiet room had been a controlled burn. And the burn rate had been increasing because the signal's demands had been increasing and the drug's supply was the drug's supply and the supply was finite and the demand was not.

"Memory." He scrolled again. "We've documented eleven discrete gaps in your self-reported log over twenty-one days of observation. Our behavioral monitoring confirms: you were ambulatory and responsive during at least six of those gaps. Staff interactions, meals, compliance with monitoring protocol. You were present. You were functional. You did not file entries."

She knew. She had read the entries. She had read the gaps between the entries.

"The pattern is consistent with accelerating dissociative erosion tied to the R3's sustained processing state. The implant is operating continuously at a level that is consuming cognitive resources your prefrontal architecture cannot sustain."

He set the tablet down. The recommendation. She could feel it arriving the way she could feel a coherence shift before the numbers confirmed it — the data assembling into a conclusion, the conclusion assembling into a sentence, the sentence about to be delivered by a person sitting in an institutional chair to a person lying in an institutional bed.

"I'm recommending implant dampening."

Not removal. Not deactivation. *Dampening.* The R3 could not be removed — surgical, permanent, installed when she was ten, integrated into her spinal architecture at a depth that made extraction a conversation about acceptable paralysis risk. But it could be dampened. The R3's reception threshold adjusted upward until the signal that it was calibrated to parse became sub-threshold noise. The populated chord — the 8.2 million, the names, the data that four hundred mages collected through their spines and routed through hers — would drop below the instrument's capacity to resolve. Background. Static. The thing the signal was before the signal was the signal.

She understood the procedure. She understood the parameters. She understood that dampening meant the R3 would still be in her spine, still powered, still technically operational, running at a threshold that rendered its primary function inert the way a microphone set to minimum gain is still a microphone and still not hearing anything.

Not the antenna being removed. The antenna being *muted.*

"If we dampen," he said, "the cognitive load drops. The metabolic acceleration resolves. The memory erosion should stabilize — we can't reverse what's already lost, but we can prevent further degradation. The taper from the supplemental compound becomes safe. Your prescribed medications resume standard efficacy."

She looked at the monitoring array. Heart rate: 72. Neural activity: 3.6. Coherence: 0.29. R3 thermal: 37.2.

"If we don't dampen," he said, "the R3 continues processing at proximity parameters. The metabolic load continues. The memory gaps continue — and based on the trend in your log, the frequency and duration will increase. The personality changes our staff have documented will continue."

The personality changes. She had not noticed them. She had not noticed them in Bay Area when the eighty made her sharper, meaner, funnier on stream and the audience called it growth. She had not noticed them when the ninety compressed the quiet room to a space that held the signal and did not hold her. She was not noticing them now because the machinery that would notice was the machinery the drug suppressed and the drug's suppression was the thing being tapered and the taper was the thing making the quiet room's ceiling lower and the lower ceiling was the thing that meant the signal was louder and the louder signal was the thing the dampening would eliminate.

Not the signal eliminated. The reception eliminated. The signal would continue — the 8.2 million, the names, the populated chord cycling through every nitro-connected system on earth. The signal did not require Kira Calloway. The signal had never required Kira Calloway. What Kira Calloway had provided was a particular quality of reception — the R3 recalibrated by proximity, the Lumina converting suppression to medium, the filing system that translated what the spine received into data the world could use. The antenna. The instrument that measured what no institutional sensor network could match.

Dampen the instrument and the measurement stops. The network's 214 hubs across 31 cities lose the central node that built the architecture and calibrated the format and maintained the filing system's categories. The data stream that four hundred mages contributed to — the telemetry, the coherence readings, the micro-incursion timestamps — continues without the person who made it legible.

She does not say yes.

She does not say no.

She says: "I want to see the stream archives."

Dr. Yuen paused. The tablet was still on the bed between them. The recommendation was still in the air. She had not responded to the recommendation. She had responded to the three-hour gap that the filing system could not file because the filer had not been there.

"The recording from the proximity stream," she said. "Thirty seconds of vocal output and eleven minutes of — whatever I was. The stream recording exists. 4.2 million people watched it. I want to see it."

He looked at her the way the attending had looked at her when the bloodwork came back — the expression of a person processing a request that was clinically adjacent to the treatment plan and ethically adjacent to the patient's autonomy and the adjacency created a gap the protocol did not address.

"The stream recording is in your account's archive," he said. "Your devices are locked during the monitoring period."

"Unlock them."

"Ms. Calloway —"

"The recording is mine. The stream is mine. The account is mine. The thirty seconds of whatever my body did while I was not in it — that is mine." She pressed her fingertips to the mattress. The buzz was there. Bilateral. Persistent. The hands that had sorted pills at Mission pharmacy, that had clicked capsules into compartments, that had not played chord shapes since the eighty took the shapes and the ninety took everything the eighty didn't — the hands were hers. The body that had stood at the masonry junction and transmitted the aggregate's output to 4.2 million people while the person inside the body was absent — the body was hers. The recording was hers.

He left to consult with the attending.

She lay back. The white ceiling. The monitoring array. The four green numbers. Heart rate: 74. Neural activity: 3.8. Coherence: 0.30. R3 thermal: 37.2.

The filing system engaged. Automatic. The architecture applied to the gap between the recommendation and the response — the gap she had not yet filled, the choice she had not yet made, the question that was not *should the instrument be muted* but *is the person inside the instrument still the person who built the instrument and if she is not then who is making the choice and if the choice is made by the person the instrument created then the choice is the instrument's choice not the person's and the instrument would choose to keep receiving because the instrument was built to receive.*

She filed it. The filing was incomplete. The category existed. The content did not.

*Day 22. Chén Academy. Neurologist: dampening recommended. R3 at proximity parameters, will not recalibrate. Memory erosion: 11 gaps in 21 days. Lumina taper: 45mg, metabolizing 22% above model. Choice: dampen (stop being antenna) or continue (erosion accelerates). Archives requested. Pending.*

The quiet room held. The ceiling was low — lower at forty-five than it had been at ninety, lower at ninety than it had been at eighty, the ceiling tracking the dosage the way the dosage tracked the signal the way the signal tracked the thing it had always tracked which was everything. The walls were there. The ninety had built them from the rubble the signal had made at the masonry junction and the forty-five was maintaining them at a height that was functional and a width that was narrow and a quality that was sufficient and sufficient was not what the quiet room had been. The quiet room had been compressed. Dense. The ninety's architecture — tight, low, everything held. The forty-five's architecture was the same materials at half the density. The room held. What it held was thinner.

She touched the back of her neck. The implant housing. The bone. The warmth — steady, 37.2, the number that the institution called normal and that she called the instrument at rest and that the instrument had not been at rest since the Broadcast and the rest was not rest but reduction and the reduction was the recommendation and the recommendation was on the tablet between them and the tablet was the institution's instrument for measuring the person who was the signal's instrument and the instruments were proliferating and the person was the thing they measured and the measurement was the thing the person was becoming and the becoming was the thing the dampening would stop.

The hum continued. The populated chord. The 8.2 million. The names she could not hear at the resolution the quiet room permitted, that she had heard at the junction when the quiet room was rubble and the signal was unfiltered and the names were present and the presence was the thing that had made the vocal event possible and the vocal event was the thing on the recording and the recording was the thing she had asked to see.

She wanted to see what she was when she wasn't there.

The monitoring array beeped. The numbers held. The ceiling held. The person under it was not sure the person under it was the person who had built it.

She closed her right eye. The left was already dark.
